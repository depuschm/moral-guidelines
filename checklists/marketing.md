# Marketing and advertising checklist

Copy this list into a product or design review, a project kickoff, a team charter or your own reflection, and tick what applies.
Rules are grouped by level and sorted by score ([criteria](../RANKING.md)). Each links to the file that explains it and cites its sources.
*Supported by* names practices and tools that help you follow the rule (see the file's "How to apply" section).

Also apply the [general checklist](general.md).

## Recommended (6–8)

- [ ] `MKT-2` (8) Hold evidence for every claim that people are likely to take as objective before you publish it, at the level of proof the claim states or implies. ([specific/marketing](../specific/marketing/README.md))
- [ ] `MKT-6` (8) Use only genuine endorsements and reviews that reflect the honest opinion and real experience of the person giving them; never write, buy or fake reviews, and don't hide or suppress negative ones. ([specific/marketing](../specific/marketing/README.md))
- [ ] `MKT-11` (8) Don't target or exploit people made vulnerable by age, illness, disability, credulity or their circumstances, such as bereavement or losing a job, and judge your marketing by its likely effect on them, not only on the average consumer. ([specific/marketing](../specific/marketing/README.md))
- [ ] `MKT-3` (7) Make comparisons fair: compare products that meet the same need, on material, relevant, verifiable and representative features, and don't denigrate competitors or create confusion with their brands. ([specific/marketing](../specific/marketing/README.md))
- [ ] `MKT-4` (7) Make every ad recognisable as an ad: label advertorials, sponsored content and paid influencer posts clearly and prominently, where people will notice the label straight away. ([specific/marketing](../specific/marketing/README.md)) *Supported by: Influencers' guide to making clear that ads are ads.*
- [ ] `MKT-5` (7) Disclose any connection between an endorser and the brand that the audience wouldn't expect, such as payment, free or discounted products, or a family, employment or business relationship, clearly and close to the endorsement. ([specific/marketing](../specific/marketing/README.md)) *Supported by: Influencers' guide to making clear that ads are ads.*
- [ ] `MKT-7` (7) Make environmental claims specific, accurate and backed by evidence, base them on the product's full life cycle, and don't hide significant harms or present as a benefit what the law requires anyway. - [ ] `MKT-8` (7) Don't use vague green claims such as "eco-friendly", "green" or "sustainable" unless you can prove them without qualification, and don't use environmental labels or symbols that suggest a certification or official approval you don't have. ([specific/marketing](../specific/marketing/README.md)) *Supported by: ICC Framework for Responsible Environmental Marketing Communications.*
- [ ] `MKT-9` (7) Base climate claims on real reductions in emissions, and say clearly how much of a claim such as "carbon neutral" rests on offsetting. ([specific/marketing](../specific/marketing/README.md)) *Supported by: ICC Framework for Responsible Environmental Marketing Communications.*
- [ ] `MKT-12` (7) Don't play on fear or distress without a good reason, and don't use shocking claims or images just to get attention; where fear is justified, for example in a safety message, keep it in proportion. ([specific/marketing](../specific/marketing/README.md))
- [ ] `MKT-13` (7) Don't use high-pressure tactics, or techniques that take advantage of people's trust or limited understanding to push them into choices they wouldn't otherwise make. ([specific/marketing](../specific/marketing/README.md))
