# Product and UX design checklist

Copy this list into a product or design review, a project kickoff, a team charter or your own reflection, and tick what applies.
Rules are grouped by level and sorted by score ([criteria](../RANKING.md)). Each links to the file that explains it and cites its sources.
*Supported by* names practices and tools that help you follow the rule (see the file's "How to apply" section).

Also apply the [general checklist](general.md).

## Fundamental (9–10)

- [ ] `DESIGN-1` (9) Tell the truth about time, stock and popularity: no fake countdown timers, false "only 2 left" messages, made-up demand or fake reviews. ([specific/design](../specific/design/README.md)) *Supported by: Deceptive Patterns, Types of deceptive pattern.*
- [ ] `DESIGN-2` (9) Show the full price, including every mandatory fee, from the start, and get the user's express agreement to every extra: no drip pricing and no pre-ticked boxes for add-ons. ([specific/design](../specific/design/README.md)) *Supported by: Deceptive Patterns, Types of deceptive pattern.*
- [ ] `DESIGN-3` (9) Make cancelling a subscription as easy as signing up, through the same channel, without a maze of screens, retention offers you can't skip or a phone line that is hard to reach. ([specific/design](../specific/design/README.md)) *Supported by: Deceptive Patterns, Types of deceptive pattern.*

## Recommended (6–8)

- [ ] `DESIGN-4` (8) Present choices neutrally: give "no" the same prominence and ease as "yes", don't shame people for declining ("No thanks, I don't like saving money"), and don't ask again once they have answered. ([specific/design](../specific/design/README.md)) *Supported by: Deceptive Patterns, Types of deceptive pattern, EDPB Guidelines 03/2022, Annex I checklist and Annex II best practices.*
- [ ] `DESIGN-5` (8) Write copy that says plainly what will happen: no trick questions or double negatives, no buttons whose label hides what they do, and no ads made to look like reviews, news or neutral rankings. ([specific/design](../specific/design/README.md)) *Supported by: EDPB Guidelines 03/2022, Annex I checklist and Annex II best practices.*
- [ ] `DESIGN-6` (8) Make the defaults protect the user: ship with the most privacy-protective settings, and leave optional data sharing, marketing and public visibility off until the user turns them on. ([specific/design](../specific/design/README.md)) *Supported by: EDPB Guidelines 03/2022, Annex I checklist and Annex II best practices.*
- [ ] `DESIGN-8` (8) Meet WCAG 2.2 level AA at least, and build accessibility in from the first designs rather than fixing it before launch. ([specific/design](../specific/design/README.md)) *Supported by: W3C WAI, Easy Checks: A First Review of Web Accessibility.*
- [ ] `DESIGN-11` (8) Design for people's wellbeing, not for maximum time spent: don't use infinite scroll, autoplay, streaks or reward loops to make it hard to stop, especially in services children use. ([specific/design](../specific/design/README.md))
- [ ] `DESIGN-7` (7) Make leaving as easy as joining: let people find and complete account deletion without hunting through settings or contacting support. ([specific/design](../specific/design/README.md)) *Supported by: EDPB Guidelines 03/2022, Annex I checklist and Annex II best practices.*
- [ ] `DESIGN-9` (7) Design for people with cognitive and learning disabilities, and for anyone tired, stressed or short of time: use clear language, help people avoid and fix mistakes, and don't make a task depend on remembering or transcribing information. ([specific/design](../specific/design/README.md))
- [ ] `DESIGN-10` (7) Test designs with disabled and older people during development, not only with automated checkers at the end. ([specific/design](../specific/design/README.md))
- [ ] `DESIGN-12` (7) Make it easy to stop and to control interruptions: offer a neutral choice to continue rather than continuing automatically, let people pause without losing progress, and give them control over notifications. ([specific/design](../specific/design/README.md))
