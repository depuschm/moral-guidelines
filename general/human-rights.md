# Human rights

The rights every person has, set out in the International Bill of Human Rights (the Universal Declaration and the two International Covenants) and the core UN treaties, and what they ask of individuals and organisations. The treaties bind the states that ratify them; this file is about what respecting those rights means in everyday life. Bodily harm is in [Harm and care](harm-and-care.md), discrimination in [Fairness and justice](fairness.md), and privacy and consent in [Respect, autonomy and consent](respect-and-consent.md). Companies' responsibility to respect human rights is covered in depth in [Business and human rights](../specific/business/README.md), and raising concerns about wrongdoing in [Responsibility and accountability](responsibility.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `10` [Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR)](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights) (United Nations, 1948): the 30 articles that set out the rights and freedoms of every person, adopted by the UN General Assembly "as a common standard of achievement for all peoples and all nations". **Follow it as the baseline.**
- `9` [International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR)](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Treaties/1976/03/19760323%2006-17%20AM/Ch_IV_04.pdf) (United Nations, 1966; certified text in the UN Treaty Collection): the binding treaty on life, freedom from torture and slavery, liberty, fair trial, privacy, thought, conscience, religion, expression, assembly and political participation. Article 4 lists the rights that no state of emergency can suspend.
- `9` [International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR)](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Treaties/1976/01/19760103%2009-57%20PM/Ch_IV_03.pdf) (United Nations, 1966; certified text in the UN Treaty Collection): the binding treaty on work and fair wages, social security, an adequate standard of living, health, education and cultural life.
- `9` [Convention against Torture and Other Cruel, Inhuman or Degrading Treatment or Punishment (CAT)](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Treaties/1987/06/19870626%2002-38%20AM/Ch_IV_9p.pdf) (United Nations, 1984; certified text in the UN Treaty Collection): defines torture and makes its prohibition absolute: no war, emergency or order from a superior can justify it.
- `9` [Convention on the Prevention and Punishment of the Crime of Genocide](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Treaties/1951/01/19510112%2008-12%20PM/Ch_IV_1p.pdf) (United Nations, 1948; certified text in the UN Treaty Collection): defines genocide, and makes it, incitement to it and complicity in it crimes, whether committed by rulers, officials or private individuals.
- `9` [Convention on the Rights of the Child (CRC)](https://www.unicef.org/child-rights-convention/convention-text) (United Nations, 1989; full text on UNICEF's site): the rights of every person under 18, including that the child's best interests come first in decisions about them, and their right to be heard and protected from violence.
- `8` [Declaration on Human Rights Defenders](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/n99/770/89/pdf/n9977089.pdf) (UN General Assembly resolution 53/144, 1998): everyone's right, alone or with others, to promote and protect human rights by peaceful means, and the responsibility of individuals and groups to do so.
- `8` [Human Rights](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rights-human/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): what human rights are, how they can be justified, which rights belong on the list, and whether they are truly universal.

## Key rules

### Absolute prohibitions

- `10` `RIGHTS-1` **Never torture anyone or treat them in a cruel, inhuman or degrading way, and never take part in it, whatever the order, threat or emergency.** The prohibition is absolute: no state of war, public emergency or order from a superior can justify torture, and the ICCPR allows no suspension of it even in an emergency. ([UN, UDHR Article 5](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights), [UN Treaty Collection, CAT Articles 1 and 2](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Treaties/1987/06/19870626%2002-38%20AM/Ch_IV_9p.pdf), [UN Treaty Collection, ICCPR Articles 4 and 7](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Treaties/1976/03/19760323%2006-17%20AM/Ch_IV_04.pdf))
- `10` `RIGHTS-2` **Never hold anyone in slavery or servitude, or make them do forced labour, and never knowingly profit from it.** Slavery and the slave trade are prohibited in all their forms, and forced labour, which the ILO estimates affects 27.6 million people, is a severe violation of human rights. ([UN, UDHR Article 4](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights), [UN Treaty Collection, ICCPR Article 8](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Treaties/1976/03/19760323%2006-17%20AM/Ch_IV_04.pdf), [ILO, Forced labour, modern slavery and trafficking in persons](https://www.ilo.org/topics-and-sectors/forced-labour-modern-slavery-and-trafficking-persons))
- `10` `RIGHTS-3` **Never take part in, incite or help genocide: acts intended to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnic, racial or religious group.** Direct and public incitement and complicity are crimes under the Genocide Convention as much as the killing itself, and private individuals are as liable as rulers and officials. ([UN Treaty Collection, Genocide Convention Articles II to IV](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Treaties/1951/01/19510112%2008-12%20PM/Ch_IV_1p.pdf))

### Respecting others' rights and freedoms

- `9` `RIGHTS-4` **Respect everyone's freedom of thought, conscience and religion, including their freedom to change their religion or belief, and never coerce anyone into or out of a belief.** The UDHR and the ICCPR protect the freedom to have, adopt, change and practise a religion or belief, and the Qur'an says "Let there be no compulsion in religion". ([UN, UDHR Article 18](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights), [UN Treaty Collection, ICCPR Article 18](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Treaties/1976/03/19760323%2006-17%20AM/Ch_IV_04.pdf), [Quran.com, Al-Baqarah 2:256](https://quran.com/al-baqarah/256))
- `9` `RIGHTS-5` **Never stir up hatred against people because of their nationality, race or religion in a way that incites discrimination, hostility or violence.** The ICCPR requires such advocacy to be prohibited by law, and the UDHR protects everyone against incitement to discrimination; laws on hate speech differ between countries (see "Open questions"). ([UN Treaty Collection, ICCPR Article 20](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Treaties/1976/03/19760323%2006-17%20AM/Ch_IV_04.pdf), [UN, UDHR Article 7](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights), [Parliament of the World's Religions, Towards a Global Ethic](https://www.weltethos.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Global-Ethic-Declaration_EN.pdf))
- `8` `RIGHTS-6` **Respect other people's freedom to hold and express opinions, including ones you reject, and answer speech you disagree with by argument, not by threats, violence or intimidation.** Everyone has the right to hold opinions without interference and to seek, receive and impart information and ideas; the restrictions the ICCPR allows are limited ones set by law, such as protecting others' rights and reputations. ([UN, UDHR Article 19](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights), [UN Treaty Collection, ICCPR Article 19](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Treaties/1976/03/19760323%2006-17%20AM/Ch_IV_04.pdf), [SEP, Freedom of Speech](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/freedom-speech/))
- `8` `RIGHTS-7` **In every decision that affects a child, make the child's best interests a primary consideration.** The CRC applies this to all actions concerning children, whether by courts, authorities or welfare institutions; listening to the child is `RESP-4`, and parents' duties are `REL-1`. ([UNICEF, CRC Article 3](https://www.unicef.org/child-rights-convention/convention-text))
- `7` `RIGHTS-8` **Use your own rights and freedoms in ways that respect the rights of others.** The UDHR allows limits on rights only to secure respect for the rights and freedoms of others and the just requirements of morality, public order and the general welfare, and no right may be used to destroy the rights of others. ([UN, UDHR Articles 29 and 30](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights), [Parliament of the World's Religions, Towards a Global Ethic](https://www.weltethos.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Global-Ethic-Declaration_EN.pdf))

### Defending rights and refusing complicity

- `9` `RIGHTS-9` **Refuse to take part in violating anyone's human rights, by what you do or by failing to act where you must, even when you are ordered or pressured to.** The Declaration on Human Rights Defenders says that no one shall take part in such violations, and that no one shall be punished for refusing; an order is no justification for torture. The general rule on orders is `ACCT-6`. ([UN, Declaration on Human Rights Defenders, Article 10](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/n99/770/89/pdf/n9977089.pdf), [UN Treaty Collection, CAT Article 2](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Treaties/1987/06/19870626%2002-38%20AM/Ch_IV_9p.pdf))
- `8` `RIGHTS-10` **Don't knowingly help others abuse human rights, by supplying, financing, encouraging or covering up for them, and don't knowingly profit from their abuses.** Knowingly giving practical help or encouragement that has a substantial effect on a crime is aiding and abetting it, and being seen to benefit from an abuse is a form of complicity too. The general rule on shared responsibility is `ACCT-7`. ([UN, Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights, Principle 17 commentary](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/g11/121/90/pdf/g1112190.pdf), [UN Treaty Collection, Genocide Convention Article III](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Treaties/1951/01/19510112%2008-12%20PM/Ch_IV_1p.pdf))
- `8` `RIGHTS-12` **If you run or manage an organisation, make sure it respects human rights: commit to it in a policy, find out what harm its activities and business relationships cause or contribute to, prevent it, and put right what it causes.** This responsibility applies to every enterprise, whatever its size, and goes beyond complying with national law. ([UN, Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights, Principles 11 to 15](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/g11/121/90/pdf/g1112190.pdf))
- `7` `RIGHTS-11` **Stand up, by peaceful means, for the rights of others, especially people who can't easily defend themselves.** Everyone has the right, alone or with others, to promote and protect human rights and to take part in peaceful activities against violations of them, and the UDHR asks every individual and every organ of society to promote respect for these rights. ([UN, Declaration on Human Rights Defenders, Articles 1, 12 and 18](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/n99/770/89/pdf/n9977089.pdf), [UN, UDHR Preamble](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights))

## How to apply

- `6` [UN Guiding Principles Reporting Framework](https://www.ungpreporting.org/) (Shift and Forvis Mazars): the first comprehensive guidance for companies on reporting how they respect human rights, which shows where they need to focus and improve. Supports `RIGHTS-10` and `RIGHTS-12`.
- `6` [Convention on the Rights of the Child: children's version](https://www.unicef.org/child-rights-convention/convention-text-childrens-version) (UNICEF): the CRC in words children can understand, with an activity book, game, video and poster for teaching it. Supports `RIGHTS-7`.

## Open questions

- `8` [SEP, Hate Speech](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/hate-speech/): where should the limits of free expression lie? Most democracies have laws against hate speech, as the ICCPR requires for incitement, while the United States protects most such speech; philosophers disagree about what hate speech is, what harm it does and whether banning it or answering it is the better response.
- `7` [SEP, Human Rights: Universal Human Rights in a World of Diverse Beliefs and Practices](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rights-human/#UnivHumaRighWorlDiveBeliPrac): are human rights universal, or do they reflect the values of one culture? Relativists and critics of ethnocentrism argue that standards are relative to the culture they come from; defenders, and the states that adopted the [Vienna Declaration](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/g93/142/33/pdf/g9314233.pdf) in 1993, hold that all human rights are universal while national, cultural and religious particularities must be borne in mind.
- `7` [SEP, Human Rights: Economic and Social Rights](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rights-human/#EconSociRigh): are rights to work, food, health and education human rights in the same sense as freedom from torture? Critics say they are too burdensome or not feasible in poor countries, and are goals rather than rights; defenders point to the ICESCR's progressive realisation and to the UN's view that all rights are indivisible and interdependent. The [UN Audiovisual Library's note on the ICCPR](https://legal.un.org/avl/ha/iccpr/iccpr.html) explains why the rights were split into two covenants.

## Tutorials and further reading

- `8` [Torture](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/torture/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): what torture is and what makes it wrong, including the philosophical debate about extreme "ticking bomb" cases, in which international law allows no exception.
- `7` [Human Rights](https://www.un.org/en/global-issues/human-rights) (United Nations): an overview of what human rights are, the International Bill of Human Rights, the core treaties and the UN bodies that monitor them.
- `7` [Chapter IV: Human Rights](https://treaties.un.org/Pages/Treaties.aspx?id=4&subid=A&clang=_en) (UN Treaty Collection): every UN human rights treaty, with which states have signed and ratified it, so you can see which treaties bind your country.
- `6` [History of the Declaration](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/udhr/history-of-the-declaration) (United Nations): how the Commission on Human Rights, chaired by Eleanor Roosevelt and made up of members of different political, cultural and religious backgrounds, drafted the UDHR.
