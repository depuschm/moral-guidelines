# Ethical reasoning and decisions

Scope: how to work through a moral decision, from noticing that a choice has an ethical side to acting and learning from the result; the biases and rationalisations that lead well-meaning people astray, which the field of behavioural ethics studies; seeking counsel; and what to do when duties conflict or you're unsure what is right. The theories used to weigh the options are in [Moral theories](moral-theories.md), especially `THEORY-1`. Owning the result, speaking up and resisting orders to do wrong are in [Responsibility and accountability](responsibility.md), and conflicts of interest are in [Fairness and justice](fairness.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `8` [A Framework for Ethical Decision Making](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/a-framework-for-ethical-decision-making/) (Markkula Center for Applied Ethics, Santa Clara University, 2021): **the baseline process**: identify the ethical issues, get the facts, evaluate the options through six ethical lenses (rights, justice, utilitarian, common good, virtue and care ethics), choose an option and test it, then act and reflect on the outcome.
- `8` [Moral Reasoning](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/reasoning-moral/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): how people recognise moral considerations, sort out conflicts between them, learn and revise their views, and reason together, and what empirical research shows about how often moral reasoning goes wrong.
- `7` [Giving Voice to Values](https://givingvoicetovaluesthebook.com/) (Mary C. Gentile, Darden School of Business, University of Virginia): a curriculum on acting on your values once you know what is right, which asks "What if I were going to act on my values? What would I say and do?" rather than only analysing the dilemma. Used in business schools and companies on every continent.
- `6` [Concepts Unwrapped](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/series/concepts-unwrapped) (Ethics Unwrapped, University of Texas at Austin): short videos with teaching notes on behavioural ethics, the study of why people make the ethical and unethical choices they do, such as self-serving bias, conformity, obedience to authority, framing, incrementalism and rationalisations.

## Key rules

### Working through a decision

- `8` `REASON-1` **Notice when a decision has an ethical side, even when it is presented as a business, technical or personal matter: ask whether it could harm someone, treat someone unfairly, or benefit some people at others' expense.** When people focus on profit, winning or a deadline, the ethical aspects of a choice can fade from view without their noticing. ([Markkula Center, A Framework for Ethical Decision Making](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/a-framework-for-ethical-decision-making/), [Ethics Unwrapped, Ethical Fading](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/ethical-fading), [Ethics Unwrapped, Framing](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/framing))
- `8` `REASON-2` **Get the facts before you judge: what you know, what you don't know, and what you are only assuming.** Many moral controversies arise simply because no one checked the facts, so if you don't know enough to decide, find out more where you can. ([Markkula Center, A Framework for Ethical Decision Making](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/a-framework-for-ethical-decision-making/), [Markkula Center, Thinking Ethically](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/thinking-ethically/))
- `8` `REASON-3` **Identify everyone the decision affects, and hear from them where you can, especially people who are vulnerable or have no voice in the decision.** Ask whose concerns carry most weight and why, and take account of each person's relationships, concerns and feelings. ([Markkula Center, A Framework for Ethical Decision Making](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/a-framework-for-ethical-decision-making/))
- `7` `REASON-4` **Look for more options than the first two that come to mind, including ones that meet competing duties at once.** Moral imagination, picturing the full range of possibilities, often finds a way to be both ethical and successful. ([Markkula Center, A Framework for Ethical Decision Making](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/a-framework-for-ethical-decision-making/), [Ethics Unwrapped, Moral Imagination](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/moral-imagination))
- `7` `REASON-5` **Test your choice before you act: would you be comfortable explaining it to someone you respect, to the people it affects, or in public?** If you would rather nobody found out, look at the decision again (`THEORY-6`). ([Markkula Center, A Framework for Ethical Decision Making](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/a-framework-for-ethical-decision-making/))
- `6` `REASON-6` **Carry out your decision with care for everyone it affects, and afterwards reflect on how it turned out, what you learned and whether anything needs putting right (`ACCT-4`).** ([Markkula Center, A Framework for Ethical Decision Making](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/a-framework-for-ethical-decision-making/))

### Biases and rationalisations

- `8` `REASON-7` **Expect your judgement to lean in your own favour, and look hardest at conclusions that benefit you, your team or your organisation.** People readily see the self-serving bias in others but not in themselves, and most believe they are more ethical than their peers. ([Ethics Unwrapped, Self-Serving Bias](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/self-serving-bias), [Ethics Unwrapped, Overconfidence Bias](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/overconfidence-bias))
- `8` `REASON-8` **Treat your own excuses as warning signs: "it didn't really hurt anyone", "others have done worse", "my boss made me", "they deserved it", "if I hadn't, someone else would have".** Rationalisations work best when you don't recognise them as such, and they dull your sense of responsibility for what you do. ([Ethics Unwrapped, Rationalizations](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/rationalizations))
- `7` `REASON-9` **Notice when the situation is steering your choice: an authority you want to please, what the people around you are doing, pressure from your organisation, or a role in which you apply standards you wouldn't apply at home.** Ask what you would decide on your own if the choice were framed as an ethical one, because such pressures often lead well-intentioned people to act against their own values. ([Ethics Unwrapped, Intro to Behavioral Ethics](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/video/intro-to-behavioral-ethics), [Ethics Unwrapped, Conformity Bias](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/conformity-bias), [Ethics Unwrapped, Obedience to Authority](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/obedience-to-authority), [Ethics Unwrapped, Role Morality](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/role-morality))
- `7` `REASON-10` **Decide where your limits are before you reach them, because each small compromise makes the next one easier.** Much serious wrongdoing, such as most accounting fraud, starts with small shortcuts that grow without anyone sensing a line being crossed. ([Ethics Unwrapped, Incrementalism](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/incrementalism), [Ethics Unwrapped, Giving Voice to Values](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/series/giving-voice-to-values))
- `6` `REASON-11` **Don't let a record of good deeds, or confidence in your own character, excuse you from thinking a choice through.** Having done good can lead people to give themselves permission to lapse, and overconfidence in their own character leads them to act without proper reflection. ([Ethics Unwrapped, Moral Equilibrium](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/moral-equilibrium), [Ethics Unwrapped, Overconfidence Bias](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/overconfidence-bias))

### Counsel and hard cases

- `7` `REASON-12` **Talk a difficult decision through with people you trust, including people who don't share your interest in the outcome or your point of view, but keep the decision your own.** The more novel and difficult the choice, the more you need others' perspectives and experience, but no group's verdict takes over your responsibility for your own judgement. ([Markkula Center, A Framework for Ethical Decision Making](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/a-framework-for-ethical-decision-making/), [SEP, Moral Reasoning: How Can We Reason, Morally, With One Another?](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/reasoning-moral/#HowWeReasMoraOneAnot), [SEP, Virtue Ethics: Practical Wisdom](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-virtue/#PracWisd))
- `7` `REASON-13` **When duties conflict and you can't meet them all, act on the weightier one, then explain yourself to the people you let down and make it up to them where you can.** Even when one obligation clearly overrides another, an apology, an explanation or compensation is often owed to those it disadvantaged. ([SEP, Moral Dilemmas](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-dilemmas/), [SEP, William David Ross](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/william-david-ross/))

## How to apply

- `6` [GVV Pillar 7: Reasons & Rationalizations](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/video/pillar-7-reasons-and-rationalizations) (Ethics Unwrapped): list the reasons and rationalisations you are likely to hear for questionable conduct, such as "everybody's doing it", and prepare a well-reasoned reply to each before you need it. Supports `REASON-8`.
- `5` [The Four-Way Test](https://rotary.fi/en/join-in/the-four-way-test/) (Rotary): four questions to ask of what you think, say and do: Is it the truth? Is it fair to all concerned? Will it build goodwill and better friendships? Will it be beneficial to all concerned? A quick check before acting. Supports `REASON-3` and `REASON-5`.

## Open questions

Questions on which serious traditions and thinkers disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `8` [Moral Dilemmas](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-dilemmas/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): whether there are genuine moral dilemmas, in which every option is wrong, or whether every apparent dilemma has a right answer that careful thought would find. Supporters point to the remorse people rightly feel whatever they choose; opponents appeal to principles such as "ought implies can", which a genuine dilemma would break.
- `7` [SEP, Moral Reasoning: Empirical Challenges to Moral Reasoning](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/reasoning-moral/#EmpiChalMoraReas): how far to trust moral intuitions. Research shows that people often can't give reasons for their moral intuitions and are swayed by irrelevant details, such as the order in which cases are presented; some explain this by a clash between fast, emotional thinking and slow, reflective thinking, while others see intuitions as a flexible learning system that is well suited to moral life.
- `7` [Moral Uncertainty](https://academic.oup.com/book/31934) (William MacAskill, Krister Bykvist and Toby Ord, Oxford University Press, 2020, open access): how to act when you're unsure which moral view is right. The authors argue that such uncertainty should itself be taken into account and, where views can be compared, that you should choose the option with the highest expected "choiceworthiness"; comparing very different theories is one of the hardest problems they address, and their approach is not universally accepted.
