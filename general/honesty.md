# Honesty and trust

Telling the truth, not deceiving, keeping your word and giving others credit: the conduct that lets people trust each other. Owning up to your own mistakes is in [Responsibility and accountability](responsibility.md), consent and manipulation are in [Respect, autonomy and consent](respect-and-consent.md), and conflicts of interest are in [Fairness and justice](fairness.md). Honesty in specific roles is in the domain files, for example in [research](../specific/research/README.md), [journalism](../specific/journalism/README.md) and [marketing](../specific/marketing/README.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `8` [Promises](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/promises/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): why promises bind, what makes them valid, and when they may be broken. It opens by noting that few moral judgements are more widely shared than that promises ought to be kept.
- `8` [The Definition of Lying and Deception](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/lying-definition/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): what counts as lying, and how lying differs from deceiving and misleading in other ways.
- `8` [Trust](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/trust/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): what trust and trustworthiness are, why trust lets us depend on others, and why it makes us vulnerable to them.
- `7` [Lying](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/lying/) (Markkula Center for Applied Ethics): why Kantian ethics, virtue ethics and utilitarianism each count lying as wrong, how far they allow exceptions, and why people overestimate the good their lies will do.
- `7` [Self-Deception](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/self-deception/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): how people come to believe what they want to believe against the evidence, and when they are responsible for it.

## Key rules

### Truthfulness

- `9` `HON-1` **Don't lie: don't state something you believe to be false in order to make someone believe it.** Lying takes away people's ability to make their own choices on the basis of the facts, and it damages the trust that everyone depends on. Every major ethical theory counts lying as wrong, even those that allow rare exceptions (see "Open questions"). ([Markkula Center, Lying](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/lying/), [SEP, The Definition of Lying and Deception](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/lying-definition/))
- `8` `HON-2` **Don't deceive by other means either: misleading but literally true statements, half-truths, selective facts or a false impression you leave uncorrected are deception too.** What matters is the false belief you mean to create, not whether each sentence you said was literally true. ([SEP, The Definition of Lying and Deception: Traditional Definition of Deception](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/lying-definition/#TraDefDec))
- `7` `HON-3` **Distrust your own case for a "helpful" lie: you have a stake in believing that it will do more good than harm, and you can't foresee its consequences.** Lies take on a life of their own, and the loss of trust when they come out is easy to underestimate. ([Markkula Center, Lying](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/lying/))
- `6` `HON-4` **Be honest with yourself: notice when you avoid or explain away evidence that would cost you something to accept.** Self-deception usually involves choices about which evidence to seek and how to weigh it, and people are responsible for those choices. ([SEP, Self-Deception: Moral Responsibility for Self-Deception](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/self-deception/#MoraRespForSelfDece))

### Promises and trust

- `9` `HON-5` **Keep your promises and agreements.** Few moral judgements are more widely shared, and the people you promised have planned their lives around your word. ([SEP, Promises](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/promises/))
- `8` `HON-6` **Don't make a promise you don't intend to keep.** A promise leads the other person to expect that you will do what you promised, so making one without the intention to keep it is a lie about your future conduct, and Kant counts refraining from such promises as a perfect duty, one that allows no exceptions. ([SEP, Promises](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/promises/), [SEP, Kant's Moral Philosophy: The Formula of the Universal Law of Nature](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/kant-moral/#ForUniLawNat), [Markkula Center, Lying](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/lying/))
- `7` `HON-7` **If you can't keep a promise, tell the person as early as you can, explain why, and do what you can to make up for it.** Early notice lets them make other plans, and it keeps the breach from becoming a deception too. ([SEP, Promises](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/promises/))
- `7` `HON-8` **Be trustworthy: be reliable in what you say you will do, especially where people depend on you and can't check.** Trust lets people depend on others without guarantees, which is exactly why breaking it harms them. ([SEP, Trust](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/trust/))

### Credit

- `8` `HON-9` **Give credit for other people's work and ideas, and never present them as your own.** Taking credit is a form of deception, and it treats the real author unfairly. Academic integrity rests on honesty first among its values, and the domain rules on plagiarism and authorship are in [research](../specific/research/README.md) and [journalism](../specific/journalism/README.md). ([ICAI, The Fundamental Values of Academic Integrity](https://academicintegrity.org/aws/ICAI/pt/sp/values))

## How to apply

- `6` [Ethics Unwrapped, Integrity](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/integrity): a short explainer of integrity as acting with honesty, fairness and decency, with videos for discussion in a team or class. Supports `HON-1` and `HON-8`.

## Open questions

- `7` [Markkula Center, Lying](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/lying/): is lying ever right, for example to protect someone from a person who means them harm, or to spare someone pain? Kant held that lying is always wrong; virtue ethicists see honesty as one virtue that can conflict with others, such as compassion; utilitarians allow a lie when it clearly does more good than harm, while their critics point to how hard that is to know.
