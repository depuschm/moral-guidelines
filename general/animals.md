# Animals

How to treat animals: recognising that they can feel, not causing them unnecessary suffering, caring well for the animals in your keeping, and choosing pets and products responsibly. The use of animals in research is in [Research with people and animals](../specific/research/README.md), and wild species and ecosystems in [Environment and future generations](environment.md). Animal welfare law differs between countries, and some rules below cite one country's law as an example; this is moral guidance, not legal or veterinary advice.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [Terrestrial Animal Health Code, Chapter 7.1: Introduction to the recommendations for animal welfare](https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahc/2024/en_chapitre_aw_introduction.htm) (World Organisation for Animal Health, 2024 edition): the intergovernmental standard's definition of animal welfare and its guiding principles, including the internationally recognised "five freedoms". WOAH's members [revised the chapter in 2025](https://www.woah.org/app/uploads/2025/07/92gs-2025-final-report-en-09525.pdf) to add the Five Domains model.
- `8` [The 2020 Five Domains Model: Including Human–Animal Interactions in Assessments of Animal Welfare](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7602120/) (David Mellor and others, Animals, 2020, open access): the model for assessing welfare through nutrition, the physical environment, health and behavioural interactions, and the animal's resulting mental state, including positive experiences.
- `8` [The Moral Status of Animals](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-animal/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): why animals matter morally, from sentience and agency to rights, and how their claims should be weighed against human interests.
- `7` [The New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness](https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/nydeclaration/declaration) (2024): a statement signed by scientists and philosophers that there is strong scientific support for conscious experience in mammals and birds, and at least a realistic possibility of it in all vertebrates and many invertebrates.
- `7` [Animal welfare: overview](https://food.ec.europa.eu/animals/animal-welfare/overview_en) (European Commission): how EU rules recognise animals as sentient beings, under Article 13 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, and follow the five freedoms.

## Key rules

### Sentience and suffering

- `9` `ANIMAL-1` **Never be cruel to an animal, and don't cause animals pain, fear or distress that isn't necessary.** Handling animals should not cause injury, panic, lasting fear or avoidable stress, and in England and Wales, for example, causing an animal unnecessary suffering is a criminal offence. The Earth Charter calls for preventing cruelty to animals, and leaders of many religions affirm that the lives of animals deserve protection and care. ([WOAH, Terrestrial Animal Health Code, Chapter 7.1](https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahc/2024/en_chapitre_aw_introduction.htm), [Animal Welfare Act 2006, section 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/45/section/4), [Parliament of the World's Religions, Towards a Global Ethic](https://www.weltethos.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Global-Ethic-Declaration_EN.pdf), [Earth Charter, Principle 15](https://earthcharter.org/read-the-earth-charter/democracy-nonviolence-and-peace/))
- `7` `ANIMAL-2` **Treat animals as beings that can feel pain, fear and pleasure, not as objects.** There is strong scientific support for conscious experience in mammals and birds, and EU law recognises animals as sentient beings. ([New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness](https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/nydeclaration/declaration), [European Commission, Animal welfare: overview](https://food.ec.europa.eu/animals/animal-welfare/overview_en), [SEP, The Moral Status of Animals: Sentience](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-animal/#Sen))
- `7` `ANIMAL-3` **If an animal has to be killed, for example because it is suffering and can't recover, have it killed quickly and humanely, by someone with the right skills.** Good welfare includes humane killing, and an animal with serious health problems should be treated promptly or killed humanely if treatment isn't feasible or recovery is unlikely. ([WOAH, Terrestrial Animal Health Code, Chapter 7.1](https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahc/2024/en_chapitre_aw_introduction.htm))
- `6` `ANIMAL-4` **Where it is realistically possible that an animal can suffer, as with fish, octopuses, crabs and insects, take that possibility into account in what you do to it.** When there is a realistic possibility of conscious experience in an animal, it is irresponsible to ignore it in decisions that affect that animal, and a review for the UK government recommended that octopuses, squid, crabs, lobsters and their relatives be regarded as sentient in animal welfare law. ([New York Declaration on Animal Consciousness](https://sites.google.com/nyu.edu/nydeclaration/declaration), [LSE, Review of the Evidence of Sentience in Cephalopod Molluscs and Decapod Crustaceans](https://www.lse.ac.uk/business/consulting/reports/review-of-the-evidence-of-sentiences-in-cephalopod-molluscs-and-decapod-crustaceans))
- `6` `ANIMAL-5` **Don't use hunting, trapping or fishing methods that cause extreme, prolonged or avoidable suffering, and avoid harming animals you didn't intend to catch.** The Earth Charter asks that wild animals be protected from such methods, and that the taking or destruction of non-targeted species be avoided as far as possible. ([Earth Charter, Principle 15](https://earthcharter.org/read-the-earth-charter/democracy-nonviolence-and-peace/))

### Animals in your care

- `8` `ANIMAL-6` **Meet the needs of every animal you are responsible for: suitable food and water, a suitable environment, company of its own kind or separation from others as it needs, the chance to behave normally, and protection from pain, injury and disease, with veterinary care when it is ill or hurt.** These needs follow the internationally recognised five freedoms, and the Animal Welfare Act 2006 makes meeting them a legal duty in England and Wales. ([WOAH, Terrestrial Animal Health Code, Chapter 7.1](https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahc/2024/en_chapitre_aw_introduction.htm), [Animal Welfare Act 2006, section 9](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2006/45/section/9))
- `6` `ANIMAL-7` **Aim for a good life, not just the absence of suffering: give the animals in your care chances for positive experiences, such as play, exploration, comfort and good contact with people.** An animal experiences good welfare when it is comfortable and able to express the behaviours that matter to it, and the Five Domains model assesses positive experiences as well as negative ones, including those that come from contact with people. ([WOAH, Terrestrial Animal Health Code, Chapter 7.1](https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahc/2024/en_chapitre_aw_introduction.htm), [Mellor and others, The 2020 Five Domains Model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7602120/))

### Choosing pets and products

- `7` `ANIMAL-8` **Before you take on a pet, make sure you can meet its needs for its whole life, in time, space, money and care when you are away, and never abandon it.** Owning an animal is a choice that comes with responsibilities for its lifetime, including arranging for its care when you can't provide it yourself. ([WOAH, Terrestrial Animal Health Code, Article 7.7.17: Promoting responsible dog ownership](https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahc/2024/en_chapitre_aw_stray_dog.htm#article_aw_stray_dog.17.))
- `6` `ANIMAL-9` **Get pets from people who treat animals well, such as rescue shelters and responsible breeders, and check where an animal comes from, especially when you buy online.** Illegal trade and fraudulent online listings are a known problem, and the EU has adopted rules on the breeding, sale and traceability of dogs and cats to tackle them. ([European Commission, Protecting the welfare of dogs and cats](https://food.ec.europa.eu/animals/animal-welfare/protecting-welfare-dogs-and-cats_en))
- `5` `ANIMAL-10` **Prevent unwanted breeding by your animals, and have them identified and registered where the law requires it.** Responsible ownership includes controlling your animal's reproduction, which prevents unwanted young and stray animals, and having it registered and identified. ([WOAH, Terrestrial Animal Health Code, Article 7.7.17: Promoting responsible dog ownership](https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahc/2024/en_chapitre_aw_stray_dog.htm#article_aw_stray_dog.17.))
- `5` `ANIMAL-11` **If you buy animal products, use the information available, such as welfare labels, to choose products from animals kept to higher welfare standards where you can.** Using animals carries an ethical responsibility to ensure their welfare as far as practicable. Whether to use animal products at all is an open question (see below). ([WOAH, Terrestrial Animal Health Code, Chapter 7.1](https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahc/2024/en_chapitre_aw_introduction.htm), [European Commission, Animal welfare labelling](https://food.ec.europa.eu/animals/animal-welfare/other-aspects-animal-welfare/animal-welfare-labelling_en))

## How to apply

- `8` [The 2020 Five Domains Model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7602120/) (Mellor and others): use the five domains (nutrition, physical environment, health, behavioural interactions and mental state) as a checklist to review how an animal in your care is doing, looking for good experiences as well as bad ones. Supports `ANIMAL-6` and `ANIMAL-7`.
- `7` [Promoting responsible dog ownership](https://www.woah.org/fileadmin/Home/eng/Health_standards/tahc/2024/en_chapitre_aw_stray_dog.htm#article_aw_stray_dog.17.) (WOAH, Terrestrial Animal Health Code, Article 7.7.17): the owner's responsibilities for a dog's lifetime, from welfare needs, training and veterinary care to registration, reproduction and care when the owner is away. Supports `ANIMAL-8` and `ANIMAL-10`.

## Open questions

- `8` [SEP, Moral Vegetarianism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/vegetarianism/): is it permissible to eat animals and use animal products? Arguments for moral vegetarianism start from what producing meat involves, such as causing pain, killing animals and harming the environment, and conclude that eating it is wrong too; moral omnivores hold that eating meat is permissible, and critics question whether every kind of farming is wrong and whether buying meat makes you responsible for how it was produced. Religious traditions differ on this too (see the [tradition pages](../traditions/README.md)).
- `8` [SEP, Animal Consciousness](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/consciousness-animal/): which animals can suffer, and how much should fish, crustaceans and insects count? A 2024 declaration signed by hundreds of researchers finds strong support for consciousness in mammals and birds and a realistic possibility in other vertebrates and many invertebrates, while some researchers think even that overstates the evidence and others disagree about which signs indicate consciousness. Philosophers have so far written little on the ethics of eating fish and insects ([SEP, Moral Vegetarianism: Sea Creatures and Insects](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/vegetarianism/#FishInse)).
- `7` [SEP, The Moral Status of Animals: How to Count Animals' Moral Claims](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-animal/#HowCouAniMorCla): how much do animals' interests count compared with humans'? Animal rights theorists hold that using animals as means to human ends violates their rights; utilitarians weigh equal interests equally, whoever has them; and other views give human beings a special moral status that animals lack.
