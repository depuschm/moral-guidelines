# Fairness and justice

Treating people as equals in everyday life: not discriminating, judging impartially, avoiding conflicts of interest, hearing both sides, dealing fairly in exchanges and sharing burdens and benefits fairly. Fair treatment at work is in [Workplace and leadership](../specific/workplace/README.md), impartiality and conflicts of interest in public office in [Public service](../specific/public-service/README.md), fair dealing and anti-corruption by companies in [Business and human rights](../specific/business/README.md), and fairness in automated decisions in [Artificial intelligence](../specific/ai/README.md). Truthfulness and promises are in [Honesty and trust](honesty.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `10` [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights) (United Nations, 1948): everyone is entitled to the rights it sets out "without distinction of any kind" (Article 2), is equal before the law and protected against discrimination (Article 7), and is entitled to a fair hearing (Article 10). **The baseline for equal treatment.**
- `9` [International Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination (ICERD)](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Treaties/1969/03/19690312%2008-49%20AM/Ch_IV_2p.pdf) (United Nations, 1965; certified text in the UN Treaty Collection): defines racial discrimination by race, colour, descent, or national or ethnic origin, and commits states to end it, including by any person, group or organisation.
- `9` [Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women (CEDAW)](https://www.un.org/womenwatch/daw/cedaw/text/econvention.htm) (United Nations, 1979): defines discrimination against women, and commits states to end it, including by any person, organisation or enterprise, and to change prejudices and practices based on stereotyped roles for men and women.
- `9` [Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD)](https://social.desa.un.org/issues/disability/crpd/convention-on-the-rights-of-persons-with-disabilities-crpd) (United Nations, 2006): equality and non-discrimination for disabled people, including the duty to provide reasonable accommodation, with each article on its own page.
- `8` [Discrimination](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/discrimination/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): what discrimination is, its direct and indirect forms, and what makes it wrong.
- `8` [Impartiality](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/impartiality/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): what impartiality requires, in which roles it is required, and how it fits with the partiality we show to family and friends.
- `7` [Justice and Fairness](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/justice-and-fairness/) (Markkula Center for Applied Ethics): a short essay on the principle that equals should be treated equally, and on distributive, retributive and compensatory justice.

## Key rules

### Equal treatment

- `9` `FAIR-1` **Don't treat anyone worse because of their race, colour, ethnic or national origin, sex, language, religion, political or other opinion, disability, social origin, birth or other status.** Treating people unequally on the basis of characteristics that are arbitrary and irrelevant to the situation violates their equal dignity, and the UN conventions ask states to end such discrimination by any person or organisation, not only by the state. ([UN, UDHR Articles 2 and 7](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights), [UN Treaty Collection, ICERD Articles 1 and 2](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Treaties/1969/03/19690312%2008-49%20AM/Ch_IV_2p.pdf), [UN, CEDAW Articles 1 and 2](https://www.un.org/womenwatch/daw/cedaw/text/econvention.htm), [UN DESA, CRPD Article 5](https://social.desa.un.org/issues/disability/crpd/article-5-equality-and-non-discrimination), [Markkula Center, Justice and Fairness](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/justice-and-fairness/))
- `8` `FAIR-2` **Judge each person on what is relevant to the decision, not on stereotypes about their group, and check your decisions for hidden bias.** People should be treated the same unless they differ in ways that are relevant to the situation, and unconscious bias can lead even well-meaning people to judge others by negative stereotypes. ([Markkula Center, Justice and Fairness](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/justice-and-fairness/), [UN, CEDAW Article 5](https://www.un.org/womenwatch/daw/cedaw/text/econvention.htm), [Ethics Unwrapped, Implicit Bias](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/implicit-bias))
- `8` `FAIR-3` **Make reasonable adjustments so that disabled people can take part on an equal basis with others, unless an adjustment would be a disproportionate or undue burden.** The CRPD defines reasonable accommodation this way, and counts denying it as a form of discrimination. ([UN DESA, CRPD Article 2](https://social.desa.un.org/issues/disability/crpd/article-2-definitions), [UN DESA, CRPD Article 5](https://social.desa.un.org/issues/disability/crpd/article-5-equality-and-non-discrimination))

### Impartiality and conflicts of interest

- `8` `FAIR-4` **When you judge, select or share out on behalf of others, as a referee, teacher, examiner, manager or official, apply the same standards to everyone and don't favour yourself, your friends or your family.** Ordinary morality allows partiality to loved ones in private life but requires impartiality in such roles, and both the Torah and the Qur'an call for judgement that favours neither rich nor poor, nor oneself or one's relatives. ([SEP, Impartiality](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/impartiality/), [Sefaria, Leviticus 19:15](https://www.sefaria.org/Leviticus.19.15), [Quran.com, An-Nisa 4:135](https://quran.com/an-nisa/135))
- `8` `FAIR-5` **If a personal interest could sway a decision you make for others, say so openly, and step aside from the decision or give up the interest.** A conflict of interest arises when what is good for you is not good for someone you owe loyalty to; even an apparent conflict damages trust, so declare it early and let someone independent decide. ([Ethics Unwrapped, Conflict of Interest](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/conflict-of-interest), [OECD, Guidelines for Managing Conflict of Interest in the Public Service](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/public/doc/130/body-text.en.html))
- `8` `FAIR-6` **Never offer or accept a bribe: money, a gift or a favour given to get someone to act against their duties.** The UN Convention against Corruption asks states to make bribery a crime, in the private sector as well as in public office, and the Torah warns that "bribes blind the clear-sighted". ([UNODC, UN Convention against Corruption, Articles 15 and 21](https://www.unodc.org/documents/treaties/UNCAC/Publications/Convention/08-50026_E.pdf), [Sefaria, Exodus 23:8](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.23.8))

### Fair procedures

- `8` `FAIR-7` **Hear both sides before you judge a dispute or blame someone, and give the person accused a chance to answer.** A fair hearing, in which each party can present their case and respond to the other's, is a basic principle of justice in law and in the traditions. ([UN, UDHR Article 10](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights), [SEP, The Rule of Law: Procedural Aspects](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rule-of-law/#ProAsp), [Sefaria, Deuteronomy 1:16-17](https://www.sefaria.org/Deuteronomy.1.16-17))
- `7` `FAIR-8` **Decide by criteria that are known in advance, apply them the same way each time, and explain your reasons.** Known, general and consistently applied rules let people plan and see that they were treated like others, and reasons let them check and challenge a decision. ([SEP, The Rule of Law](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/rule-of-law/), [Markkula Center, Justice and Fairness](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/justice-and-fairness/))

### Fair exchange and fair shares

- `8` `FAIR-9` **Deal fairly when you buy, sell, lend or hire: give honest measure and value, and don't exploit someone's ignorance, need or dependence to get terms they would never otherwise accept.** Exploitation means taking unfair advantage of another person's vulnerability, and honest weights and measures and trade by mutual consent are ancient requirements. ([SEP, Exploitation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/exploitation/), [Parliament of the World's Religions, Towards a Global Ethic](https://www.weltethos.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Global-Ethic-Declaration_EN.pdf), [Sefaria, Leviticus 19:35-36](https://www.sefaria.org/Leviticus.19.35-36), [Quran.com, An-Nisa 4:29](https://quran.com/an-nisa/29))
- `8` `FAIR-10` **Pay people fairly and on time for their work, with equal pay for equal work.** Everyone who works has a right to just pay without discrimination, and the Torah requires wages to be paid on the day they are due because workers depend on them. The rules for employers are in [Workplace and leadership](../specific/workplace/README.md). ([UN, UDHR Article 23](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights), [UN Treaty Collection, ICESCR Article 7](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Treaties/1976/01/19760103%2009-57%20PM/Ch_IV_03.pdf), [Sefaria, Deuteronomy 24:14-15](https://www.sefaria.org/Deuteronomy.24.14-15))
- `7` `FAIR-11` **Do your fair share of shared work and costs, and don't free-ride on the efforts of others.** If you accept the benefits of a fair joint effort, the others who keep to its rules have a claim that you do your part too. ([SEP, The Free Rider Problem](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/free-rider/))
- `6` `FAIR-12` **When you share something out among people, agree openly on a fair basis, such as equal shares, need or effort, and apply it to everyone in the same way.** Equals should be treated equally and unequals unequally, in proportion to differences that are relevant to what is being shared. ([Markkula Center, Justice and Fairness](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/justice-and-fairness/), [SEP, Distributive Justice](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-distributive/))

## How to apply

- `8` [A Framework for Ethical Decision Making](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/a-framework-for-ethical-decision-making/) (Markkula Center for Applied Ethics): its justice lens asks which option treats people fairly, giving each what they are due by a defensible standard such as merit or need. Supports `FAIR-2`, `FAIR-4` and `FAIR-12`.
- `7` [OECD Guidelines for Managing Conflict of Interest in the Public Service](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/public/doc/130/body-text.en.html) (OECD, 2003): definitions of actual, apparent and potential conflicts, and the ways to resolve them, such as declaring, recusal, divesting the interest or giving up the role. Written for public officials, but the methods work anywhere. Supports `FAIR-5`.
- `6` [Implicit Bias](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/implicit-bias) (Ethics Unwrapped): a short video and explainer on unconscious bias and the safeguards that reduce its effect on decisions. Supports `FAIR-2`.

## Open questions

- `8` [SEP, Distributive Justice](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-distributive/): what is a just distribution of income, wealth and opportunity in a society? Strict egalitarians want equal shares; Rawls's difference principle allows inequalities that benefit the worst off; luck egalitarians, welfare-based, desert-based, libertarian and feminist theories each propose a different basis.
- `8` [SEP, Affirmative Action](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/affirmative-action/): is it fair to give preference to members of groups that have been excluded in the past, for example in hiring or admissions? Supporters see it as redress for past exclusion or as serving goods such as diversity; critics see it as a violation of equal consideration and of selection on merit.
- `7` [SEP, Impartiality: Partiality and Impartiality](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/impartiality/#PartImpa): how much may you favour family, friends and your own community over strangers? Some philosophers hold that morality is impartial through and through; many others think it also allows, or even requires, partiality in close relationships, but there is no agreed account of where the line falls.

## Tutorials and further reading

- `8` [Equality of Opportunity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/equal-opportunity/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): the different ideas behind "equal opportunity", from careers open to talent to correcting for social disadvantage.
- `8` [Equality](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/equality/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): the concept of equality, the principles of equal treatment and equal respect, and debates about what should be equal.
- `8` [Exploitation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/exploitation/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): what makes taking advantage of someone's vulnerability unfair, in single transactions and in whole systems.
