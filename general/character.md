# Character and values

What values and virtues are, which virtues cultures widely share, how people build good character through practice and example, and integrity: acting consistently on your values. Virtue ethics as a theory, beside the other moral theories, is in [Moral theories](moral-theories.md). Honesty, care and fairness have their own files ([Honesty and trust](honesty.md), [Harm and care](harm-and-care.md), [Fairness and justice](fairness.md)), and owning your mistakes and standing up to pressure to do wrong are in [Responsibility and accountability](responsibility.md). What each religious and philosophical tradition teaches about the virtues is on the [tradition pages](../traditions/README.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `8` [Virtue Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-virtue/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): what a virtue is, why virtues need practical wisdom, and the main forms of virtue ethics, including the exemplarist view that we learn what goodness is from people we admire. Its roots are traced to Plato and Aristotle in the West and to Confucius and Mencius in the East.
- `8` [Integrity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/integrity/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): the main accounts of integrity, from integrating the self to standing for something, and the traits that undermine it, from hypocrisy and self-deception to dogmatism and fanaticism.
- `8` [Moral Character](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-character/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): how philosophers from the ancient Greeks to today have understood good character and how it is formed, and the challenge to it from social psychology.
- `8` [Nicomachean Ethics, Book II](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html) (Aristotle, translated by W. D. Ross, Internet Classics Archive): the founding text on how moral virtue comes from habit, and on each virtue as a mean between too much and too little.
- `7` [Ethics and Virtue](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/ethics-and-virtue/) (Markkula Center for Applied Ethics): a short introduction to virtues as character traits that people develop through learning and practice, within their communities and from role models.
- `6` [The 24 character strengths](https://www.viacharacter.org/character-strengths) (VIA Institute on Character): the classification of character strengths from a study led by the psychologists Christopher Peterson and Martin Seligman ([VIA Institute, About](https://www.viacharacter.org/about)), grouped under six virtues that the institute describes as universal across cultures: wisdom, courage, humanity, justice, temperance and transcendence.

## Key rules

### Values and virtues

- `7` `CHAR-1` **Know your values: think through and name the few values you most want to live by, before a hard choice tests them.** People tend to adopt the values they were raised with, and values often conflict, so reflecting on yours lets you hold them as your own and weigh them when they clash. Giving Voice to Values advises defining your purpose explicitly before conflicts arise. ([Ethics Unwrapped, Values](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/values), [Ethics Unwrapped, Giving Voice to Values](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/series/giving-voice-to-values))
- `7` `CHAR-2` **Cultivate the virtues that cultures widely recognise, such as practical wisdom, courage, justice, self-control, compassion, honesty, humility and gratitude.** The VIA classification of character strengths groups them under six broad virtues that it describes as universal across cultures, and philosophers' lists of the virtues overlap with it widely. ([VIA Institute, The 24 character strengths](https://www.viacharacter.org/character-strengths), [Markkula Center, Ethics and Virtue](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/ethics-and-virtue/), [SEP, Virtue Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-virtue/))
- `7` `CHAR-3` **Use practical wisdom: work out what each virtue asks in the situation in front of you, so that it doesn't turn into a fault.** Courage without judgement becomes recklessness and honesty becomes tactlessness. Aristotle described each virtue as the response that fits the situation, neither too much nor too little. ([SEP, Virtue Ethics: Practical Wisdom](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-virtue/#PracWisd), [Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics, Book II](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html))

### Building character

- `7` `CHAR-4` **Build good character by practice: act honestly, fairly, kindly and bravely in small everyday matters, until it becomes your habit.** Aristotle observed that we become just by doing just acts and brave by doing brave acts, and that moral virtue comes from habit, as skills do from training. ([Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics, Book II](https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.2.ii.html), [Markkula Center, Ethics and Virtue](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/ethics-and-virtue/), [Ethics Unwrapped, Virtue Ethics](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/virtue-ethics))
- `6` `CHAR-5` **Learn from good examples: notice the people you admire, work out what makes them admirable, and let that guide what you do.** Communities pass on character through the role models they hold up, and on the exemplarist view our understanding of the virtues grows out of admiring good people. ([Markkula Center, Ethics and Virtue](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/ethics-and-virtue/), [SEP, Virtue Ethics: Agent-Based and Exemplarist Virtue Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-virtue/#AgenBaseExemVirtEthi))
- `6` `CHAR-6` **Choose your company and surroundings with care, because the people, groups and habits around you shape your character and your conduct.** Character develops within the communities you belong to, and experiments in social psychology suggest that the situation people are in affects what they do more than they expect. ([Markkula Center, Ethics and Virtue](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/ethics-and-virtue/), [SEP, Moral Character: The challenge posed by situationism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-character/#ChaPosSit))
- `6` `CHAR-7` **Examine your conduct regularly: look back at what you did, where you fell short of your values and what you will do differently next time.** Socrates held that "the life which is unexamined is not worth living", and knowing what has helped and hindered you in the past makes it easier to act on your values next time. ([Plato, Apology](https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/apology.html), [Ethics Unwrapped, Giving Voice to Values](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/series/giving-voice-to-values))
- `5` `CHAR-8` **When you raise or teach children, help them build character by example, guidance and practice, not by rules alone.** Virtue ethics has always treated moral education as the training of character rather than the drilling of rules, and children learn from the traits their communities encourage. ([SEP, Virtue Ethics: Future Directions](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-virtue/#FutuDire), [Markkula Center, Ethics and Virtue](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/ethics-and-virtue/))

### Integrity

- `7` `CHAR-9` **Act on your values consistently: in public and in private, at home and at work, and also when it costs you something.** Integrity means living by your principles, including honesty, fairness and decency, whatever the circumstances; in a saying attributed to C. S. Lewis, it is "doing the right thing, even when no one is looking", and it often takes moral courage. ([Ethics Unwrapped, Integrity](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/integrity), [SEP, Integrity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/integrity/))
- `7` `CHAR-10` **Let your deeds match your words: don't demand of others what you excuse in yourself, and don't claim values you don't act on.** Hypocrisy, self-deception and weakness of will are among the main ways integrity fails. ([SEP, Integrity: Integrity as a Virtue](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/integrity/#InteVirt), [Ethics Unwrapped, Integrity](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/integrity))
- `6` `CHAR-11` **Hold your values with humility: listen to people who see things differently, and change your mind when you find that you were wrong.** Dogmatism, fanaticism and rigidity defeat integrity too; on one influential account, people of integrity stand up for their best judgement while respecting the judgement of others. ([SEP, Integrity: Integrity as Standing for Something](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/integrity/#InteStanForSome), [SEP, Integrity: Integrity as a Virtue](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/integrity/#InteVirt))

## How to apply

- `6` [Giving Voice to Values](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/series/giving-voice-to-values) (Mary Gentile, videos by Ethics Unwrapped): seven principles and short videos on acting on your values: define your purpose before conflicts arise, know what has helped you before, and script and rehearse what you will say. Supports `CHAR-1`, `CHAR-7` and `CHAR-9`.
- `5` [VIA Survey of character strengths](https://www.viacharacter.org/survey/account/register) (VIA Institute on Character): a free ten-minute questionnaire that ranks your 24 character strengths, as a starting point for reflecting on which strengths you use and which you want to develop. Supports `CHAR-1` and `CHAR-2`.

## Open questions

- `7` [SEP, Moral Character: The challenge posed by situationism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-character/#ChaPosSit): do stable character traits exist at all? Situationist philosophers, drawing on experiments in social psychology, argue that behaviour depends mostly on the situation rather than on robust traits; their critics reply that the situationists rely on a simplified idea of traits, and that character includes judgement and can be imperfect without being absent.
- `7` [IEP, Virtue Ethics](https://iep.utm.edu/virtue/): is there one set of virtues for everyone? Some point to the broad overlap between the virtues that different traditions praise; others, following Alasdair MacIntyre, hold that each account of the virtues depends on the practices and tradition it comes from, so the lists can't simply be merged. The [VIA classification](https://www.viacharacter.org/character-strengths) is an example of the first view.
