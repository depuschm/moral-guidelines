# Respect, autonomy and consent

Treating every person as someone with equal dignity who runs their own life: respecting their choices, asking for their consent, respecting their privacy, influencing them only by honest means, and knowing the limits of acting "for their own good". Lying and deception are in [Honesty and trust](honesty.md), discrimination is in [Fairness and justice](fairness.md), and the right to freedom of thought and expression is in [Human rights](human-rights.md). Consent in specific settings is in the domain files: to medical treatment in [Healthcare](../specific/healthcare/README.md), to research in [Research with people and animals](../specific/research/README.md), to the use of personal data in [Data and analytics](../specific/data/README.md), and to sharing other people's images online in [Online life and social media](../specific/online/README.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `10` [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights) (United Nations, 1948): every human being is "born free and equal in dignity and rights" (Article 1), and no one may be subjected to arbitrary interference with their privacy, family, home or correspondence (Article 12). **The baseline for respect for persons.**
- `8` [Autonomy in Moral and Political Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/autonomy-moral/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): what it means to be your own person and live by reasons that are your own, and why autonomy is central in both Kantian and Millian ethics.
- `8` [Informed Consent](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/informed-consent/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): what makes consent informed, voluntary and given by someone able to decide, and why consent makes otherwise wrongful interventions legitimate.
- `8` [Privacy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/privacy/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): what privacy is, why it matters, the main accounts of it, such as control over access to information about yourself, and how it conflicts with other values.
- `8` [The Ethics of Manipulation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-manipulation/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): what manipulation is, how it differs from persuasion and coercion, and why it is usually wrong, with everyday examples such as guilt trips and peer pressure.
- `8` [Paternalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/paternalism/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): interfering with people's choices for their own good, and the arguments for and against it.

## Key rules

### Dignity and autonomy

- `9` `RESP-1` **Treat every person as someone with equal dignity, never merely as a tool for your own purposes.** All human beings are equal in dignity and rights, and Kant's principle that persons must never be treated merely as means is at the heart of respect for persons. ([UN, UDHR Article 1](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights), [SEP, Respect](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/respect/), [Markkula Center, Rights](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/rights/))
- `8` `RESP-2` **Respect people's right to make their own decisions about their own lives, even decisions you think unwise.** Autonomy, living by reasons that are your own, is central in both Kantian and Millian ethics, and the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities names "the freedom to make one's own choices" among its general principles; the limits are harm to others and the cases in `RESP-11`. ([SEP, Autonomy in Moral and Political Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/autonomy-moral/), [UN DESA, CRPD Article 3](https://social.desa.un.org/issues/disability/crpd/article-3-general-principles), [NHS, Mental Capacity Act](https://www.nhs.uk/social-care-and-support/making-decisions-for-someone-else/mental-capacity-act/))
- `8` `RESP-3` **When someone finds it hard to decide, help them decide for themselves; if you must decide for them, follow their wishes, values and preferences as far as you can.** Assume a person can decide unless it is shown otherwise, give them the support they need, and choose the option that restricts their rights and freedom least. ([UN DESA, CRPD Article 12](https://social.desa.un.org/issues/disability/crpd/article-12-equal-recognition-before-the-law), [NHS, Mental Capacity Act](https://www.nhs.uk/social-care-and-support/making-decisions-for-someone-else/mental-capacity-act/))
- `7` `RESP-4` **Listen to children, and give their views a weight that grows with their age and maturity.** The Convention on the Rights of the Child gives every child who can form views the right to express them freely in all matters that affect them. ([UNICEF, CRC Article 12](https://www.unicef.org/child-rights-convention/convention-text))

### Consent

- `9` `RESP-5` **Get a person's consent before you do something to their body or their private life, and respect a refusal.** Without informed and voluntary consent, a substantial intervention in someone's body or private sphere is wrong, however well meant; for their belongings, see `HARM-3` and `RESP-8`. ([SEP, Informed Consent](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/informed-consent/), [UN Treaty Collection, ICCPR Article 7](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Treaties/1976/03/19760323%2006-17%20AM/Ch_IV_04.pdf))
- `9` `RESP-6` **Make sure consent is real: the person understands what they are agreeing to, agrees freely without pressure or threats, and is able to decide.** Someone too drunk or drugged to understand what is happening can't consent; consent obtained by deceiving someone isn't informed either (`HON-2`). In England and Wales, the law on sexual offences defines consent as agreeing by choice with the freedom and capacity to make that choice. ([NHS, Consent to treatment](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/consent-to-treatment/), [SEP, Informed Consent](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/informed-consent/), [legislation.gov.uk, Sexual Offences Act 2003, section 74](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2003/42/section/74), [Crown Prosecution Service, Rape and sexual offences guidance (England and Wales)](https://www.cps.gov.uk/prosecution-guidance/rape-and-sexual-offences-prosecution-guidance-full-page-version))
- `8` `RESP-7` **Treat consent as specific and revocable: agreeing to one thing is not agreeing to another, agreeing once is not agreeing every time, and anyone can change their mind at any point.** ([NHS, Consent to treatment](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/consent-to-treatment/), [Crown Prosecution Service, Rape and sexual offences guidance (England and Wales)](https://www.cps.gov.uk/prosecution-guidance/rape-and-sexual-offences-prosecution-guidance-full-page-version))

### Privacy

- `8` `RESP-8` **Respect other people's privacy: don't read their messages, search their belongings, track them or record them in private without their agreement.** Everyone has the right to protection from arbitrary interference with their privacy, home and correspondence, and the Qur'an forbids spying on one another. ([UN, UDHR Article 12](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights), [UN Treaty Collection, ICCPR Article 17](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Treaties/1976/03/19760323%2006-17%20AM/Ch_IV_04.pdf), [Quran.com, Al-Hujurat 49:12](https://quran.com/al-hujurat/12))
- `7` `RESP-9` **Keep what people tell you in confidence, and don't spread personal information or gossip that could hurt them.** Disclosing what you were asked to keep confidential is a breach of trust, privacy on a common account means control over who has access to information about you, and attacks on someone's honour and reputation are a recognised wrong. If keeping a confidence would leave someone in serious danger, helping them (`HARM-6`) comes first. ([SEP, Privacy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/privacy/), [Fabre, The Morality of Gossip](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:d4cfcf17-1414-422f-bc09-46c310e0d59f/files/sw6634524c), [UN, UDHR Article 12](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights), [Quran.com, Al-Hujurat 49:12](https://quran.com/al-hujurat/12))

### Influence and paternalism

- `8` `RESP-10` **Influence people only by honest means, such as reasons, evidence and sincere requests: don't coerce them with threats, and don't manipulate them with guilt trips, emotional blackmail, peer pressure, stoked-up fears or by making them doubt their own judgement.** Coercion forces a choice through threats, and manipulation gets round people's reasoning instead of engaging it; both deny them a choice of their own. Deceptive design is covered in [Product and UX design](../specific/design/README.md). ([SEP, The Ethics of Manipulation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-manipulation/), [SEP, Coercion](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/coercion/))
- `7` `RESP-11` **Before you override someone's choice for their own good, make sure they know the risks and are able to decide; warn, inform and offer help rather than force, and intervene only as far as needed.** Stopping someone who is unaware of a danger, like Mill's example of a person about to cross an unsafe bridge, is widely accepted; overriding an informed, voluntary choice is contested (see "Open questions"). ([SEP, Paternalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/paternalism/), [NHS, Mental Capacity Act](https://www.nhs.uk/social-care-and-support/making-decisions-for-someone-else/mental-capacity-act/))

## How to apply

- `8` [A Framework for Ethical Decision Making](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/a-framework-for-ethical-decision-making/) (Markkula Center for Applied Ethics): its rights lens asks which option best respects the dignity and rights of everyone affected, including their right to choose for themselves. Supports `RESP-1` and `RESP-2`.
- `6` [Mental Capacity Act](https://www.nhs.uk/social-care-and-support/making-decisions-for-someone-else/mental-capacity-act/) (NHS): the Act's principles for England and Wales as a practical checklist: assume capacity, help people decide, accept unwise decisions, act in their best interests and choose the least restrictive option. Supports `RESP-3` and `RESP-11`.

## Open questions

- `8` [SEP, Paternalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/paternalism/): may you ever override a person's informed and voluntary choice for their own good? Soft paternalists, following Mill, allow interference only to make sure a choice is informed and voluntary; hard paternalists hold that it is sometimes justified even then, for example to prevent grave and irreversible harm.
- `8` [SEP, The Ethics of Manipulation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-manipulation/): are "nudges", which steer choices through defaults, framing or the order of options, a form of manipulation? Defenders point out that every choice has to be presented in some way, so framing it deliberately is not wrong in itself; critics say nudges that exploit biases bypass people's reasoning.

## Tutorials and further reading

- `8` [Dignity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dignity/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): the many meanings of human dignity, from rank and honour to the equal basic worth that grounds human rights.
- `8` [Personal Autonomy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/personal-autonomy/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): what it takes to be a self-governing person, and the competing accounts of the conditions for it.
- `7` [Rights](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/rights/) (Markkula Center for Applied Ethics): a short essay on moral rights, grounded in Kant's idea that each person's dignity includes the freedom to choose for themselves.
