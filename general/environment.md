# Environment and future generations

Caring for the natural environment and for the people who will live after us: sustainability, the interests of future generations, precaution, paying for the damage you cause, climate change, and the right to a clean, healthy and sustainable environment. The rules address people in everyday life and the organisations they run. Most of the instruments below were adopted by states and bind them, not individuals; this is moral guidance, not legal advice. The welfare of animals is in [Animals](animals.md), human rights in general in [Human rights](human-rights.md), companies' wider responsibilities in [Business and human rights](../specific/business/README.md), and honest environmental claims in advertising in [Marketing and advertising](../specific/marketing/README.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [Rio Declaration on Environment and Development](https://www.un.org/esa/documents/ga/conf151/aconf15126-1.htm) (UN Conference on Environment and Development, 1992): 27 principles agreed by states, including development that meets the needs of present and future generations (Principle 3), public participation (10), the precautionary approach (15), the polluter pays (16) and environmental impact assessment (17).
- `9` [AR6 Synthesis Report: Summary for Policymakers](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/syr/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_SYR_SPM.pdf) (Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change, 2023): the scientific consensus that human activities have unequivocally caused global warming, its impacts and risks, and the options to limit it and adapt.
- `8` [Our Common Future](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/n87/184/67/pdf/n8718467.pdf) (World Commission on Environment and Development, the Brundtland report, UN document A/42/427, 1987): the report that defined sustainable development as development that "meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs".
- `8` [Resolution 76/300, The human right to a clean, healthy and sustainable environment](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/n22/442/77/pdf/n2244277.pdf) (UN General Assembly, 2022): recognises that right as a human right and calls on states, businesses and others to scale up efforts to secure it for all.
- `8` [Environmental Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-environmental/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): the main positions on the moral standing of nature, from human-centred to life-centred and ecosystem-centred views, and their bearing on sustainability and climate change.
- `8` [Declaration on the Responsibilities of the Present Generations Towards Future Generations](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/declaration-responsibilities-present-generations-towards-future-generations) (UNESCO, 1997): what the present generations owe future ones, including an Earth not irreversibly damaged, protection from pollution and preserved natural resources.
- `7` [The Earth Charter](https://earthcharter.org/read-the-earth-charter/) (Earth Charter Commission, 2000): a declaration of sixteen principles for a just, sustainable and peaceful world, agreed after a worldwide consultation as a civil society initiative ([history](https://earthcharter.org/about-the-earth-charter/history/)).

## Key rules

### Sustainability and future generations

- `8` `ENV-1` **Meet your needs in ways that don't compromise the ability of future generations to meet theirs.** Each generation holds the Earth only for a time, and should hand it on without irreversible damage, using natural resources reasonably. Leaders of many religions share the commitment to consider the effects of our actions on future generations. ([Our Common Future, Chapter 2](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/n87/184/67/pdf/n8718467.pdf), [UNESCO, Declaration on the Responsibilities of the Present Generations Towards Future Generations, Article 4](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/declaration-responsibilities-present-generations-towards-future-generations), [Parliament of the World's Religions, Towards a Global Ethic, commitment 5](https://www.weltethos.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Global-Ethic-Declaration_EN.pdf))
- `8` `ENV-2` **Don't pollute: keep waste, chemicals and other harmful substances out of the air, water and soil, and dispose of waste properly.** Pollution endangers people's health, and protecting future generations from it is a responsibility of the present ones. ([UNESCO, Declaration on the Responsibilities of the Present Generations Towards Future Generations, Article 5](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/declaration-responsibilities-present-generations-towards-future-generations), [Earth Charter, Ecological Integrity, Principle 6](https://earthcharter.org/read-the-earth-charter/ecological-integrity/), [UN ActNow, Ten actions](https://www.un.org/en/actnow/ten-actions))
- `7` `ENV-3` **Respect everyone's right to a clean, healthy and sustainable environment, and give special weight to the people most exposed to environmental harm.** The UN General Assembly recognised the right in 2022, and the communities that have contributed least to climate change are disproportionately affected by it. ([UN General Assembly, Resolution 76/300](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/n22/442/77/pdf/n2244277.pdf), [IPCC, AR6 Synthesis Report: Summary for Policymakers, A.2](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/syr/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_SYR_SPM.pdf))
- `6` `ENV-4` **Use resources sparingly: buy less, choose durable and repairable products, reuse, repair and recycle, and waste less food.** Reducing waste through prevention, reuse and recycling is a Sustainable Development Goal target, and using longer-lived, repairable products and wasting less food also cut emissions. ([UN, Sustainable Development Goal 12, target 12.5](https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal12), [Earth Charter, Ecological Integrity, Principle 7](https://earthcharter.org/read-the-earth-charter/ecological-integrity/), [IPCC, AR6 Working Group III: Summary for Policymakers, C.10](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGIII_SPM.pdf))

### Precaution and responsibility for damage

- `8` `ENV-5` **When an action could cause serious or irreversible harm to the environment, don't wait for full scientific certainty before taking reasonable steps to prevent it.** This is the precautionary approach: lack of full certainty is no reason to postpone cost-effective measures against such harm. ([Rio Declaration, Principle 15](https://www.un.org/esa/documents/ga/conf151/aconf15126-1.htm), [Earth Charter, Ecological Integrity, Principle 6](https://earthcharter.org/read-the-earth-charter/ecological-integrity/))
- `7` `ENV-6` **Before a project or decision with lasting effects, such as a building, a new product or an investment, assess its impact on the environment and on people not yet born, and choose the less harmful option.** The Rio Declaration calls for environmental impact assessments of activities likely to have a significant adverse impact, and UNESCO's declaration says the present generations should consider the consequences of major projects for future generations before they are carried out. ([Rio Declaration, Principle 17](https://www.un.org/esa/documents/ga/conf151/aconf15126-1.htm), [UNESCO, Declaration on the Responsibilities of the Present Generations Towards Future Generations, Article 5](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/declaration-responsibilities-present-generations-towards-future-generations))
- `7` `ENV-7` **Bear the cost of the environmental damage you cause: clean up, restore and compensate, rather than leaving the cost to others.** The polluter should, in principle, bear the cost of pollution, which reflects the widely held principle that agents are responsible for their actions. ([Rio Declaration, Principle 16](https://www.un.org/esa/documents/ga/conf151/aconf15126-1.htm), [Earth Charter, Ecological Integrity, Principle 6](https://earthcharter.org/read-the-earth-charter/ecological-integrity/), [SEP, Climate Justice: The Polluter Pays Principle](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-climate/#PollPaysPrin))

### Climate change

- `7` `ENV-8` **Reduce the greenhouse gas emissions of your own life where you reasonably can, starting with the largest sources, such as travel, heating and cooling, food and the things you buy.** Changes in demand and behaviour could cut emissions from end uses by 40 to 70% by 2050 compared with baseline scenarios, and better-off people, who contribute disproportionately to emissions, have the greatest potential to reduce them. How far this is an individual duty is an open question (see below). ([IPCC, AR6 Working Group III: Summary for Policymakers, C.10](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGIII_SPM.pdf), [Parliament of the World's Religions, Towards a Global Ethic, commitment 5](https://www.weltethos.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Global-Ethic-Declaration_EN.pdf), [UN ActNow, Ten actions](https://www.un.org/en/actnow/ten-actions))
- `7` `ENV-9` **If you run or influence an organisation, measure its greenhouse gas emissions, including in its value chain, set reduction targets, and report progress honestly and openly; don't count purchased carbon credits as your own reductions.** A credible net-zero commitment has interim targets, a published transition plan and independently verified annual reporting. ([GHG Protocol, Corporate Standard](https://ghgprotocol.org/corporate-standard), [UN High-Level Expert Group, Integrity Matters](https://www.un.org/en/climatechange/high-level-expert-group), [UN, Sustainable Development Goal 12, target 12.6](https://sdgs.un.org/goals/goal12))
- `6` `ENV-10` **Use your voice as a citizen, employee, investor and consumer to support action on climate change and the environment.** People can influence emissions in all these roles, environmental issues are best handled with the participation of all concerned citizens, and leaders of many religions call on people to speak up where abuse of the environment is tolerated. ([IPCC, AR6 Working Group III: Summary for Policymakers, C.10](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGIII_SPM.pdf), [Rio Declaration, Principle 10](https://www.un.org/esa/documents/ga/conf151/aconf15126-1.htm), [Parliament of the World's Religions, Towards a Global Ethic, commitment 5](https://www.weltethos.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Global-Ethic-Declaration_EN.pdf))

## How to apply

- `8` [Corporate Standard](https://ghgprotocol.org/corporate-standard) (Greenhouse Gas Protocol): requirements and guidance for preparing an organisation's greenhouse gas inventory, from the publisher of the [world's most widely used](https://ghgprotocol.org/about-us) greenhouse gas accounting standards, with guidance on emissions from purchased energy. Supports `ENV-9`.
- `7` [Integrity Matters: Net Zero Commitments by Businesses, Financial Institutions, Cities and Regions](https://www.un.org/en/climatechange/high-level-expert-group) (UN High-Level Expert Group on Net Zero, 2022): ten recommendations, with an implementation checklist, for credible net-zero pledges and transition plans. Supports `ENV-9`.
- `6` [Ten actions](https://www.un.org/en/actnow/ten-actions) (UN ActNow): ten everyday actions to reduce your environmental impact, from home energy, travel and food to buying less and speaking up. Supports `ENV-2`, `ENV-4`, `ENV-8` and `ENV-10`.

## Open questions

- `8` [SEP, Intergenerational Justice](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-intergenerational/): how much do we owe people who don't exist yet, and how much should their interests count against ours? Philosophers disagree on whether future people can have rights against us, on how to respond to the non-identity problem (our choices decide who will exist), and, with economists, on whether to discount future harms ([SEP, Climate Justice: Intertemporal Discounting](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-climate/#InteDisc)).
- `8` [SEP, The Repugnant Conclusion](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/repugnant-conclusion/): how should we compare futures with different numbers of people, and should environmental policy aim to limit population growth? It has proved hard to find a theory of population ethics that avoids Derek Parfit's Repugnant Conclusion without other counterintuitive results, and some argue for limiting family size to protect the climate, while others reply that reducing consumption and changing energy systems are better responses, and that programmes to limit population can reinforce control over women's bodies ([SEP, Climate Justice: Population](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-climate/#Popu)).
- `7` [SEP, Climate Justice: Who are the Duty Bearers?](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-climate/#WhoDutyBear): do individuals have a duty to cut their own emissions, or are the duties mainly collective, for states and companies? Some argue that one person's emissions make no difference; others reply that they still carry an expected harm, or that people have a duty not to take part in collective processes that produce unjust outcomes, or that individuals' main duty is political: to vote and press for climate policy.
- `7` [SEP, Environmental Ethics: Introduction](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-environmental/#IntChaEnvEth): does nature have value in itself, or only for what it does for people? Human-centred views give intrinsic value to human beings alone, or far more to them than to anything else; other views give it to all living things, or to species and ecosystems as wholes.

## Tutorials and further reading

- `8` [AR6 Working Group III: Summary for Policymakers](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/downloads/report/IPCC_AR6_WGIII_SPM.pdf) (IPCC, 2022): the options for cutting emissions in every sector, including what changes in demand, behaviour and lifestyles can achieve.
- `8` [Climate Justice](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-climate/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): who should bear the burdens of climate change, what we owe future generations, and how to decide under risk and uncertainty.
- `7` [Towards a Global Ethic](https://www.weltethos.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Global-Ethic-Declaration_EN.pdf) (Parliament of the World's Religions): its fifth commitment, to a culture of sustainability and care for the Earth, sets out what religious and spiritual traditions share on caring for the environment.
