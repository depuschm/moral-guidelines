## Morality and ethics: what's the difference?

In everyday speech the two words are used almost interchangeably: "my morals" and "my ethics" usually mean the same thing ([Ethics Unwrapped, Morals](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/morals)). Where people do distinguish them, the distinction usually runs like this.

**Morality** is the set of standards of right and wrong that people live by: the principles that guide how a person acts and how people live together, such as honesty, fairness and not harming others. In the descriptive sense, it is the code of conduct that a society, a group such as a religion, or an individual actually accepts ([Ethics Unwrapped, Morals](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/morals), [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, The Definition of Morality](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/morality-definition/)).

**Ethics** is the reasoned study of those standards, and the well-founded standards that result. It asks which standards are justified and why, and it includes the written codes of conduct of professions and organisations ([Markkula Center, What is Ethics?](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/what-is-ethics/), [Ethics Unwrapped, Ethics](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/ethics)).

Ethics as a field has three parts: **normative ethics** asks what we ought to do and which theories explain it ([Moral theories](moral-theories.md)); **applied ethics** brings that thinking to real-world problems, such as medicine, business or AI ([Ethics Unwrapped, Applied Ethics](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/applied-ethics)), which is what the [domain guidelines](../specific/README.md) do; and **metaethics** asks what moral claims are at all, for example whether there are moral facts and whether standards are relative to cultures ([Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Metaethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/metaethics/)).

Being ethical is also not the same as following your feelings, the law or whatever your society accepts, because each of these can depart from what is right ([Markkula Center, What is Ethics?](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/what-is-ethics/)). Nor is it the same as religion, although many people's morality comes from their religion, and the major religions agree on much of it ([Religious and philosophical traditions](../traditions/README.md)).

This repository covers both: the morality that everyone shares, in the topics below, and the ethics of reasoning about it and of specific fields.

## Start here

If you only read three external sources, read these:

- `10` [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights) (United Nations, 1948): the most widely accepted statement of what every person is owed, adopted by the UN General Assembly.
- `8` [A Framework for Ethical Decision Making](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/a-framework-for-ethical-decision-making/) (Markkula Center for Applied Ethics): a practical method for working through a moral decision, looking at it through several ethical lenses.
- `8` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, The Definition of Morality](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/morality-definition/): what "morality" means, in the sense of what a society accepts and in the sense of what would be justified.
