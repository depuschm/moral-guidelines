# Harm and care

How to treat other people in everyday life: not harming them in body, mind or property, using force only to defend and no more than needed, helping people in need when you can, and kindness, compassion and generosity. Honesty and promises are in [Honesty and trust](honesty.md), putting right harm you have caused is in [Responsibility and accountability](responsibility.md), care within families and friendships is in [Relationships, family and community](relationships.md), and cruelty to animals is in [Animals](animals.md). The gravest harms, such as torture and slavery, are absolute prohibitions in [Human rights](human-rights.md), stepping in as a bystander and the open question of harm caused as a side effect (double effect) are in [Responsibility and accountability](responsibility.md), and care ethics as a theory is in [Moral theories](moral-theories.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `10` [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights) (United Nations, 1948): everyone's right to life, liberty and security of person (Article 3), and to protection from torture and cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment (Article 5). **The baseline for what no one may do to another person.**
- `8` [The Principle of Beneficence in Applied Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/principle-beneficence/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): what we owe others in helping them, the line between duties and ideals of helping, the duty to rescue strangers at little risk to ourselves, and how demanding helping can be.
- `8` [Self-Defense](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/self-defense/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): when force in defence of yourself or others is allowed, and the limits that proportionality and necessity set on it.
- `8` [Towards a Global Ethic: An Initial Declaration](https://www.weltethos.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Global-Ethic-Declaration_EN.pdf) (Parliament of the World's Religions, 1993): the principles that leaders of many religious and ethical traditions agreed they share, among them the Golden Rule, respect for life, honest dealing and compassion for those who suffer. Use it to see where the traditions meet.
- `7` [Care Ethics](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/care-ethics/) (Markkula Center for Applied Ethics): what caring for others well involves, attending to their needs, feelings and dependence on others, with roots in feminist, Confucian, African and Buddhist thought.
- `7` [Care Ethics](https://iep.utm.edu/care-ethics/) (Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy): the ethics of care in more depth, including Joan Tronto's four elements of good care: attentiveness, responsibility, competence and responsiveness.

## Key rules

### Not harming others

- `10` `HARM-1` **Don't kill, injure or physically attack anyone.** Everyone has the right to life and security of person, and the ban on killing is shared by the major religious and ethical traditions, from "You shall not murder" to the Buddhist precept against taking life. The narrow exception, defence against an attack, is `HARM-5`. ([UN, UDHR Article 3](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights), [Parliament of the World's Religions, Towards a Global Ethic](https://www.weltethos.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Global-Ethic-Declaration_EN.pdf), [Sefaria, Exodus 20:13](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.20.13), [Access to Insight, Abhisanda Sutta (AN 8.39)](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an08/an08.039.than.html), [Quran.com, Al-Ma'idah 5:32](https://quran.com/al-maidah/32))
- `9` `HARM-2` **Don't harm people's minds either: don't threaten, intimidate, humiliate, bully or torment them.** The World Health Organization counts threats and psychological harm as violence, and the Global Ethic holds that no one has the right "physically or psychically" to torture or injure another person. ([WHO Eastern Mediterranean, Violence](https://www.emro.who.int/violence-injuries-disabilities/violence/), [Parliament of the World's Religions, Towards a Global Ethic](https://www.weltethos.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Global-Ethic-Declaration_EN.pdf), [UN, UDHR Article 5](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights))
- `9` `HARM-3` **Don't steal, damage or cheat people out of what is theirs.** Theft, fraud and scams take away the security people have worked for, and the prohibition is as widely shared as the one on killing; deceiving someone to get their money also breaks `HON-2`. ([UN, UDHR Article 17](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights), [Parliament of the World's Religions, Towards a Global Ethic](https://www.weltethos.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Global-Ethic-Declaration_EN.pdf), [Sefaria, Exodus 20:13](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.20.13), [Access to Insight, Abhisanda Sutta (AN 8.39)](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/an/an08/an08.039.than.html), [Quran.com, An-Nisa 4:29](https://quran.com/an-nisa/29))
- `8` `HARM-4` **Don't expose others to needless risk: think ahead about what could go wrong for them, and take reasonable precautions.** Each person has a prima facie right not to be put at risk by others' actions, so a risk you impose on others needs a good reason and a fair share of the benefits for them; an ancient example is the Torah's rule to build a parapet round a flat roof. ([SEP, Risk](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/risk/), [Sefaria, Deuteronomy 22:8](https://www.sefaria.org/Deuteronomy.22.8))
- `8` `HARM-5` **If you must use force to protect yourself or someone else from an attack, use only as much as is needed to stop it.** Defensive force must be necessary, meaning no less harmful way would work, and proportionate, meaning its harm is not excessive compared with the harm it prevents. The law on self-defence differs from country to country. ([SEP, Self-Defense](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/self-defense/))

### Helping people in need

- `9` `HARM-6` **Help someone in serious danger when you can do it at little risk or cost to yourself: call the emergency services, give first aid if you know how, or get others to help.** Ordinary morality treats this "easy rescue" as a duty, not a favour, and some countries punish failing to help a person in danger. How to step in safely as a bystander is `ACCT-11`. ([SEP, The Principle of Beneficence in Applied Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/principle-beneficence/), [Quran.com, Al-Ma'idah 5:32](https://quran.com/al-maidah/32))
- `7` `HARM-7` **Give generously to people in need, in money, time or skills, as far as your means allow.** Helping beyond what is strictly owed is an ideal that traditions widely share: the Qur'an counts giving to relatives, orphans and the poor as true righteousness, and the Global Ethic asks for special care for children, older people, the poor, disabled people, refugees and the lonely. ([SEP, The Principle of Beneficence in Applied Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/principle-beneficence/), [Parliament of the World's Religions, Towards a Global Ethic](https://www.weltethos.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Global-Ethic-Declaration_EN.pdf), [Quran.com, Al-Baqarah 2:177](https://quran.com/al-baqarah/177))
- `6` `HARM-8` **When you give, check what the help actually achieves, and prefer help that works and lets people stand on their own feet.** Charities differ greatly in how much good each gift does, and good intentions can still do harm if a programme isn't evaluated; Maimonides ranked highest the gift, loan or job that makes a person self-sufficient. ([Giving What We Can, Effective Giving 101](https://www.givingwhatwecan.org/effective-giving-101), [Sefaria, Mishneh Torah, Gifts to the Poor 10:7](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Gifts_to_the_Poor.10.7))

### Kindness and care

- `8` `HARM-9` **Treat others as you would want to be treated in their place.** The Golden Rule, in its positive form or its negative one ("that which is hateful to you, do not do to another"), is found in many religious and ethical traditions and asks you to give others' interests the same weight as your own. ([IEP, The Golden Rule](https://iep.utm.edu/goldrule/), [Parliament of the World's Religions, Towards a Global Ethic](https://www.weltethos.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Global-Ethic-Declaration_EN.pdf), [Sefaria, Shabbat 31a:6](https://www.sefaria.org/Shabbat.31a.6), [USCCB, Matthew 7:12](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/matthew/7))
- `7` `HARM-10` **Be kind: notice when someone is struggling, and respond with patience and compassion, especially to people who are vulnerable or alone.** Care ethics stresses the sympathy and compassion that make us attentive to others' needs, and Buddhist teaching asks for loving-kindness towards all beings. ([Markkula Center, Care Ethics](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/care-ethics/), [Parliament of the World's Religions, Towards a Global Ethic](https://www.weltethos.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Global-Ethic-Declaration_EN.pdf), [Access to Insight, Karaniya Metta Sutta (Snp 1.8)](https://accesstoinsight.org/tipitaka/kn/snp/snp.1.08.amar.html))
- `7` `HARM-11` **When you help or care for someone, find out what they need and want instead of deciding for them, and check how your help is received.** Good care starts with attentiveness to the other person's needs and ends with responsiveness to how they experience it, from their point of view; respecting their choices is `RESP-2`. ([IEP, Care Ethics](https://iep.utm.edu/care-ethics/), [Markkula Center, Care Ethics](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/care-ethics/))

## How to apply

- `8` [A Framework for Ethical Decision Making](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/a-framework-for-ethical-decision-making/) (Markkula Center for Applied Ethics): its care ethics lens asks how each option affects the relationships, needs and feelings of everyone involved. Supports `HARM-10` and `HARM-11`.
- `5` [First aid advice](https://www.sja.org.uk/first-aid-advice/) (St John Ambulance): free step-by-step guides to CPR, choking, bleeding and other emergencies, and courses to learn them. Supports `HARM-6`.
- `5` [Effective Giving 101](https://www.givingwhatwecan.org/effective-giving-101) (Giving What We Can): how to choose a cause and a charity using research on what each gift achieves. Supports `HARM-7` and `HARM-8`.

## Open questions

- `8` [SEP, Moral Demands and Permissions/Prerogatives](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-demands-permissions/): how much must we give up to help strangers far away? Peter Singer argues that, just as we must save a child drowning in a pond at the cost of our clothes, we must prevent suffering from poverty whenever we can do so without sacrificing anything of comparable importance, which would mean giving far more than most people do; critics reply that a reasonable morality can't demand that much, and must leave room for our own lives and projects.
- `8` [SEP, Doing vs. Allowing Harm](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/doing-allowing/): is doing harm worse than allowing harm that you could prevent? Most non-consequentialists say it is, while consequentialists hold that only the outcome counts; the answer matters for questions such as aid to the global poor, euthanasia and war.

## Tutorials and further reading

- `8` [Altruism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/altruism/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): what acting for someone else's sake means, whether people are capable of it, and how it relates to morality.
- `6` [Altruism](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/altruism) (Ethics Unwrapped): a short video and explainer on acting in the best interest of others rather than your own.
- `6` [The Life You Can Save](https://www.thelifeyoucansave.org/book/) (Peter Singer, 10th anniversary edition): the case for giving much more, and more effectively, to people in extreme poverty, free as an ebook or audiobook. Read it with the critics in "Open questions".
