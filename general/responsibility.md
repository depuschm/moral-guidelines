# Responsibility and accountability

Scope: owning what you do and its foreseeable consequences; admitting mistakes, apologising and making amends; not hiding behind orders, a role or the group; holding others to account fairly; and speaking up, blowing the whistle and stepping in when others are being harmed. The biases that make wrongdoing easier, such as obedience to authority and conformity, are in [Ethical reasoning and decisions](reasoning.md) (`REASON-9`). Truthfulness and keeping promises are in [Honesty and trust](honesty.md), helping people in need in [Harm and care](harm-and-care.md), forgiveness in [Relationships, family and community](relationships.md), and speaking up at work in [Workplace and leadership](../specific/workplace/README.md). Where a rule mentions a law, it says where the law applies. This is not legal advice: before you report wrongdoing outside your organisation, get confidential advice on the protection you have.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [Principles of International Law Recognized in the Charter of the Nürnberg Tribunal and in the Judgment of the Tribunal](https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/draft_articles/7_1_1950.pdf) (UN International Law Commission, 1950): **the baseline on personal responsibility for grave wrongs**: anyone who commits a crime under international law is responsible for it, even if national law doesn't punish it, whatever office they hold, and even if they acted on orders, provided a moral choice was in fact possible; complicity is itself a crime.
- `8` [Moral Responsibility](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-responsibility/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): what it means to be responsible for actions and their consequences, the conditions of control and knowledge it requires, and what holding someone responsible involves.
- `8` [Directive (EU) 2019/1937 on the protection of persons who report breaches of Union law](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/1937/oj) (EU Whistleblower Directive): the law in the EU member states on reporting breaches of EU rules, in areas such as public procurement, financial services, food and transport safety, consumer protection, the environment and public health: internal and external reporting channels, follow-up, confidentiality and protection against retaliation.
- `7` [Resource Guide on Good Practices in the Protection of Reporting Persons](https://www.unodc.org/documents/corruption/Publications/2015/15-04741_Person_Guide_eBook.pdf) (UN Office on Drugs and Crime, 2015): how to encourage and protect people who report wrongdoing, with examples of law and practice from many countries, under the UN Convention against Corruption.
- `7` [Handbook on Restorative Justice Programmes](https://www.unodc.org/documents/justice-and-prison-reform/20-01146_Handbook_on_Restorative_Justice_Programmes.pdf) (UN Office on Drugs and Crime, 2nd edition, 2020): how restorative processes help people who caused harm to acknowledge it, take responsibility and repair it, a practice with roots in most societies.
- `6` [How to make a good apology](https://www.spso.org.uk/sites/spso/files/csa/ApologyGuide.pdf) (Scottish Public Services Ombudsman): practical guidance on apologising: the four Rs of regret, responsibility, reason and remedy, what a good apology includes, and the mistakes that make one fail.

## Key rules

### Owning your actions and mistakes

- `8` `ACCT-1` **Take responsibility for what you do and for its reasonably foreseeable consequences, not only the ones you intended.** Responsibility for consequences is usually judged by what you could reasonably have foreseen, not only by what you actually foresaw, so failing to think ahead doesn't excuse you. ([SEP, The Epistemic Condition for Moral Responsibility](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-responsibility-epistemic/), [SEP, Moral Responsibility](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-responsibility/))
- `8` `ACCT-2` **When you get something wrong, admit it promptly and plainly to the people affected, without minimising it, hiding it or shifting the blame.** The earlier a mistake is acknowledged, the better the chance of repairing the damage and the trust. ([SPSO, How to make a good apology](https://www.spso.org.uk/sites/spso/files/csa/ApologyGuide.pdf), [Victorian Ombudsman, Apologies](https://www.ombudsman.vic.gov.au/learn-from-us/practice-guides/apologies-guide/))
- `8` `ACCT-3` **Apologise sincerely and specifically: say what went wrong, accept responsibility for it without excuses, acknowledge its effect on the other person, and say what you will do to put it right and stop it happening again.** A standard or hedged apology, such as "I apologise for any faults", can leave people angrier than before. ([SPSO, How to make a good apology](https://www.spso.org.uk/sites/spso/files/csa/ApologyGuide.pdf), [Victorian Ombudsman, Apologies](https://www.ombudsman.vic.gov.au/learn-from-us/practice-guides/apologies-guide/))
- `8` `ACCT-4` **Make amends: repair the harm you caused as far as you can, and change whatever led to it.** Restorative justice asks those who caused harm to repair it and avoid repeating it, W. D. Ross counted the duty to correct a wrong you have done among the basic moral duties, and Maimonides teaches that a wrong done to another person is not forgiven until the wrongdoer has repaid and appeased them. ([UNODC, Handbook on Restorative Justice Programmes](https://www.unodc.org/documents/justice-and-prison-reform/20-01146_Handbook_on_Restorative_Justice_Programmes.pdf), [SEP, William David Ross](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/william-david-ross/), [Maimonides, Mishneh Torah, Repentance 2:9](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah%2C_Repentance.2.9))
- `6` `ACCT-5` **Hold others to account as fairly as you would want to be held: before you blame, consider what they knew, what choice they had and the situation they were in, and don't condemn in others what you excuse in yourself.** People tend to put others' failings down to character and their own to circumstances, and blame from someone guilty of the same fault is widely seen as lacking standing. ([Ethics Unwrapped, Fundamental Attribution Error](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/fundamental-attribution-error), [SEP, Blame: Facts about the Blamer](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/blame/#FacAboBla), [SEP, Moral Responsibility](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-responsibility/))

### Orders, roles and groups

- `9` `ACCT-6` **Don't take part in serious wrongdoing because you were told to: an order from a superior or a government doesn't remove your responsibility when a moral choice was possible.** International law has said so since the Nuremberg trials, and Milgram's experiments showed how strong the pull to obey can be, so decide in advance where you would refuse. ([ILC, Nuremberg Principles, Principle IV](https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/draft_articles/7_1_1950.pdf), [Ethics Unwrapped, Obedience to Authority](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/video/obedience-to-authority))
- `8` `ACCT-7` **Don't let a role, a group or a division of labour dilute your sense of responsibility: if you knowingly help bring about a wrong, you share responsibility for it.** People acting in a role or as one of many often accept what they would never do alone, and complicity in the gravest crimes is itself a crime under international law. ([ILC, Nuremberg Principles, Principle VII](https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/draft_articles/7_1_1950.pdf), [Ethics Unwrapped, Role Morality](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/role-morality), [Ethics Unwrapped, Diffusion of Responsibility](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/diffusion-of-responsibility))
- `7` `ACCT-8` **If you have authority over others, take responsibility for what you ask of them and for the pressure your position creates, even when you never give an explicit order.** High office gives no exemption, and people often act to please their superiors without being asked. ([ILC, Nuremberg Principles, Principle III](https://legal.un.org/ilc/texts/instruments/english/draft_articles/7_1_1950.pdf), [Ethics Unwrapped, Obedience to Authority](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/obedience-to-authority))

### Speaking up and stepping in

- `8` `ACCT-9` **Speak up about wrongdoing that harms others or the public: report what you have reasonable grounds to believe, first where it is most likely to be put right, and go further if nothing is done or if reporting inside your organisation isn't safe.** In the EU, the Whistleblower Directive protects people who report breaches of EU law internally, to a competent authority, or publicly when those channels fail or the danger is imminent; elsewhere, protection depends on national law. ([EU Directive 2019/1937, Articles 6, 7 and 15](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/1937/oj), [UNODC, Resource Guide on Good Practices in the Protection of Reporting Persons](https://www.unodc.org/documents/corruption/Publications/2015/15-04741_Person_Guide_eBook.pdf), [Ethics Unwrapped, Moral Muteness](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/moral-muteness))
- `8` `ACCT-10` **Protect people who raise concerns: never retaliate against them, keep their identity confidential, and look properly into what they report.** The fear of retaliation and the belief that nothing will be done are among the main reasons people stay silent. ([EU Directive 2019/1937, Articles 16 and 19](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/1937/oj), [UN Convention against Corruption, Article 33](https://www.unodc.org/documents/treaties/UNCAC/Publications/Convention/08-50026_E.pdf), [UNODC, Resource Guide on Good Practices in the Protection of Reporting Persons](https://www.unodc.org/documents/corruption/Publications/2015/15-04741_Person_Guide_eBook.pdf))
- `8` `ACCT-11` **When you see someone being harmed or in danger, don't assume someone else will act: do something, in a way that is safe for you and for them.** You can step in directly, create a distraction, get help from someone better placed or from the emergency services, or support the person afterwards; the more bystanders there are, the less likely each one is to help. ([Ethics Unwrapped, Diffusion of Responsibility](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/glossary/diffusion-of-responsibility), [Step UP!, Additional Strategies for Effective Helping](https://stepupprogram.org/how/additional-strategies-for-effective-helping/))
- `7` `ACCT-12` **Prepare to act on your values before the pressure comes: know what you stand for, expect conflicts of values, and rehearse what you will say and how you will answer the usual objections.** People are more likely to speak up when they have scripted and practised what to say. ([Ethics Unwrapped, GVV Pillar 6: Voice](https://ethicsunwrapped.utexas.edu/video/pillar-6-voice), [Giving Voice to Values](https://givingvoicetovaluesthebook.com/))

## How to apply

- `6` [Apologies](https://www.ombudsman.vic.gov.au/learn-from-us/practice-guides/apologies-guide/) (Victorian Ombudsman, Australia): a guide with templates to the six Rs of an effective apology: recognition, responsibility, regret, reasons, redress and release. Supports `ACCT-2`, `ACCT-3` and `ACCT-4`.
- `5` [Protect](https://protect-advice.org.uk/) (UK whistleblowing charity): a free, confidential advice line on whether a concern counts as whistleblowing, where to raise it and what legal protection you have. Supports `ACCT-9` and `ACCT-10`.
- `5` [Additional Strategies for Effective Helping](https://stepupprogram.org/how/additional-strategies-for-effective-helping/) (Step UP!): bystander strategies for emergencies and slower situations, direct, distract, delegate and delay, guided by the SEEK model: safe, early, effective and kind. Supports `ACCT-11`.

## Open questions

Questions on which serious traditions and thinkers disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `8` [Doctrine of Double Effect](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/double-effect/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): whether causing serious harm as a foreseen but unintended side effect of pursuing a good end can be permissible when causing the same harm as a means would not be. Supporters use the distinction in war and end-of-life care; critics, including consequentialists, deny that it matters morally.
- `8` [Collective Responsibility](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/collective-responsibility/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): whether groups such as companies and nations can be morally responsible as groups, over and above their members, and how far members share the blame for what their group does.
- `8` [Moral Luck](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-luck/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): whether factors beyond your control can change how blameworthy you are, for example whether a driver who kills a child deserves a worse moral assessment than an equally careless one who doesn't.
- `7` [SEP, Moral Responsibility: Freedom, Responsibility, and Determinism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-responsibility/#FreeRespDete): whether people can be morally responsible if every action is the product of prior causes. Compatibilists hold that responsibility and determinism are compatible; incompatibilists hold that responsibility needs a freedom that determinism would rule out, and some sceptics conclude that no one deserves blame in the full sense.
