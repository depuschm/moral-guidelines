# Relationships, family and community

How to treat the people closest to you and the communities you live in: duties to children and to older parents, not abusing the power that close relationships give, friendship, gratitude, loyalty and its limits, forgiveness, and being a good neighbour and citizen. Helping strangers in need and kindness in general are in [Harm and care](harm-and-care.md), keeping promises in [Honesty and trust](honesty.md), apologising and making amends in [Responsibility and accountability](responsibility.md), and consent and privacy, including between partners, in [Respect, autonomy and consent](respect-and-consent.md). Family law differs between countries; this is moral guidance, not legal advice.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [Convention on the Rights of the Child](https://www.unicef.org/child-rights-convention/convention-text) (UN, 1989, text published by UNICEF): parents' primary responsibility for their children's upbringing and development, with the child's best interests as their basic concern (Articles 3, 5, 18 and 27), and children's protection from violence, abuse and neglect, including by their parents (Article 19).
- `8` [Violence against women](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/violence-against-women) (World Health Organization fact sheet): what intimate partner violence is, including psychological abuse and controlling behaviour, how common it is, its causes and how to prevent it.
- `8` [Friendship](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/friendship/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): what friendship is, why it matters, and how the special duties it creates fit with impartial moral theories.
- `8` [Loyalty](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/loyalty/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): what loyalty is, whether it is a virtue, and where its limits lie, including whistleblowing.
- `8` [Forgiveness](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/forgiveness/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): what forgiving is, how it differs from excusing, forgetting and reconciling, and when it is right to forgive.
- `8` [Gratitude](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/gratitude/) (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy): when gratitude is owed, what a grateful response involves, and whether gratitude can be a duty.

## Key rules

### Family

- `9` `REL-1` **Care for your children: provide for their needs, protect them, and guide them as their abilities grow, with their best interests as your basic concern.** The Convention on the Rights of the Child gives parents the primary responsibility for their children's upbringing and living conditions, within their means, and asks them to give direction suited to the child's evolving capacities. ([Convention on the Rights of the Child, Articles 5, 18 and 27](https://www.unicef.org/child-rights-convention/convention-text))
- `9` `REL-2` **Never use violence, threats, intimidation or coercive control against a partner, a child, an older relative or anyone else in your household, and never neglect someone who depends on you.** Abuse in close relationships, whether physical, sexual, psychological or financial, violates human rights, and it exploits the trust and power that the relationship gives. ([WHO, Violence against women](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/violence-against-women), [WHO, Child maltreatment](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/child-maltreatment), [WHO, Abuse of older people](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/abuse-of-older-people), [Convention on the Rights of the Child, Article 19](https://www.unicef.org/child-rights-convention/convention-text))
- `7` `REL-3` **Respect your parents and other older relatives, treat them with dignity, and support them when they need help or care.** The African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights names a duty to respect your parents and to maintain them in case of need, and the Torah, the Qur'an and the Buddha's advice to Sigālaka all teach honouring and caring for parents. Neglecting an older person or treating them without dignity is a form of elder abuse, and their own choices deserve respect too ([Respect, autonomy and consent](respect-and-consent.md)); how far the duty of care goes is an open question (see below). ([African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights, Article 29](https://achpr.au.int/en/charter/african-charter-human-and-peoples-rights), [WHO, Abuse of older people](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/abuse-of-older-people), [Sefaria, Exodus 20:12](https://www.sefaria.org/Exodus.20.12), [Quran.com, Qur'an 17:23–24](https://quran.com/al-isra/23-24), [SuttaCentral, Advice to Sigālaka (DN 31)](https://suttacentral.net/dn31/en/sujato))
- `6` `REL-4` **Share the work of bringing up children and caring for relatives fairly, so that it doesn't fall on one person, and in particular not on women by default.** Both parents have common responsibilities for their children, and caring for older parents still falls disproportionately on women. ([Convention on the Rights of the Child, Article 18](https://www.unicef.org/child-rights-convention/convention-text), [Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women, Article 5](https://www.un.org/womenwatch/daw/cedaw/text/econvention.htm#article5), [IEP, Filial Obligation](https://iep.utm.edu/fil-obli/))

### Friends and close relationships

- `7` `REL-5` **Be loyal to your family and friends, but never let loyalty lead you to do wrong, cover up wrongdoing or harm others for them.** Loyalty isn't servility: a loyal friend can criticise and oppose, and the demand for loyalty is often used to justify unethical conduct. When speaking up is needed, see [Responsibility and accountability](responsibility.md). ([SEP, Loyalty: Loyalty as a virtue](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/loyalty/#LoyaVirt), [SEP, Loyalty: Limiting loyalty](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/loyalty/#LimiLoya))
- `6` `REL-6` **Be a good friend: care about your friends for their own sake, share in their joys and troubles, and help them when they need it.** Nearly every account of friendship makes mutual caring, felt and acted on for the friend's sake, a condition of it. ([SEP, Friendship: Mutual Caring](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/friendship/#MutuCari))
- `6` `REL-7` **Thank the people who help you, and look for ways to return or pass on their kindness.** Gratitude includes acknowledging the benefit to the person who gave it, by thanking them or through a return favour, and it strengthens relationships. ([SEP, Gratitude: Communicative Elements](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/gratitude/#ComEle), [Greater Good, What is Gratitude?](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/topic/gratitude/definition))
- `6` `REL-8` **Be willing to forgive, but don't confuse forgiving with excusing the wrong, or with going back to someone who may harm you again.** Forgiving means letting go of resentment toward the person who wronged you; it doesn't mean condoning what they did, and it doesn't oblige you to reconcile with them or release them from legal accountability. ([SEP, Forgiveness: Reconciliation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/forgiveness/#Rec), [Greater Good, What is Forgiveness?](https://greatergood.berkeley.edu/topic/forgiveness/definition))

### Community

- `6` `REL-9` **Be a considerate neighbour: treat the people who live around you with respect, and look out for those who are old, ill or alone.** The African Charter names a duty to respect and consider your fellow beings and to promote mutual respect and tolerance, and the declaration of a global ethic agreed by leaders of many religions asks people never to forget the aged, the suffering and the lonely. ([African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights, Article 28](https://achpr.au.int/en/charter/african-charter-human-and-peoples-rights), [Parliament of the World's Religions, Towards a Global Ethic](https://www.weltethos.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Global-Ethic-Declaration_EN.pdf))
- `6` `REL-10` **Contribute to the common good of the communities you belong to, for example by volunteering your time and skills.** Everyone has duties to the community, and shared goods, such as a clean park or clean air, last only if the people who benefit from them contribute. ([UDHR, Article 29](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights), [Markkula Center, The Common Good](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/the-common-good/), [UN Volunteers, State of the World's Volunteerism Report 2022](https://swvr2022.unv.org/))
- `5` `REL-11` **Take part in the public life of your community: stay informed, vote where you can, and take part in decisions that affect the people around you.** Everyone has the right to take part in the government of their country, and the common good depends on citizens' cooperation. ([UDHR, Article 21](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights), [Markkula Center, The Common Good](https://www.scu.edu/ethics/ethics-resources/ethical-decision-making/the-common-good/))

## How to apply

- `7` [Parenting for Lifelong Health](https://www.who.int/teams/social-determinants-of-health/parenting-for-lifelong-health) (WHO, UNICEF and universities): free, open-access parenting programmes, tested in trials, that help parents build relationships with their children through play and praise and use positive discipline instead of violence. Supports `REL-1` and `REL-2`.
- `7` [iSupport for dementia](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241515863) (WHO): a free skills and training programme, online or as a printable manual, for family members who care for a person with dementia, including how to look after themselves. Supports `REL-3`.
- `6` [Gratitude Letter](https://ggia.berkeley.edu/practice/gratitude_letter) and [Nine Steps to Forgiveness](https://ggia.berkeley.edu/practice/nine_steps_to_forgiveness) (Greater Good in Action, University of California, Berkeley): short, research-based exercises for thanking someone who helped you and for working toward forgiving someone who hurt you. Supports `REL-7` and `REL-8`.

## Open questions

- `7` [SEP, Impartiality: Partiality and Impartiality](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/impartiality/#PartImpa): how far may you favour your family and friends over strangers? Many philosophers hold that morality has both partial and impartial parts, so that some favouritism toward those close to you is allowed or even required, while strict impartialists hold that it must be justified from an impartial point of view; there is no agreed account of where the limits lie. See also [SEP, Friendship: Friendship and Moral Theory](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/friendship/#FrieMoraTheo).
- `7` [IEP, Filial Obligation](https://iep.utm.edu/fil-obli/): what do adult children owe their parents, and why? Theories ground the duty in a debt for what parents gave, in gratitude, in friendship or in the special goods that only this relationship provides, and they reach very different answers, from substantial duties of care to the view that grown children owe their parents only what a loving relationship asks. See also [SEP, Special Obligations: Filial Duty](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/special-obligations/#FiliDuty).
- `7` [SEP, Forgiveness: Moral Disagreements about Forgiveness](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/forgiveness/#MorDisAboFor): should you forgive everyone who wrongs you? Some hold that forgiveness is always called for, and some Christians believe it is commanded; a few philosophers think it is always flawed; many take a middle view, that forgiving is good in some cases and wrong in others, with the wrongdoer's repentance and apology counted as reasons in favour.
