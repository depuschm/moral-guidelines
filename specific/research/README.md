# Research with people and animals

Scope: anyone who plans, carries out, funds, reviews or publishes research that involves people, their data or biological samples, or animals, in medicine, the social and behavioural sciences and other fields. The care of patients is covered in [Healthcare](../healthcare/README.md), handling data about people in [Data and analytics](../data/README.md), finding and disclosing security vulnerabilities in [Security research](../security/README.md), and the wider question of how people may use animals in [Animals](../../general/animals.md). The general guidelines on [respect, autonomy and consent](../../general/respect-and-consent.md), [harm and care](../../general/harm-and-care.md) and [honesty and trust](../../general/honesty.md) matter most here. National laws and regulators add their own requirements for research, so ask your research ethics committee which apply to you: this is moral guidance, not legal advice.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `10` [WMA Declaration of Helsinki](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/) (World Medical Association, revised October 2024): **the baseline for medical research with people**, including research on identifiable human material and data: risks and benefits, vulnerable groups, research ethics committees, privacy, informed consent, placebo use, post-trial provisions, and the registration and publication of results.
- `9` [The Belmont Report](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1979-04-18/pdf/FR-1979-04-18.pdf#page=134) (US National Commission for the Protection of Human Subjects of Biomedical and Behavioral Research, published in the Federal Register, 1979): the three principles of respect for persons, beneficence and justice, and how they apply to informed consent, the assessment of risks and benefits, and the selection of participants. Read it to understand why the other rules exist.
- `9` [International Ethical Guidelines for Health-related Research Involving Humans](https://cioms.ch/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/WEB-CIOMS-EthicalGuidelines.pdf) (CIOMS, with WHO, 2016): 25 guidelines with commentary on how to apply the Declaration of Helsinki, especially in low-resource settings, with vulnerable people, and when collecting and reusing data and biological samples.
- `9` [European Code of Conduct for Research Integrity](https://allea.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/European-Code-of-Conduct-Revised-Edition-2023.pdf) (ALLEA, revised edition 2023): **the baseline for research integrity in every discipline**: the principles of reliability, honesty, respect and accountability, good research practices, and the definitions of fabrication, falsification, plagiarism and other unacceptable practices.
- `8` [The Nuremberg Code](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-nuremberg-code) (1947, United States Holocaust Memorial Museum): the ten points set out by the judges of the Doctors' Trial after the Nazi medical experiments, beginning with "The voluntary consent of the human subject is absolutely essential." The historical root of modern research ethics.
- `8` [Singapore Statement on Research Integrity](https://www.wcrif.org/statement) (2nd World Conference on Research Integrity, 2010): four principles and fourteen responsibilities for researchers and research institutions worldwide, short enough to read in a few minutes.
- `8` [Defining the Role of Authors and Contributors](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/defining-the-role-of-authors-and-contributors.html) (ICMJE Recommendations): the four criteria for authorship that medical journals and many others use, and how to acknowledge contributors who don't meet them.
- `8` [The 3Rs](https://nc3rs.org.uk/who-we-are/3rs) (NC3Rs): the definitions of replacement, reduction and refinement in research with animals, with examples of each.

## Key rules

### Protecting participants

- `10` `RES-1` **Get each participant's free and informed consent before they take part, and respect their right to refuse or to withdraw at any time without penalty.** Explain the aims, methods, risks, benefits, funding and any conflicts of interest in plain language, check that the person has understood, and record their consent, having someone independent ask if the person depends on you, for example as your patient or student. The International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights forbids medical or scientific experimentation on anyone without their free consent. See `RESP-6`. ([UN, ICCPR Article 7](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/UNTS/Volume%20999/volume-999-I-14668-English.pdf#page=5), [WMA, Declaration of Helsinki, paragraphs 25–27](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/), [The Nuremberg Code, point 1](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-nuremberg-code), [Belmont Report, C.1 Informed consent](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1979-04-18/pdf/FR-1979-04-18.pdf#page=134))
- `9` `RES-2` **Have the research approved by an independent research ethics committee before it starts, and keep the committee informed until it ends.** Get approval for any change to the protocol, report serious adverse events, and send a final report; in international studies, get approval in both the sponsoring and the host country. ([WMA, Declaration of Helsinki, paragraph 23](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/), [CIOMS, Guideline 23: Research ethics committees](https://cioms.ch/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/WEB-CIOMS-EthicalGuidelines.pdf#page=86))
- `9` `RES-3` **Do research only when its expected value outweighs the risks and burdens to participants, keep those risks as low as possible, and stop or change the study if the balance turns.** The design must be scientifically sound, because research that can't produce valid knowledge exposes people to risk for nothing. See `HARM-4`. ([WMA, Declaration of Helsinki, paragraphs 16–18 and 21](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/), [Belmont Report, C.2 Assessment of risks and benefits](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1979-04-18/pdf/FR-1979-04-18.pdf#page=134), [The Nuremberg Code, points 2–10](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/the-nuremberg-code))
- `9` `RES-4` **Choose participants for scientific reasons, not because they are easy to recruit, and give people in vulnerable situations specific protections instead of excluding them by default.** Include people who can't consent, such as young children or unconscious patients, only when the research needs them and offers them direct benefit or involves only minimal risk, with a legal representative's consent and, where possible, their own agreement (assent). See `FAIR-12`. ([CIOMS, Guideline 3: Equitable distribution of benefits and burdens](https://cioms.ch/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/WEB-CIOMS-EthicalGuidelines.pdf#page=18), [CIOMS, Guideline 15: Vulnerable persons and groups](https://cioms.ch/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/WEB-CIOMS-EthicalGuidelines.pdf#page=61), [WMA, Declaration of Helsinki, paragraphs 19–20 and 28–30](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/), [Belmont Report, C.3 Selection of subjects](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1979-04-18/pdf/FR-1979-04-18.pdf#page=134))
- `8` `RES-5` **Protect participants' privacy and the confidentiality of their data and samples, and reuse them only with consent or with a research ethics committee's approval.** Ask for specific or broad consent when you store data or samples for future research, and give other researchers anonymised or coded data, with the key kept by the data's custodian; data protection law also applies (see [Data and analytics](../data/README.md)). See `RESP-9`. ([WMA, Declaration of Helsinki, paragraphs 24 and 32](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/), [CIOMS, Guideline 12: Data in health-related research](https://cioms.ch/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/WEB-CIOMS-EthicalGuidelines.pdf#page=51))
- `7` `RES-6` **Make sure that participants harmed by the research get free treatment and fair compensation, and arrange before a clinical trial starts how participants who still need a treatment shown to be beneficial will get it afterwards.** Never ask participants to give up these rights. ([CIOMS, Guideline 14: Treatment and compensation for research-related harms](https://cioms.ch/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/WEB-CIOMS-EthicalGuidelines.pdf#page=59), [WMA, Declaration of Helsinki, paragraphs 15 and 34](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/))

### Integrity and publication

- `9` `RES-7` **Never fabricate, falsify or plagiarise, whether you are proposing, doing, reviewing or reporting research.** Fabrication is making up data or results, falsification is manipulating materials, images or processes or leaving out data without justification, and plagiarism is using other people's work or ideas without credit. See `HON-2`. ([ALLEA, European Code of Conduct, 3.1 Research misconduct](https://allea.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/European-Code-of-Conduct-Revised-Edition-2023.pdf), [Singapore Statement on Research Integrity](https://www.wcrif.org/statement), [WMA, Declaration of Helsinki, paragraph 12](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/))
- `8` `RES-8` **Register each clinical trial and other medical study with people in a public registry before the first participant enrols, and make all the results public, including negative and inconclusive ones.** Aim to post summary results in the registry within 12 months of the study's primary completion, the target set by WHO and major research funders, and give the registration number in every publication; unreported results leave a misleading picture of what works. See `HON-2`. ([WMA, Declaration of Helsinki, paragraphs 35–36](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/), [WHO, Joint statement on public disclosure of results from clinical trials](https://www.who.int/news/item/18-05-2017-joint-statement-on-registration), [ICMJE, Clinical trial registration](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/publishing-and-editorial-issues/clinical-trial-registration.html), [CIOMS, Guideline 24: Public accountability](https://cioms.ch/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/WEB-CIOMS-EthicalGuidelines.pdf#page=90))
- `8` `RES-9` **List as authors everyone who meets the authorship criteria, and only them, and acknowledge everyone else who contributed.** Under the ICMJE criteria an author contributed substantially to the work, drafted or critically revised it, approved the final version and accepts accountability for it; funding, supervision or writing help alone don't qualify, and AI tools can't be authors, so disclose how you used them instead. See `HON-9`. ([ICMJE, Defining the role of authors and contributors](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/defining-the-role-of-authors-and-contributors.html), [Singapore Statement, Authorship and Publication acknowledgement](https://www.wcrif.org/statement), [ALLEA, European Code of Conduct, 3.1 Other unacceptable practices](https://allea.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/European-Code-of-Conduct-Revised-Edition-2023.pdf))
- `8` `RES-10` **Disclose financial and other interests that could affect, or seem to affect, your research, in proposals, ethics applications, publications, peer reviews and public statements.** A conflict that only seems to exist can damage trust as much as a real one, so let readers judge for themselves; editors and reviewers with a significant conflict should step aside from the decision. See `FAIR-5`. ([Singapore Statement, Conflict of interest](https://www.wcrif.org/statement), [ICMJE, Disclosure of relationships and activities, and conflicts of interest](https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/author-responsibilities--conflicts-of-interest.html), [CIOMS, Guideline 25: Conflicts of interest](https://cioms.ch/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/WEB-CIOMS-EthicalGuidelines.pdf#page=93), [COPE, DOAJ, OASPA and WAME, Principles of Transparency and Best Practice](https://doaj.org/apply/transparency/))
- `7` `RES-11` **Report suspected research misconduct to the body responsible for investigating it, and handle allegations fairly: protect whistleblowers who act in good faith, and presume the accused innocent until proven otherwise.** Ignoring violations by others, or covering them up, is itself an unacceptable practice. See `ACCT-9`. ([Singapore Statement, Reporting irresponsible research practices](https://www.wcrif.org/statement), [ALLEA, European Code of Conduct, 3.1 and 3.2 Dealing with violations](https://allea.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/European-Code-of-Conduct-Revised-Edition-2023.pdf))

### Animals

- `8` `RES-12` **Use animals only when no method without animals can answer the research question, and apply the 3Rs: replace animals wherever possible, reduce their number to what a sound design needs, and refine housing, care and procedures to minimise pain, suffering and distress.** Good experimental design and statistics make sure the animals used give reliable results; in the EU, the law on animals in science, Directive 2010/63/EU, is built on the 3Rs. See `ANIMAL-1`. ([NC3Rs, The 3Rs](https://nc3rs.org.uk/who-we-are/3rs), [European Commission, Animals in science](https://environment.ec.europa.eu/topics/chemicals/animals-science_en), [WMA, Declaration of Helsinki, paragraph 21](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/))

## How to apply

Practices, methods and tools that help people follow the key rules.

- `7` [ARRIVE guidelines 2.0](https://arriveguidelines.org/) (NC3Rs): a checklist for reporting research with animals fully and transparently, so that others can assess and repeat it, with a study plan to use at the design stage. Supports `RES-12`.
- `7` [WHO International Clinical Trials Registry Platform](https://www.who.int/tools/clinical-trials-registry-platform): a search portal across the world's trial registries, and the list of WHO primary registries in which you can register a trial. Supports `RES-8`.
- `7` [ICMJE Disclosure of Interest form](https://www.icmje.org/disclosure-of-interest/): a standard form for declaring your relationships and activities, which you complete and send to the journal with your manuscript. Supports `RES-10`.
- `6` [CRediT, Contributor Role Taxonomy](https://credit.niso.org/) (NISO): 14 standard roles, such as conceptualisation, data curation and writing, for recording who did what on a paper and settling authorship early. Supports `RES-9`.

## Open questions

Questions on which serious traditions or experts disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `8` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, The Ethics of Stem Cell Research](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/stem-cells/): may human embryos be created or destroyed for research, and should embryos ever be genetically edited in ways that pass to future generations? Some hold that an embryo has the same moral status, including a right not to be killed, as a human being at any later stage; others hold that moral status depends on capacities, such as self-awareness, that early embryos lack. On heritable genome editing, some reject it outright, while the [Nuffield Council on Bioethics](https://www.nuffieldbioethics.org/publication/genome-editing-and-human-reproduction-social-and-ethical-issues/) concluded that it could be acceptable if it serves the welfare of the future person and doesn't increase disadvantage, discrimination or division in society.
- `7` [CIOMS, Guideline 5: Choice of control in clinical trials](https://cioms.ch/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/WEB-CIOMS-EthicalGuidelines.pdf#page=25): when may a new treatment be tested against a placebo (a dummy treatment) instead of the best treatment already proven to work? Some hold that withholding an established effective treatment from participants is never acceptable; others accept it when there are compelling scientific reasons and the extra risk is small. CIOMS takes a middle position (no more than a minor increase above minimal risk), and the [Declaration of Helsinki, paragraph 33](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-helsinki/) allows it only when participants face no additional risk of serious or irreversible harm.
- `7` [Selgelid, Gain-of-Function Research: Ethical Analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4996883/) (Science and Engineering Ethics, 2016): should "dual-use" research that could be misused to cause great harm, such as making dangerous pathogens more transmissible, be done or published in full? Supporters point to better understanding of disease, preparedness and vaccines; critics point to the risk of accidents or misuse, argue that safer methods could bring the same benefits, and say the public, not only scientists, should decide. WHO's [Global guidance framework for the responsible use of the life sciences](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240056107) sets out how to govern such research.

## Tutorials and further reading

- `8` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, The Ethics of Clinical Research](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/clinical-research/): the philosophical questions behind research with people, such as how research differs from clinical care, clinical equipoise, minimal risk and industry-sponsored research.
- `7` [Principles of Transparency and Best Practice in Scholarly Publishing](https://doaj.org/apply/transparency/) (COPE, DOAJ, OASPA and WAME, fifth version, 2026): what journals and publishers must do about peer review, authorship, AI, conflicts of interest, misconduct allegations and corrections.
- `6` [EQUATOR Network](https://equator-network.org/): a library of reporting guidelines for health research, such as CONSORT for randomised trials, SPIRIT for trial protocols and STROBE for observational studies.
- `6` [TRREE, Training and Resources in Research Ethics Evaluation](https://elearning.trree.org/): online modules, with certification, on research ethics evaluation, informed consent and related topics, in several languages.
- `5` [The Embassy of Good Science](https://embassy.science/wiki/Main_Page): a wiki platform for research integrity and ethics, with guidance, cases and training materials contributed by the research community.
