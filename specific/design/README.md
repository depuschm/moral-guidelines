# Product and UX design

Scope: designers, product managers, researchers, writers and engineers who shape the screens, flows, copy and defaults of digital products, such as websites, apps, online shops and subscription services. It covers deceptive design patterns, defaults, accessibility, honest copy and designing for people's wellbeing rather than compulsive use. The general rules on [honesty](../../general/honesty.md), [respect, autonomy and consent](../../general/respect-and-consent.md) and [harm and care](../../general/harm-and-care.md) matter most here. Collecting and using personal data is covered in [Data and analytics](../data/README.md), advertising and marketing to children in [Marketing and advertising](../marketing/README.md), and monetisation and loot boxes in [Games](../games/README.md). This is moral guidance, not legal advice: where a rule names a law, check with a lawyer how it applies to your product.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `10` [Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) (W3C Recommendation): the international standard for accessible web content and apps, organised under four principles (perceivable, operable, understandable and robust), with testable success criteria at levels A, AA and AAA. **Follow level AA as the baseline for accessibility.**
- `9` [EDPB Guidelines 03/2022 on deceptive design patterns in social media platform interfaces](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-032022-on-deceptive-design-patterns-in-social-media-platform_en) (European Data Protection Board, version 2.0, 2023): **the baseline for honest interfaces**: a taxonomy of deceptive design patterns (overloading, skipping, stirring, obstructing, fickle and left in the dark) across the life of an account, from sign-up to deletion, with a checklist and best practices. It is written for social media under the GDPR, but the patterns occur in any interface.
- `9` [Regulation (EU) 2022/2065, Digital Services Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065/oj) (EUR-Lex): Article 25 forbids online platforms in the EU, where consumer and data protection law don't already apply, from designing interfaces that deceive or manipulate users, or that impair their ability to make free and informed decisions, and names giving one choice more prominence, asking again after a choice was made, and making cancelling harder than subscribing. Article 28 requires a high level of privacy, safety and security for minors.
- `8` [Bringing Dark Patterns to Light](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf) (US Federal Trade Commission staff report, 2022): how dark patterns create false beliefs, hide or delay costs, lead to unauthorised charges and subvert privacy choices, with FTC cases and an appendix that defines over thirty patterns with examples.
- `8` [Dark commercial patterns](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2022/10/dark-commercial-patterns_9f6169cd/44f5e846-en.pdf) (OECD Digital Economy Papers No. 336, 2022): a working definition of dark commercial patterns, an example taxonomy, evidence of how common and harmful they are, and the policy and enforcement responses available.
- `8` [Commission guidelines on the protection of minors under the Digital Services Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors) (European Commission, 2025): the measures the Commission uses to assess Article 28(1), including private accounts by default, features that drive excessive use (streaks, autoplay and push notifications) switched off by default, no exploitative commercial practices, and blocking, muting and reporting tools.
- `8` [Age appropriate design: a code of practice for online services](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/) (UK Information Commissioner's Office, Children's code): 15 standards for online services that children are likely to use, including high-privacy defaults, no detrimental use of their data and no nudge techniques.

## Key rules

Only rules the adopted references don't already cover, or choices they leave open.

### Honest choices

- `9` `DESIGN-1` **Tell the truth about time, stock and popularity: no fake countdown timers, false "only 2 left" messages, made-up demand or fake reviews.** False urgency, scarcity and social proof pressure people to decide before they have thought, and regulators treat them as deceptive. See `HON-2`. ([FTC, Bringing Dark Patterns to Light](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf), [OECD, Dark commercial patterns](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2022/10/dark-commercial-patterns_9f6169cd/44f5e846-en.pdf))
- `9` `DESIGN-2` **Show the full price, including every mandatory fee, from the start, and get the user's express agreement to every extra: no drip pricing and no pre-ticked boxes for add-ons.** In the EU, a trader that infers consent to an extra payment from a default option the consumer had to untick must refund it. See `HON-2`. ([FTC, Bringing Dark Patterns to Light](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf), [Consumer Rights Directive 2011/83/EU, Article 22](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2011/83/oj#art_22))
- `9` `DESIGN-3` **Make cancelling a subscription as easy as signing up, through the same channel, without a maze of screens, retention offers you can't skip or a phone line that is hard to reach.** Article 25 of the EU Digital Services Act names cancelling that is harder than subscribing among the practices it targets. In the US, the FTC enforces this under the Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act, as in its 2025 settlement with Amazon over Prime; its 2024 "click-to-cancel" rule was vacated, and in March 2026 it asked for comments on a new one. See `RESP-2`. ([Digital Services Act, Article 25](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065/oj#art_25), [FTC, Historic $2.5 Billion Settlement Against Amazon](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-secures-historic-25-billion-settlement-against-amazon), [FTC, Advance notice of proposed rulemaking on negative option marketing](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/03/ftc-seeks-public-comment-response-advance-notice-proposed-rulemaking-regarding-negative-option))
- `8` `DESIGN-4` **Present choices neutrally: give "no" the same prominence and ease as "yes", don't shame people for declining ("No thanks, I don't like saving money"), and don't ask again once they have answered.** Emotional steering, visual tricks and repeated prompts push people into choices they wouldn't otherwise make. See `RESP-10`. ([EDPB Guidelines 03/2022](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-032022-on-deceptive-design-patterns-in-social-media-platform_en), [Digital Services Act, Article 25](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065/oj#art_25), [ICO Children's code, 13. Nudge techniques](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/13-nudge-techniques/))
- `8` `DESIGN-5` **Write copy that says plainly what will happen: no trick questions or double negatives, no buttons whose label hides what they do, and no ads made to look like reviews, news or neutral rankings.** Online platforms in the EU must label every advertisement clearly and say who paid for it. See `HON-1`. ([FTC, Bringing Dark Patterns to Light](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf), [Digital Services Act, Article 26](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2065/oj#art_26))

### Defaults and control

- `8` `DESIGN-6` **Make the defaults protect the user: ship with the most privacy-protective settings, and leave optional data sharing, marketing and public visibility off until the user turns them on.** People rarely change a default, so the default often becomes their choice; for children's services the UK Children's code requires high-privacy defaults. The rules for personal data are in [Data and analytics](../data/README.md). See `RESP-8`. ([EDPB Guidelines 4/2019 on data protection by design and by default](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-42019-on-article-25-data-protection-by-design-and-by-default_en), [ICO Children's code, 7. Default settings](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/7-default-settings/))
- `7` `DESIGN-7` **Make leaving as easy as joining: let people find and complete account deletion without hunting through settings or contacting support.** Accounts that are hard or impossible to delete keep people tied to a service, and to its use of their data, after they have decided to leave. ([FTC, Bringing Dark Patterns to Light, "Immortal Accounts"](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf), [EDPB Guidelines 03/2022, use case 5](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-032022-on-deceptive-design-patterns-in-social-media-platform_en))

### Accessible and inclusive design

- `8` `DESIGN-8` **Meet WCAG 2.2 level AA at least, and build accessibility in from the first designs rather than fixing it before launch.** The UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities asks states to promote accessible technology designed in at an early stage, when it costs least, and the European Accessibility Act makes accessibility a legal requirement for services such as e-commerce, banking and e-books in the EU. See `FAIR-3`. ([W3C, WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/), [UN CRPD, Article 9: Accessibility](https://social.desa.un.org/issues/disability/crpd/article-9-accessibility), [European Commission, European Accessibility Act](https://commission.europa.eu/strategy-and-policy/policies/justice-and-fundamental-rights/disability/european-accessibility-act-eaa_en))
- `7` `DESIGN-9` **Design for people with cognitive and learning disabilities, and for anyone tired, stressed or short of time: use clear language, help people avoid and fix mistakes, and don't make a task depend on remembering or transcribing information.** WCAG 2.2 adds an "accessible authentication" criterion for this, and W3C's guidance on cognitive accessibility explains how to design for these users. ([W3C, Making Content Usable for People with Cognitive and Learning Disabilities](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/), [W3C, WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/))
- `7` `DESIGN-10` **Test designs with disabled and older people during development, not only with automated checkers at the end.** A few users' feedback doesn't replace conformance testing, so combine both. See `REASON-3`. ([W3C WAI, Involving Users in Evaluating Web Accessibility](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/involving-users/))

### Attention and wellbeing

- `8` `DESIGN-11` **Design for people's wellbeing, not for maximum time spent: don't use infinite scroll, autoplay, streaks or reward loops to make it hard to stop, especially in services children use.** The European Commission expects platforms that minors use to switch such features off by default, and the UK Children's code treats data-driven features that make it hard for children to disengage as likely to be unfair. See `HARM-4`. ([European Commission, Guidelines on the protection of minors](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors), [ICO Children's code, 5. Detrimental use of data](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/5-detrimental-use-of-data/))
- `7` `DESIGN-12` **Make it easy to stop and to control interruptions: offer a neutral choice to continue rather than continuing automatically, let people pause without losing progress, and give them control over notifications.** Don't suggest that people will lose out if they take a break. ([ICO Children's code, 5. Detrimental use of data](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/5-detrimental-use-of-data/), [European Commission, Guidelines on the protection of minors](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors))

## How to apply

Practices, methods and tools that help people follow the key rules.

- `7` [EDPB Guidelines 03/2022, Annex I checklist and Annex II best practices](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/documents/2023-02/edpb_03-2022_guidelines_on_deceptive_design_patterns_in_social_media_platform_interfaces_v2_en_0.pdf): go through each screen of a flow with the checklist of deceptive pattern types, and use the best practices, such as consistent wording and layout, as design principles. Supports `DESIGN-4`, `DESIGN-5`, `DESIGN-6` and `DESIGN-7`.
- `6` [W3C WAI, Easy Checks: A First Review of Web Accessibility](https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/easy-checks/): quick checks anyone can run on a page, such as alternative text, headings, contrast, keyboard focus, zoom, captions and form labels, before a full WCAG evaluation. Supports `DESIGN-8`.
- `5` [Deceptive Patterns, Types of deceptive pattern](https://deceptive.design/types/) (Harry Brignull): a catalogue of deceptive pattern types, such as hard to cancel, preselection, confirmshaming and fake urgency, each mapped to examples, laws and enforcement actions. Use it in design reviews to name what you see. Supports `DESIGN-1`, `DESIGN-2`, `DESIGN-3` and `DESIGN-4`.

## Open questions

Questions on which serious traditions or experts disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `8` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, The Ethics of Manipulation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-manipulation/): when does nudging become manipulation? Some argue that every interface frames choices somehow, so deliberate framing that helps people is legitimate; others reply that intentionally exploiting biases that bypass people's reasoning is manipulative even when the goal is good, and there is no agreement on which psychological mechanisms cross the line.

## Tutorials and further reading

- `7` [Online Choice Architecture: How digital design can harm competition and consumers](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/online-choice-architecture-how-digital-design-can-harm-competition-and-consumers) (UK Competition and Markets Authority, 2022): how the way choices are presented online can work against consumers and competition, with the evidence behind each practice.
- `6` [Dark Patterns at Scale: Findings from a Crawl of 11K Shopping Websites](https://webtransparency.cs.princeton.edu/dark-patterns/) (Mathur and others, Princeton University): a large study of how common dark patterns are on shopping sites, with categories, examples and the third parties that supply them.
