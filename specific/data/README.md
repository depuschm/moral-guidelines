# Data and analytics

Scope: anyone who collects, shares, links, analyses or publishes data about people, such as analysts, data scientists, statisticians and the organisations they work for. Data protection laws set the minimum in many countries; where a rule follows a law, it names it, and none of this is legal advice. Training, testing and explaining AI models are covered in [Artificial intelligence](../ai/README.md); this file covers the data. Building secure systems is in [Software engineering](../software/README.md), research with human participants in [Research with people and animals](../research/README.md), and what individuals share about others online in [Online life and social media](../online/README.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [Data and AI Ethics Framework](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/data-ethics-framework/data-and-ai-ethics-framework) (UK government, 2025, formerly the Data Ethics Framework): principles of transparency, accountability, fairness, privacy, environmental sustainability, societal impact and safety, each with recommended actions, for teams that develop, buy or use data and AI. Written for the public sector, but usable by any organisation. **Follow it as the baseline for running a data project.**
- `9` [OECD Privacy Guidelines](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/public/doc/114/114.en.pdf) (Recommendation of the Council concerning Guidelines Governing the Protection of Privacy and Transborder Flows of Personal Data, 1980, revised 2013): eight basic principles for handling personal data: collection limitation, data quality, purpose specification, use limitation, security safeguards, openness, individual participation and accountability.
- `8` [Principles of the GDPR](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/principles-gdpr_en) (European Commission): a plain-language explanation of the principles in Article 5 of the General Data Protection Regulation: lawfulness, fairness and transparency, purpose limitation, data minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation, integrity and confidentiality, and accountability. They are law in the EU.
- `8` [CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance](https://www.gida-global.org/careprinciples) (Global Indigenous Data Alliance): people- and purpose-oriented principles (Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility and Ethics) for data about Indigenous Peoples, which complement the FAIR principles for data sharing.
- `8` [Ethical Guidelines for Statistical Practice](https://www.amstat.org/your-career/ethical-guidelines-for-statistical-practice) (American Statistical Association, 2022): the statistics profession's code: integrity of data and methods, and responsibilities to stakeholders, data subjects, team members and the profession.
- `7` [The Data Ethics Canvas](https://theodi.org/insights/tools/the-data-ethics-canvas-2021/) (Open Data Institute, 2021): a tool for identifying and managing ethical issues at the start of a project that uses data and throughout it, with questions such as what the primary purpose is and who could be negatively affected.

## Key rules

### Purpose and minimisation

- `9` `DATA-1` **Before you collect or reuse data about people, state a specific, legitimate purpose, and don't use the data for a new purpose that is incompatible with it.** Write down what the project is for in plain terms, and tell people the purpose when you collect their data. See `RESP-8`. ([European Commission, Purpose limitation](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/principles-gdpr_en#purpose-limitation), [OECD Privacy Guidelines, Purpose Specification and Use Limitation Principles](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/public/doc/114/114.en.pdf), [ODI, Data Ethics Canvas](https://theodi.org/insights/tools/the-data-ethics-canvas-2021/))
- `8` `DATA-2` **Collect and keep only the data your purpose needs, for no longer than it needs it, and use anonymised or synthetic data where that will do.** See `RESP-8`. ([European Commission, Data minimisation](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/principles-gdpr_en#data-minimisation), [OECD Privacy Guidelines, Collection Limitation Principle](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/public/doc/114/114.en.pdf), [UK Data and AI Ethics Framework, Privacy](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/data-ethics-framework/data-and-ai-ethics-framework#privacy-section))
- `7` `DATA-3` **Check that your data is accurate, up to date and fit for your purpose, and document where it came from, how it was collected and processed, and its gaps and limits.** See `HON-1`. ([OECD Privacy Guidelines, Data Quality Principle](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/public/doc/114/114.en.pdf), [ASA, Principle B: Integrity of Data and Methods](https://www.amstat.org/your-career/ethical-guidelines-for-statistical-practice), [UK Data and AI Ethics Framework, Transparency](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/data-ethics-framework/data-and-ai-ethics-framework#transparency-section))

### Transparency, consent and control

- `8` `DATA-4` **Tell people, in plain words and when you collect their data, what you collect, why, who will receive it, how long you will keep it and what rights they have.** See `HON-2`. ([European Commission, Principles of the GDPR](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/principles-gdpr_en), [OECD Privacy Guidelines, Openness Principle](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/public/doc/114/114.en.pdf))
- `8` `DATA-5` **Where you rely on consent, make it a real choice, freely given, specific and informed, let people withdraw it as easily as they gave it, and use their data only as they agreed.** Under the GDPR, consent must also be unambiguous, and it is one of six lawful bases for using personal data. See `RESP-7`. ([EDPB Guidelines 05/2020 on consent](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/guideline/guidelines-052020-on-consent-under-regulation-2016679_en), [ASA, Principle D](https://www.amstat.org/your-career/ethical-guidelines-for-statistical-practice), [OECD Privacy Guidelines, Collection Limitation Principle](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/public/doc/114/114.en.pdf))
- `7` `DATA-6` **Let people find out what data you hold about them, get a copy, and have it corrected or deleted.** Under the GDPR, organisations must answer such requests without undue delay, in principle within one month. See `RESP-2`. ([OECD Privacy Guidelines, Individual Participation Principle](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/public/doc/114/114.en.pdf), [European Commission, Dealing with requests from individuals](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/dealing-requests-individuals_en))

### Anonymisation and security

- `8` `DATA-7` **Don't treat data as anonymous until you have tested whether people could be identified from it, alone or linked with other data, and treat pseudonymised data as personal data.** Removing names is not enough: a study estimated that 99.98% of Americans could be correctly re-identified in any dataset using 15 demographic attributes. See `RESP-8`. ([ICO, How do we ensure anonymisation is effective?](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-sharing/anonymisation/how-do-we-ensure-anonymisation-is-effective/), [Article 29 Working Party, Opinion 05/2014 on Anonymisation Techniques](https://ec.europa.eu/justice/article-29/documentation/opinion-recommendation/files/2014/wp216_en.pdf), [Rocher, Hendrickx and de Montjoye, Nature Communications 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-10933-3))
- `8` `DATA-8` **Don't try to re-identify people in data that was shared or published in de-identified form unless whoever de-identified it has agreed, for example to test how well it was done; report a weakness you find to them instead.** In the UK, knowingly or recklessly re-identifying de-identified personal data without the consent of the controller who de-identified it is a criminal offence, with defences such as the public interest. Reporting weaknesses in systems is covered in [Security research](../security/README.md). See `RESP-8`. ([ASA, Principle D](https://www.amstat.org/your-career/ethical-guidelines-for-statistical-practice), [Data Protection Act 2018, section 171](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2018/12/section/171))
- `8` `DATA-9` **Protect the data you hold against unauthorised access, loss and misuse, limit who can see it, and plan how you will respond to a breach.** See `RESP-9`. ([OECD Privacy Guidelines, Security Safeguards Principle](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/public/doc/114/114.en.pdf), [European Commission, Principles of the GDPR](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/principles-gdpr_en), [UK Data and AI Ethics Framework, Privacy](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/data-ethics-framework/data-and-ai-ethics-framework#privacy-section))

### Fairness and benefit

- `8` `DATA-10` **Check data for bias before you rely on it: whether it represents the people it will be used about, and whether some variables act as proxies for protected characteristics, as a postcode can for age or race.** Testing models built on the data for bias is covered by `AI-6` in [Artificial intelligence](../ai/README.md). See `FAIR-2`. ([UK Data and AI Ethics Framework, Fairness](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/data-ethics-framework/data-and-ai-ethics-framework#fairness-section), [ASA, Principle B: Integrity of Data and Methods](https://www.amstat.org/your-career/ethical-guidelines-for-statistical-practice))
- `7` `DATA-11` **Make sure the people the data is about share in its benefits, and involve them in decisions about how it is used, especially communities with collective rights and interests in data, such as Indigenous Peoples.** See `FAIR-12`. ([CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance](https://datascience.codata.org/articles/10.5334/dsj-2020-043), [UK Data and AI Ethics Framework, Societal impact](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/data-ethics-framework/data-and-ai-ethics-framework#societal-impact-section))

## How to apply

- `7` [Data Protection Impact Assessments](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/data-protection-impact-assessments-dpias/) (UK Information Commissioner's Office): when a data protection impact assessment is needed, what processing is likely to be high risk, and how to carry one out step by step, with a template. Under review after the UK Data (Use and Access) Act 2025. Supports `DATA-1`, `DATA-2` and `DATA-9`.
- `6` [Motivated intruder test](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-sharing/anonymisation/how-do-we-ensure-anonymisation-is-effective/#motivatedintruder) (ICO): assess whether someone motivated to identify the people in apparently anonymous data could do so with the means reasonably likely to be used. Supports `DATA-7` and `DATA-8`.
- `6` [Data and AI Ethics Self-Assessment Tool](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/data-ethics-framework) (UK government): a companion to the Data and AI Ethics Framework that helps teams capture information about a project and share what they learn, their challenges and their progress. Supports `DATA-1`, `DATA-10` and `DATA-11`.

## Open questions

Questions on which serious traditions or experts disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Privacy and Information Technology: Technology, agency and responsibility](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/it-privacy/#TechAgRes): how much responsibility for privacy can rest on individuals' consent. Data protection rests largely on informed consent, but people often click away consent requests, act against the privacy concerns they state (the "privacy paradox") and face interfaces designed to make them share more, so it is debated whether such choices can carry that weight and, if not, who should create better conditions.
- `7` [Internet Research: Ethical Guidelines 3.0](https://aoir.org/reports/ethics3.pdf) (Association of Internet Researchers, 2019): whether it is right to collect and analyse what people post in public or semi-public online spaces without their consent. People often expect such exchanges to stay private even where they are technically public; strongly deontological traditions, as in much of Europe, call for respecting that expectation, while more utilitarian approaches, as in the US and the UK, weigh it against the benefit to society.

## Tutorials and further reading

- `8` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Privacy and Information Technology](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/it-privacy/) (revised 2024): what privacy is and why it matters, how the internet, social media, big data and surveillance affect it, and how technology itself can protect it.
- `7` [Declaration on Professional Ethics](https://isi-web.org/modern-declaration-professional-ethics) (International Statistical Institute, 2010, updated 2023): the shared values of statisticians worldwide (respect, professionalism, truthfulness and integrity) and the ethical principles that follow from them.
- `6` [The CARE Principles for Indigenous Data Governance](https://datascience.codata.org/articles/10.5334/dsj-2020-043) (Carroll and others, Data Science Journal, 2020): the article that sets out each principle and how CARE relates to the FAIR principles.
- `5` [FAIR Principles](https://www.gofair.foundation/fair-principles) (GO FAIR Foundation): guidelines for making data findable, accessible, interoperable and reusable, which the CARE Principles complement.
