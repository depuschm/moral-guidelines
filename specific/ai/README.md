# Artificial intelligence

Scope: people and organisations who design, develop, deploy, buy or use AI systems, including machine-learning models, generative AI and automated decision-making. The guidelines adopt the intergovernmental instruments on AI ethics. Where a rule follows the EU AI Act, it says so: the Act applies to AI systems placed on the market or used in the EU, many of its duties apply only to high-risk systems, and it was amended in 2026, so check with a lawyer what applies to you. The duties of everyone who builds software are in [Software engineering](../software/README.md), collecting and preparing the data that AI uses in [Data and analytics](../data/README.md), and research with human participants in [Research with people and animals](../research/README.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `10` [UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence](https://www.unesco.org/en/artificial-intelligence/recommendation-ethics) (2021): the first global standard on AI ethics, adopted by UNESCO's 193 Member States. Four core values, ten principles (among them proportionality and do no harm, safety and security, privacy, accountability, transparency and explainability, human oversight and determination, and fairness and non-discrimination) and policy actions. **Follow it as the baseline.**
- `9` [OECD AI Principles](https://oecd.ai/en/ai-principles) (OECD Recommendation on Artificial Intelligence, 2019, updated 2024): the first intergovernmental standard on AI, with 47 adherents. Five values-based principles for trustworthy AI (inclusive growth and well-being; human rights, fairness and privacy; transparency and explainability; robustness, security and safety; accountability) and recommendations for governments.
- `9` [EU AI Act, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act-explorer) (European Commission, AI Act Explorer): binding EU law with a risk-based approach: prohibited practices (Article 5), requirements for high-risk systems such as risk management, data governance, human oversight and robustness, and transparency duties for chatbots and AI-generated content (Article 50). The Explorer shows the consolidated text, including the 2026 amendments by the Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744).
- `9` [Council of Europe Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence and Human Rights, Democracy and the Rule of Law](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-12516-2024-INIT/en/pdf) (2024, text as published by the Council of the EU): a legally binding treaty, open to states outside Europe, that commits its parties to measures on human dignity, transparency and oversight, accountability, equality, privacy, reliability, remedies and risk and impact management across the life cycle of AI systems.
- `8` [NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework) (US National Institute of Standards and Technology, 2023): a voluntary framework for managing the risks of AI to people, organisations and society, organised in four functions (govern, map, measure, manage), with the characteristics of trustworthy AI and a Generative AI Profile.

## Key rules

### Limits on use

- `9` `AI-1` **Don't build or use AI for the practices that the EU AI Act prohibits, such as manipulation or exploitation of people's vulnerabilities that causes them significant harm, social scoring, or generating intimate images of real people without their consent.** Article 5 also bans predicting crime from profiling alone, building face databases by untargeted scraping, inferring emotions at work or in education, and inferring race, beliefs or sexual orientation from biometric data, with narrow exceptions; the ban on non-consensual intimate images applies from December 2026. The Commission describes these practices as violating fundamental rights by their nature, and the Council of Europe convention asks states to consider bans on uses that are incompatible with human rights. See `RIGHTS-12`. ([EU AI Act, Article 5](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-5), [European Commission, AI Act](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai), [Council of Europe Framework Convention, Article 16](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-12516-2024-INIT/en/pdf))
- `8` `AI-2` **Use AI only where it is necessary and proportionate to a legitimate aim, and assess the risks to people's rights, safety and the environment before you deploy it and whenever it changes.** In the EU, public bodies and some other deployers of high-risk systems must do a fundamental rights impact assessment first. See `HARM-4`. ([UNESCO, Principle 1: Proportionality and do no harm](https://www.unesco.org/en/artificial-intelligence/recommendation-ethics), [NIST AI RMF 1.0](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf), [EU AI Act, Article 27](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-27))

### Human oversight and accountability

- `9` `AI-3` **Keep people in control of AI that makes or shapes decisions about people: someone competent must be able to understand its output, ignore or override it, and stop the system.** AI must not displace ultimate human responsibility. See `ACCT-1`. ([UNESCO, Principle 7: Human oversight and determination](https://www.unesco.org/en/artificial-intelligence/recommendation-ethics), [EU AI Act, Article 14](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-14), [OECD, Human-centred values and fairness](https://oecd.ai/en/dashboards/ai-principles/P6))
- `8` `AI-4` **Make clear who is accountable for each AI system, keep records so that its outputs can be traced and audited, and give people a way to challenge its decisions and get redress.** See `ACCT-1`. ([OECD, Accountability](https://oecd.ai/en/dashboards/ai-principles/P9), [UNESCO, Principle 5: Responsibility and accountability](https://www.unesco.org/en/artificial-intelligence/recommendation-ethics), [Council of Europe Framework Convention, Articles 9, 14 and 15](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-12516-2024-INIT/en/pdf))
- `7` `AI-5` **Train the people who use or oversee AI to understand what it can and can't do, and guard against automation bias, the tendency to rely too much on a system's output.** ([EU AI Act, Article 14(4)](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-14), [Article 4: AI literacy](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-4), [UNESCO, Principle 9: Awareness and literacy](https://www.unesco.org/en/artificial-intelligence/recommendation-ethics))

### Fairness, transparency and explanation

- `9` `AI-6` **Test AI systems for harmful bias before you deploy them and while they are in use, across the groups of people they affect, and fix the system or stop using it if it treats people unfairly.** Bias in AI can be systemic and human as well as statistical, and what counts as fair differs between cultures and applications, so decide what fairness means in your context; a system in which harmful biases are mitigated is not necessarily fair. See `FAIR-2`. ([NIST AI RMF 1.0, 3.7 and MEASURE 2.11](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf), [NIST SP 1270](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.1270.pdf), [UNESCO, Principle 10: Fairness and non-discrimination](https://www.unesco.org/en/artificial-intelligence/recommendation-ethics))
- `8` `AI-7` **Tell people when they are interacting with an AI system rather than a person, and label deepfakes and other AI-generated content, unless it is obvious.** In the EU, these transparency duties apply from August 2026. See `HON-2`. ([EU AI Act, Article 50](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-50), [Council of Europe Framework Convention, Article 15](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-12516-2024-INIT/en/pdf), [OECD, Transparency and explainability](https://oecd.ai/en/dashboards/ai-principles/P7))
- `8` `AI-8` **When AI is used to make or support a decision that significantly affects someone, give them a clear explanation of the main factors and logic behind it, in terms they can understand, so that they can challenge it.** In the EU, people affected by decisions based on certain high-risk systems have a right to such an explanation. See `FAIR-8`. ([OECD, Transparency and explainability](https://oecd.ai/en/dashboards/ai-principles/P7), [EU AI Act, Article 86](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-86), [ICO and The Alan Turing Institute, Explaining decisions made with AI](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/artificial-intelligence/explaining-decisions-made-with-artificial-intelligence/))
- `8` `AI-9` **Be open about what an AI system is for, what it can and can't do, how well it performs and for whom, and the limits of the conditions it was tested in.** See `HON-2`. ([OECD, Transparency and explainability](https://oecd.ai/en/dashboards/ai-principles/P7), [NIST AI RMF 1.0, MEASURE 2.5](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf), [UNESCO, Principle 6: Transparency and explainability](https://www.unesco.org/en/artificial-intelligence/recommendation-ethics))

### Safety, security and privacy

- `9` `AI-10` **Test AI systems for safety, robustness and security before release and throughout their use, including against foreseeable misuse and attacks such as data poisoning, and make sure they can be overridden, repaired or shut down safely.** See `HARM-4`. ([OECD, Robustness, security and safety](https://oecd.ai/en/dashboards/ai-principles/P8), [NIST AI RMF 1.0, MEASURE 2.6 and 2.7](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf), [EU AI Act, Article 15](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-15))
- `8` `AI-11` **Protect the privacy of the people whose data is used to train, test or run an AI system, throughout its life cycle.** The rules for collecting, minimising and anonymising that data are in [Data and analytics](../data/README.md). See `RESP-8`. ([UNESCO, Principle 3: Right to privacy and data protection](https://www.unesco.org/en/artificial-intelligence/recommendation-ethics), [Council of Europe Framework Convention, Article 11](https://data.consilium.europa.eu/doc/document/ST-12516-2024-INIT/en/pdf))
- `7` `AI-12` **Monitor AI systems after deployment, record and fix incidents and errors, tell the people affected, and report serious incidents to the authorities where the law requires it.** In the EU, providers of high-risk systems must monitor them and report serious incidents. See `ACCT-2`. ([NIST AI RMF 1.0, MANAGE 4.1 and 4.3](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.100-1.pdf), [EU AI Act, Article 72](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-72), [Article 73](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-73))

## How to apply

- `7` [Model Cards for Model Reporting](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993) (Mitchell and others, 2019): short documents released with a trained model that state its intended uses and report its performance across conditions and groups of people. Supports `AI-6` and `AI-9`.
- `7` [Datasheets for Datasets](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09010) (Gebru and others): a datasheet for every dataset that documents its motivation, composition, collection process and recommended uses. Supports `AI-6`, `AI-9` and `AI-11`.
- `7` [Algorithmic Impact Assessment tool](https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/digital-government-innovations/responsible-use-ai/algorithmic-impact-assessment.html) (Government of Canada): a mandatory questionnaire for federal automated decision systems, with 65 risk and 41 mitigation questions, that sets an impact level from I to IV, which decides the safeguards required. Other organisations can adapt its questions. Supports `AI-2` and `AI-4`.
- `7` [Explaining decisions made with AI](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/artificial-intelligence/explaining-decisions-made-with-artificial-intelligence/) (UK Information Commissioner's Office and The Alan Turing Institute): practical guidance on the types of explanation, how to give them in practice and what organisations need, for compliance, technical and management teams. Under review after the UK Data (Use and Access) Act 2025. Supports `AI-8`.

## Open questions

Questions on which serious traditions or experts disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Ethics of Artificial Intelligence and Robotics: Moral status, machine ethics, responsibility](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-ai/#MorStaMacEthRes): whether AI systems could have moral status, so that we would owe them something. The standard view is that they have none; some authors argue that robots should be given rights or that our relationship with them matters, others reject robot rights, and a growing view treats sentience as at least necessary, which raises the question whether it is right to build AI that might be conscious.
- `7` [International AI Safety Report 2026, section 3.4: Open-weight models](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026) (written by over 100 experts and backed by over 30 countries and international organisations): whether developers should release the weights of powerful models openly. Open weights bring research and commercial benefits, especially for less-resourced actors, but can't be recalled once released, and their safeguards are easier to remove, which makes misuse harder to prevent and trace. The [NTIA report on open model weights](https://www.ntia.gov/programs-and-initiatives/artificial-intelligence/open-model-weights-report) (2024) sets out the same trade-off for US policy.

## Tutorials and further reading

- `8` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Ethics of Artificial Intelligence and Robotics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-ai/) (revised 2026): the main debates, from privacy, manipulation, opacity and bias to autonomous systems, moral status and AI policy.
- `7` [NIST AI 600-1, Generative Artificial Intelligence Profile](https://www.nist.gov/publications/artificial-intelligence-risk-management-framework-generative-artificial-intelligence) (2024): a companion to the AI RMF on the risks that are specific to generative AI and the actions that manage them.
- `6` [NIST SP 1270, Towards a Standard for Identifying and Managing Bias in Artificial Intelligence](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/SpecialPublications/NIST.SP.1270.pdf) (2022): the systemic, statistical and human sources of bias in AI, and how they arise across the life cycle.
- `6` [Fairness and Machine Learning: Limitations and Opportunities](https://fairmlbook.org/) (Barocas, Hardt and Narayanan): a free textbook on when automated decisions are legitimate, formal non-discrimination criteria and their limits.
