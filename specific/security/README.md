# Security research

Scope: people who look for security vulnerabilities in software, devices and online services, whether as independent researchers, penetration testers, academics or bug bounty hunters, and the organisations that receive their reports. It covers getting permission, limiting harm while testing, reporting and coordinated vulnerability disclosure (CVD), and the limits of hacking for good. Anti-hacking laws differ between countries and these guidelines are not legal advice: check the law where you and the system are, and the terms of any programme you take part in. Building secure software is covered in [Software engineering](../software/README.md), handling personal data in [Data and analytics](../data/README.md), and research that studies people in [Research with people and animals](../research/README.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `10` [The CERT Guide to Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure](https://certcc.github.io/CERT-Guide-to-CVD/) (CERT Coordination Center, Carnegie Mellon University; web edition of the 2017 report): the principles of CVD (reduce harm, presume benevolence, avoid surprise, incentivise desired behaviour), the roles of finders, vendors, deployers and coordinators, and each phase from discovery to publication. **Follow it as the baseline.**
- `9` [The Menlo Report: Ethical Principles Guiding Information and Communication Technology Research](https://www.impactcybertrust.org/link_docs/Menlo-Report.pdf) (Dittrich, Kenneally and others, funded by the US Department of Homeland Security, 2012): applies the Belmont Report's principles of respect for persons, beneficence and justice to security and network research, and adds respect for law and public interest.
- `7` [Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure Program](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/programs/coordinated-vulnerability-disclosure-cvd-program) (US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency): how CISA coordinates reports between researchers and vendors, from intake to the published advisory, and when it may publish without a fix, for example as early as 45 days after first trying to reach a vendor that doesn't respond.
- `7` [Justice Manual 9-48.000, Computer Fraud and Abuse Act](https://www.justice.gov/jm/jm-9-48000-computer-fraud) (US Department of Justice, policy of May 2022): tells federal prosecutors to decline to charge good-faith security research under the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA), and defines what counts as good faith. It guides US federal prosecutors only and gives no one legal rights.
- `7` [Guidelines and Practices for Multi-Party Vulnerability Coordination and Disclosure](https://www.first.org/global/sigs/vulnerability-coordination/multiparty/guidelines-v1.1) (FIRST, version 1.1, 2020): how to coordinate when a vulnerability affects several vendors or a shared component.
- `6` [The disclose.io Framework](https://disclose.io/framework/): public-domain boilerplate for vulnerability disclosure policies, bug bounty programmes and safe-harbour terms, which set out what an organisation commits to and what it expects of researchers, with a maturity model for programmes.

## Key rules

### Permission and limits while testing

- `9` `SECRES-1` **Test only systems you own, have permission to test, or that the owner has opened to research in a published vulnerability disclosure policy, and stay within its scope and terms.** Unauthorised access is a crime under laws such as the UK Computer Misuse Act 1990 and the US CFAA, and a policy's safe-harbour terms bind only the organisation that published it. See `RESP-5`. ([ACM Code, 2.8](https://www.acm.org/code-of-ethics), [disclose.io, Vulnerability Disclosure Policy](https://disclose.io/framework/terms/vdp/), [Computer Misuse Act 1990, section 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1990/18/section/1), [Justice Manual 9-48.000](https://www.justice.gov/jm/jm-9-48000-computer-fraud))
- `9` `SECRES-3` **If a vulnerability gives you access to personal or confidential data, access only the minimum needed to prove it, stop testing and report it straight away; use only test accounts you own or have permission to use.** See `RESP-8`. ([disclose.io, Vulnerability Disclosure Policy](https://disclose.io/framework/terms/vdp/), [Menlo Report, Respect for Persons](https://www.impactcybertrust.org/link_docs/Menlo-Report.pdf))
- `8` `SECRES-2` **Do only what you need to show that a vulnerability exists: don't disrupt services, change or destroy data, or harm the experience of users.** US Department of Justice policy treats research as good faith only if it is carried out in a way designed to avoid any harm to individuals or the public. See `HARM-4`. ([disclose.io, Vulnerability Disclosure Policy](https://disclose.io/framework/terms/vdp/), [Justice Manual 9-48.000](https://www.justice.gov/jm/jm-9-48000-computer-fraud), [Menlo Report, Beneficence](https://www.impactcybertrust.org/link_docs/Menlo-Report.pdf))
- `6` `SECRES-4` **Get ethics review for research that collects data about people or could affect systems at scale, such as internet-wide measurements, and weigh the risks to third parties who never agreed to take part.** The Menlo Report asks researchers to consider secondary, collateral risks, for example to the customers of the people whose data they study, and to be prepared to inform those affected. ([Menlo Report](https://www.impactcybertrust.org/link_docs/Menlo-Report.pdf))

### Reporting and disclosure

- `8` `SECRES-5` **Report a vulnerability first to the vendor or owner through its official channel, or through a coordinator such as a national CERT if you can't reach it or it doesn't respond.** Don't assume that a vendor already knows about a vulnerability unless it has been publicly reported. See `ACCT-9`. ([CERT Guide to CVD, Reporting](https://certcc.github.io/CERT-Guide-to-CVD/topics/phases/reporting/), [CISA, Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure Program](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/programs/coordinated-vulnerability-disclosure-cvd-program), [disclose.io, Vulnerability Disclosure Policy](https://disclose.io/framework/terms/vdp/))
- `8` `SECRES-6` **Agree a disclosure date with the vendor, give it a reasonable time to fix the problem before you publish, and publish once a fix is available or the agreed deadline has passed.** Common deadlines are 45 days at CERT/CC and 90 days at Google Project Zero, which plans to shorten its deadline to 60 days in February 2027. See `HON-5`. ([CERT/CC, Vulnerability Disclosure Policy](https://certcc.github.io/certcc_disclosure_policy/), [Google Project Zero, Vulnerability Disclosure Policy](https://projectzero.google/vulnerability-disclosure-policy.html), [FIRST, Multi-Party Vulnerability Coordination and Disclosure](https://www.first.org/global/sigs/vulnerability-coordination/multiparty/guidelines-v1.1))
- `8` `SECRES-7` **Move faster when a vulnerability is being exploited or threatens critical infrastructure, health or safety: involve a coordinator, and shorten the disclosure timeline so that people can protect themselves.** Project Zero, for example, discloses vulnerabilities exploited in the wild after 7 days. See `HARM-6`. ([CISA, Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure Program](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/programs/coordinated-vulnerability-disclosure-cvd-program), [CERT/CC, Vulnerability Disclosure Policy](https://certcc.github.io/certcc_disclosure_policy/), [Google Project Zero, Vulnerability Disclosure Policy](https://projectzero.google/vulnerability-disclosure-policy.html))
- `7` `SECRES-8` **When you publish, give users and defenders enough detail to judge their risk and protect themselves, and weigh what extra detail, such as a working exploit, would give attackers.** CVD aims to let defenders act without increasing attackers' advantage. See `HARM-4`. ([CERT Guide to CVD, Reduce Harm](https://certcc.github.io/CERT-Guide-to-CVD/topics/principles/reduce_harm/), [Ethical Considerations](https://certcc.github.io/CERT-Guide-to-CVD/topics/principles/ethics/))
- `6` `SECRES-9` **When a vulnerability affects several vendors or a shared component, coordinate with all of them, directly or through a coordinator, so that users of every affected product can be protected.** ([FIRST, Multi-Party Vulnerability Coordination and Disclosure](https://www.first.org/global/sigs/vulnerability-coordination/multiparty/guidelines-v1.1))

### Good faith

- `9` `SECRES-10` **Never use a vulnerability to threaten its owner or demand payment, and don't make your report or your silence depend on a reward.** Rewards are legitimate when a bug bounty programme offers them on its own terms, but research done to extort the owners of systems is not good faith. See `HARM-2`. ([Justice Manual 9-48.000](https://www.justice.gov/jm/jm-9-48000-computer-fraud), [disclose.io, Vulnerability Disclosure Policy](https://disclose.io/framework/terms/vdp/), [disclose.io, Bug Bounty Program Policy](https://disclose.io/framework/terms/bbp/))
- `7` `SECRES-11` **If you receive vulnerability reports, make reporting easy, presume the reporter acts in good faith, respond promptly, keep them informed and don't take legal action against good-faith research.** See `ACCT-10`. ([CERT Guide to CVD, Presume Benevolence](https://certcc.github.io/CERT-Guide-to-CVD/topics/principles/presume_benevolence/), [Reporting](https://certcc.github.io/CERT-Guide-to-CVD/topics/phases/reporting/), [disclose.io, Vulnerability Disclosure Policy](https://disclose.io/framework/terms/vdp/))

## How to apply

- `6` [security.txt, RFC 9116](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9116.html) (IETF, 2022): a machine-readable file, usually at `/.well-known/security.txt` on a website, that tells researchers how to report vulnerabilities and where the disclosure policy is. Supports `SECRES-5` and `SECRES-11`.
- `6` [Vulnerability Disclosure Policy Templates](https://certcc.github.io/CERT-Guide-to-CVD/reference/policy_templates/) (CERT/CC): reusable policy statements, gathered from many disclosure policies, for organisations writing or improving their own. Supports `SECRES-6` and `SECRES-11`.
- `5` [VDP with Coordinated Disclosure Window](https://disclose.io/framework/terms/vdp-with-cvd/) (disclose.io): a disclosure policy template with safe harbour and an explicit number of days before public disclosure. Supports `SECRES-1` and `SECRES-6`.

## Open questions

Questions on which serious traditions or experts disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `6` [ENISA, Good Practice Guide on Vulnerability Disclosure](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/sites/default/files/publications/Good%20practice%20guide%20on%20vulnerability%20disclosure.pdf) (2015), section 2.5: full disclosure, publishing a vulnerability at once, against coordinated disclosure. Critics of full disclosure say vendors get no time to fix the problem before attackers use it; advocates such as Bruce Schneier say public scrutiny is the only reliable way to improve security and that vendors otherwise lack the incentive to fix. The guide notes that finders and vendors each argue from their own worst case.
- `5` [Council on Foreign Relations, Using Incentives to Shape the Zero-Day Market](https://www.cfr.org/reports/using-incentives-shape-zero-day-market) (2016): whether it is acceptable to sell vulnerabilities and exploits to buyers other than the vendor, such as governments and brokers. Markets range from white (sold to the vendor) through grey (reaching the vendor late) to black (never reaching it); some propose that governments buy and disclose them, others want to restrict sales, and others prefer incentives to disclose. The [Pall Mall Process Code of Practice for States](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-pall-mall-process-code-of-practice-for-states/the-pall-mall-process-code-of-practice-for-states) (2025) accepts that such intrusion tools can have legitimate uses, but asks states to control their trade.

## Tutorials and further reading

- `6` [The CERT Guide to CVD in a Nutshell](https://certcc.github.io/CERT-Guide-to-CVD/tutorials/cvd_in_a_nutshell/) (CERT/CC): a short introduction to why vulnerabilities are inevitable and how coordinated disclosure deals with them.
- `6` [Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure Policies in the EU](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/publications/coordinated-vulnerability-disclosure-policies-in-the-eu) (ENISA, 2022): an overview of national CVD policies in the EU member states, with findings and recommendations.
- `5` [Zero Days, Thousands of Nights](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RR1751.html) (RAND, 2017): data on how long zero-day vulnerabilities and their exploits survive, to inform the debate on keeping them secret or disclosing them.
