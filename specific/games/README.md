# Games

Scope: people who design, build, publish and run video games, from solo developers to large studios, and the players who share them. It covers monetisation and loot boxes, children, player time and wellbeing, toxic play, accessibility, and the people who make games. The general rules on [honesty](../../general/honesty.md), [harm and care](../../general/harm-and-care.md) and [fairness](../../general/fairness.md) matter most here. Deceptive interface patterns, protective defaults and accessible interfaces in general are covered in [Product and UX design](../design/README.md), advertising to children in [Marketing and advertising](../marketing/README.md), working conditions in [Workplace and leadership](../workplace/README.md), and how players treat each other online in [Online life and social media](../online/README.md). This is moral guidance, not legal advice: rating rules, consumer law and children's privacy law differ between countries, so check what applies where your game is sold.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `8` [The Consumer Protection Cooperation Network's Key Principles on In-Game Virtual Currencies](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/8af13e88-6540-436c-b137-9853e7fe866a_en?filename=Key+principles+on+in-game+virtual+currencies.pdf) (EU consumer protection authorities, 2025): seven principles on how EU consumer law applies to virtual currencies bought with real money: real-money prices, no hidden costs, no forced currency purchases, clear information, the right of withdrawal, fair terms and respect for vulnerable players, especially children. **The baseline for monetisation in the EU.**
- `8` [New loot box principles agreed by industry](https://ukie.org.uk/news/new-loot-box-principles-agreed-by-industry) (Ukie, 2023): the UK games industry's 11 principles on paid loot boxes, developed in a working group convened by the UK government: parental consent for under-18s, disclosure before purchase and download, clear probability disclosure, lenient refunds and spending information for all players.
- `8` [PEGI expands age rating criteria with interactive risk categories](https://pegi.info/news/pegi-expands-age-rating-criteria-interactive-risk-categories) (PEGI, 2026): from June 2026, the European age ratings take account of in-game purchases, paid random items, rewards for returning to the game and unrestricted communication, not only content.
- `8` [Age appropriate design: a code of practice for online services](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/) (UK Information Commissioner's Office, Children's code): 15 standards for online services, including games and connected toys, that children are likely to use: high-privacy defaults, no detrimental use of data, no nudge techniques and parental controls that children know about.
- `8` [Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/): a developer-friendly reference, written by studios, specialists and academics, of ways to avoid excluding players with motor, cognitive, vision, hearing and speech impairments, sorted into basic, intermediate and advanced guidelines.
- `8` [Xbox Accessibility Guidelines](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/xbox/accessibility/guidelines) (Microsoft): detailed best practices for accessible games, developed with experts and disabled gamers, each with scoping questions, implementation guidance and examples, which test teams can also use as a checklist.
- `8` [IGDA Game Crediting Guide 10.1](https://igda.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/IGDA-Game-Crediting-Guidelines-10.1-March-2023.pdf) (International Game Developers Association, 2023): rules for who gets credited, what credit they get and how, including contractors, outsourced studios and people who left before launch.
- `7` [Disruption and Harms in Online Gaming Framework](https://thrivingingames.org/disruption-and-harms-in-gaming-framework/) (Fair Play Alliance, now Thriving in Games Group, with the ADL Center for Technology and Society, 2020): a shared catalogue of disruptive and harmful conduct in online games, with guides to community guidelines and to penalty and reporting systems.

## Key rules

Only rules the adopted references don't already cover, or choices they leave open.

### Monetisation and loot boxes

- `8` `GAME-1` **Tell players before they buy or download a game that it sells paid random items, such as loot boxes, card packs or gacha pulls, and show the odds of each item or category before every purchase, in plain words, next to the buy button.** The Apple and Google app stores require odds before purchase, and the UK industry principles add disclosure before download. Since June 2026, PEGI rates new games with paid random items PEGI 16 by default, and sometimes PEGI 18. See `HON-2`. ([Apple, App Review Guidelines 3.1.1](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#in-app-purchase), [Google Play, Payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738?hl=en), [Ukie, Loot box principles](https://ukie.org.uk/news/new-loot-box-principles-agreed-by-industry), [PEGI, Interactive risk categories](https://pegi.info/news/pegi-expands-age-rating-criteria-interactive-risk-categories))
- `8` `GAME-2` **Show the real-money price of everything that players buy with virtual currency, and sell currency in amounts that match what items cost, so that players aren't left with leftovers that push them to buy more.** EU consumer authorities name hiding the cost behind virtual currency, and bundles that don't match item prices, as practices to avoid, and the FTC lists virtual currency as a way of hiding the real cost. ([CPC Network, Key Principles on In-Game Virtual Currencies](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/8af13e88-6540-436c-b137-9853e7fe866a_en?filename=Key+principles+on+in-game+virtual+currencies.pdf), [FTC, Bringing Dark Patterns to Light](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P214800%20Dark%20Patterns%20Report%209.14.2022%20-%20FINAL.pdf))
- `8` `GAME-3` **Don't let children buy loot boxes or other paid random items without a parent's or carer's consent, and never urge children to buy or to ask their parents to.** The UK industry principles commit to controls that stop under-18s getting paid loot boxes without consent, and directly urging children to buy is one of the practices EU consumer law bans in all circumstances. See `RIGHTS-7`. ([Ukie, Loot box principles](https://ukie.org.uk/news/new-loot-box-principles-agreed-by-industry), [CPC Network, Key Principles, Principle 7](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/8af13e88-6540-436c-b137-9853e7fe866a_en?filename=Key+principles+on+in-game+virtual+currencies.pdf), [European Commission, Guidelines on the protection of minors](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors))
- `8` `GAME-4` **Ask for clear confirmation before every real-money purchase, give refunds when a child or someone else spent without permission, and never lock the accounts of players who dispute a charge.** In 2022 Epic Games agreed to pay $245 million in refunds after the FTC alleged that Fortnite's button layout led players into purchases with a single press and that Epic locked the accounts of customers who disputed unauthorised charges. See `RESP-6`. ([FTC, Epic Games settlement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/12/fortnite-video-game-maker-epic-games-pay-more-half-billion-dollars-over-ftc-allegations), [Ukie, Loot box principles](https://ukie.org.uk/news/new-loot-box-principles-agreed-by-industry))
- `7` `GAME-5` **Don't build the game's income on a few very high spenders: give every player spending limits and a spending history, and treat extreme spending as a warning sign, not a target.** EU consumer authorities consider such "whales" likely to be vulnerable, for example to impulse-control or gambling problems, and judge games that target them by a stricter standard. ([CPC Network, Key Principles, Principle 7](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/8af13e88-6540-436c-b137-9853e7fe866a_en?filename=Key+principles+on+in-game+virtual+currencies.pdf), [UK DCMS, Loot boxes: industry-led protections](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/loot-boxes-in-video-games-update-on-improvements-to-industry-led-protections))

### Children and safe play

- `9` `GAME-6` **If children may play, switch voice and text chat with strangers off by default, use high-privacy settings, and get a parent's consent before collecting a child's data where the law requires it.** The FTC alleged that Fortnite's on-by-default chat exposed children and teens to bullying, threats and harassment; Epic paid a $275 million penalty under the US children's privacy rule (COPPA) and must keep chat off by default for them. See `RIGHTS-7`. ([FTC, Epic Games settlement](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/12/fortnite-video-game-maker-epic-games-pay-more-half-billion-dollars-over-ftc-allegations), [ICO Children's code, 7. Default settings](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/7-default-settings/))
- `8` `GAME-7` **Give every player easy ways to mute, block and report others, publish clear rules of conduct, and act on reports quickly and consistently.** Since June 2026, PEGI rates games with entirely unrestricted communication, such as no blocking or reporting, PEGI 18. ([PEGI, Interactive risk categories](https://pegi.info/news/pegi-expands-age-rating-criteria-interactive-risk-categories), [Fair Play Alliance, Disruption and Harms in Online Gaming Framework](https://thrivingingames.org/disruption-and-harms-in-gaming-framework/))
- `8` `GAME-8` **As a player, treat other players as you would face to face: no harassment, hate, threats, or cheating and griefing that spoil other people's games.** Bullying on gaming platforms is still bullying, and it often goes hand in hand with bullying offline. See `HARM-2`. ([Fair Play Alliance, Disruption and Harms in Online Gaming Framework](https://thrivingingames.org/disruption-and-harms-in-gaming-framework/), [UNICEF, Cyberbullying: what is it and how to stop it](https://www.unicef.org/stories/how-to-stop-cyberbullying))

### Time and wellbeing

- `7` `GAME-9` **Don't punish players for taking a break: avoid streaks, timers and daily rewards that take away progress or items when people stop playing, and let players pause and save at any point.** Since June 2026, PEGI rates games that punish players for not returning at least PEGI 12, and the UK Children's code asks for pause features that don't lose progress and for neutral offers to keep playing. See `RESP-10`. ([PEGI, Interactive risk categories](https://pegi.info/news/pegi-expands-age-rating-criteria-interactive-risk-categories), [ICO Children's code, 5. Detrimental use of data](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/5-detrimental-use-of-data/))
- `6` `GAME-10` **Support healthy play: show players their playtime, offer reminders and limits, work with the platforms' parental controls, and tell players where to find help if gaming starts to harm their life.** The WHO recognises gaming disorder in ICD-11; it affects only a small proportion of players, but the WHO advises all players to watch how much time they spend. ([WHO, Addictive behaviours: Gaming disorder](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/addictive-behaviours-gaming-disorder), [PEGI, Parental control tools](https://pegi.info/parental-controls))

### Accessibility

- `8` `GAME-11` **Plan accessibility from the game design document onwards, starting with the basics: remappable controls, subtitles for all important speech, no information given by colour or sound alone, a choice of difficulty levels, and playtests with disabled players.** Retrofitting late costs far more, and the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities asks states to enable disabled people to take part in recreation and leisure on an equal basis. See `FAIR-1`. ([Game Accessibility Guidelines, Basic](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/basic/), [Game Accessibility Guidelines, Why and how](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/why-and-how/), [UN CRPD, Article 30](https://social.desa.un.org/issues/disability/crpd/article-30-participation-in-cultural-life-recreation-leisure-and-sport))

### People who make games

- `8` `GAME-12` **Plan schedules without crunch: don't treat long hours as a normal part of the job, and pay for overtime or give time off in return.** Everyone has the right to reasonable limits on working hours, yet in the IGDA's 2023 survey 28% of developers said their job involved crunch, a further 25% had periods of long hours, and a third got nothing extra for overtime. See `FAIR-10`. ([UN, Universal Declaration of Human Rights, Article 24](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights), [IGDA, Developer Satisfaction Survey 2023](https://igda-website.s3.us-east-2.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/01161842/2023-04-14_IGSA-DSS-2023_SummaryReport.pdf))
- `7` `GAME-13` **Credit everyone who worked on the game, including contractors, outsourced and localisation staff, QA testers and people who left before launch, and let each person choose whether to be named.** Don't give extra credit as a reward for crunch. See `HON-9`. ([IGDA Game Crediting Guide 10.1](https://igda.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/IGDA-Game-Crediting-Guidelines-10.1-March-2023.pdf))

## How to apply

Practices, methods and tools that help people follow the key rules.

- `7` [Planning a Penalty and Reporting System](https://thrivingingames.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/FPA-Framework-Planning-Penalty-Reporting-System.pdf) (Fair Play Alliance and ADL): how to plan reporting and penalties for a new or live game, and what they mean for players and the team. Supports `GAME-7`.
- `6` [Game Accessibility Guidelines, Full list](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/full-list/): every guideline in one categorised list, with a downloadable checklist to review a design and track progress. Supports `GAME-11`.
- `6` [PEGI, Parental control tools](https://pegi.info/parental-controls): what the parental controls on consoles, PCs and phones can do (age limits, spending, chat and playtime), so you can design your game to work with them and point parents to them. Supports `GAME-3` and `GAME-10`.

## Open questions

Questions on which serious traditions or experts disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [UK government response to the call for evidence on loot boxes in video games](https://www.gov.uk/government/calls-for-evidence/loot-boxes-in-video-games-call-for-evidence/outcome/government-response-to-the-call-for-evidence-on-loot-boxes-in-video-games) (DCMS, 2022): are paid loot boxes gambling? Belgium treats them as gambling because a prize there need not have money value, and Dutch law on them has been tested in court. The UK found an association between loot box spending and problem gambling but no proven causal link, decided that most loot boxes are not gambling under its law because their prizes can't be turned into money, and chose industry measures instead.
- `7` [APA Resolution on Violent Video Games](https://www.apa.org/about/policy/resolution-violent-video-games.pdf) (American Psychological Association, 2020): do violent games make players violent? Research finds a reliable link between violent games and aggressive behaviour, but researchers interpret its size very differently, there is too little research on serious violence, and the APA warns that blaming violence such as mass shootings on games is not scientifically sound.
