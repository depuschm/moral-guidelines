# Marketing and advertising

Scope: everyone who plans, makes, approves or publishes marketing communications, including advertisers, agencies, publishers, platforms and influencers, in any medium. It covers truthful and substantiated claims, making clear what is an ad, endorsements and reviews, environmental claims, children and vulnerable people, fair comparisons and pressure tactics. The general guidelines on [honesty and trust](../../general/honesty.md) and [respect, autonomy and consent](../../general/respect-and-consent.md) also apply. Deceptive interfaces, such as fake countdown timers and hard-to-cancel subscriptions, are covered in [Product and UX design](../design/README.md), the use of personal data for targeting in [Data and analytics](../data/README.md), and a company's wider duties to its customers in [Business and human rights](../business/README.md). This is moral guidance, not legal advice: advertising law differs between countries, so check what applies to you with a lawyer or your national advertising regulator.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [ICC Advertising and Marketing Communications Code](https://iccwbo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/ICC_2024_MarketingCode_2024.pdf) (International Chamber of Commerce, 11th edition, 2024): **the baseline**: the global self-regulatory code for marketing in every medium, on which national codes are built. It covers honesty, truthfulness, substantiation, identification of ads, comparisons, endorsements and influencers, children and teens, data-driven marketing and environmental claims.
- `8` [UK Code of Non-broadcast Advertising and Direct & Promotional Marketing](https://www.asa.org.uk/codes-and-rulings/advertising-codes/non-broadcast-code.html) (CAP Code, Committee of Advertising Practice): the UK rule book for non-broadcast ads, sales promotions and direct marketing, enforced by the Advertising Standards Authority (ASA), with detailed rules on misleading advertising, children, harm and offence, and environmental claims.
- `8` [Unfair Commercial Practices Directive](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/consumer-protection-law/unfair-commercial-practices-and-price-indication/unfair-commercial-practices-directive_en) (European Commission): the EU law against misleading and aggressive practices towards consumers, as amended in 2019 and by the Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition (ECGT) Directive (EU) 2024/825, which has applied since 27 September 2026. The Commission's 2021 guidance covers influencer marketing, reviews, environmental claims and dark patterns.
- `8` [FTC Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/part-255) (16 CFR Part 255, US Federal Trade Commission, revised 2023): when endorsements by consumers, experts, organisations and influencers mislead, and how to disclose a connection between an endorser and the brand.
- `7` [AMA Statement of Ethics](https://www.ama.org/ama-statement-of-ethics/) (American Marketing Association): the profession's statement of ethical norms (do no harm, foster integrity, embrace ethical values) and its five values of honesty, responsibility, equity, transparency and citizenship.
- `7` [Green Claims Code](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/green-claims-code-making-environmental-claims/environmental-claims-on-goods-and-services) (UK Competition and Markets Authority, 2021): six principles for environmental claims: truthful and accurate, clear and unambiguous, not omitting important information, fair comparisons, the full life cycle, and substantiated.
- `7` [Questions & Answers on the Empowering Consumers for the Green Transition Directive](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/3c257883-bb2a-4dd9-a6dc-501d587bb34f_en) (European Commission, September 2026): how the EU's new rules on generic environmental claims, sustainability labels, offsetting claims and claims about future performance apply.

## Key rules

### Truthful claims

- `9` `MKT-1` **Don't mislead: make sure the overall impression of every ad is true, including what it implies, what it leaves out and how it is presented.** An ad can mislead through exaggeration, ambiguity, omission or an altered image even when each statement is literally true; obvious exaggeration that nobody takes literally ("puffery") is allowed. ([ICC Code, Article 5](https://iccwbo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/ICC_2024_MarketingCode_2024.pdf), [CAP Code, rules 3.1 to 3.3](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html)) See `HON-2`.
- `8` `MKT-2` **Hold evidence for every claim that people are likely to take as objective before you publish it, at the level of proof the claim states or implies.** A claim such as "clinically proven" needs at least that level of evidence, and without adequate evidence regulators treat the claim as misleading. ([ICC Code, Article 6](https://iccwbo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/ICC_2024_MarketingCode_2024.pdf), [CAP Code, rule 3.7](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html))
- `7` `MKT-3` **Make comparisons fair: compare products that meet the same need, on material, relevant, verifiable and representative features, and don't denigrate competitors or create confusion with their brands.** ([CAP Code, rules 3.33 to 3.35 and 3.41](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/03.html), [ICC Code, Articles 14 and 17](https://iccwbo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/ICC_2024_MarketingCode_2024.pdf))

### Ads, endorsements and reviews

- `8` `MKT-6` **Use only genuine endorsements and reviews that reflect the honest opinion and real experience of the person giving them; never write, buy or fake reviews, and don't hide or suppress negative ones.** ([FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR 255.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.1), [FTC Rule on the Use of Consumer Reviews and Testimonials, 16 CFR Part 465](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/part-465), [ICC Code, Article 18](https://iccwbo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/ICC_2024_MarketingCode_2024.pdf))
- `7` `MKT-4` **Make every ad recognisable as an ad: label advertorials, sponsored content and paid influencer posts clearly and prominently, where people will notice the label straight away.** Don't disguise marketing as news, independent reviews, user content or private posts. ([ICC Code, Article 7](https://iccwbo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/ICC_2024_MarketingCode_2024.pdf), [CAP Code, rules 2.1 to 2.4](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/02.html)) See `HON-2`.
- `7` `MKT-5` **Disclose any connection between an endorser and the brand that the audience wouldn't expect, such as payment, free or discounted products, or a family, employment or business relationship, clearly and close to the endorsement.** A disclosure buried in a string of hashtags, behind "see more" or only in the terms and conditions isn't enough. ([FTC Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR 255.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/255.5), [ICC Code, Article 18](https://iccwbo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/ICC_2024_MarketingCode_2024.pdf))

### Environmental claims

- `7` `MKT-7` **Make environmental claims specific, accurate and backed by evidence, base them on the product's full life cycle, and don't hide significant harms or present as a benefit what the law requires anyway.** ([CMA Green Claims Code](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/green-claims-code-making-environmental-claims/environmental-claims-on-goods-and-services), [CAP Code, section 11](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/11.html), [ICC Code, Chapter D](https://iccwbo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/ICC_2024_MarketingCode_2024.pdf)) See `HON-2`.
- `7` `MKT-8` **Don't use vague green claims such as "eco-friendly", "green" or "sustainable" unless you can prove them without qualification, and don't use environmental labels or symbols that suggest a certification or official approval you don't have.** In the EU, generic environmental claims are banned unless the trader can show recognised excellent environmental performance, for example through the EU Ecolabel, and sustainability labels must be based on a certification scheme or set up by public authorities. ([ICC Code, Articles D2.2 and D7](https://iccwbo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/ICC_2024_MarketingCode_2024.pdf), [European Commission, ECGT Directive Q&A, questions 7 and 8](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/3c257883-bb2a-4dd9-a6dc-501d587bb34f_en))
- `7` `MKT-9` **Base climate claims on real reductions in emissions, and say clearly how much of a claim such as "carbon neutral" rests on offsetting.** In the EU, claims that a product has a neutral, reduced or positive climate impact because of offsetting outside its value chain have been banned since 27 September 2026. ([CMA Green Claims Code](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/green-claims-code-making-environmental-claims/environmental-claims-on-goods-and-services), [European Commission, ECGT Directive Q&A, question 10](https://commission.europa.eu/document/download/3c257883-bb2a-4dd9-a6dc-501d587bb34f_en))

### Children, vulnerable people and pressure

- `9` `MKT-10` **Take special care with children: don't exploit their credulity, inexperience or loyalty, don't urge them directly to buy or to get their parents to buy, and don't market products restricted by age, such as alcohol, gambling and tobacco, to minors.** ([ICC Code, Article 20 and Chapter E](https://iccwbo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/ICC_2024_MarketingCode_2024.pdf), [CAP Code, section 5](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/05.html)) See `FAIR-9`.
- `8` `MKT-11` **Don't target or exploit people made vulnerable by age, illness, disability, credulity or their circumstances, such as bereavement or losing a job, and judge your marketing by its likely effect on them, not only on the average consumer.** ([CMA, Unfair commercial practices guidance (CMA207): vulnerable consumers](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unfair-commercial-practices-cma207/unfair-commercial-practices#average-member-of-a-vulnerable-group-of-consumers), [AMA Statement of Ethics, Equity](https://www.ama.org/ama-statement-of-ethics/))
- `7` `MKT-12` **Don't play on fear or distress without a good reason, and don't use shocking claims or images just to get attention; where fear is justified, for example in a safety message, keep it in proportion.** ([CAP Code, rule 4.2](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/04.html), [ICC Code, Article 2](https://iccwbo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/ICC_2024_MarketingCode_2024.pdf)) See `RESP-10`.
- `7` `MKT-13` **Don't use high-pressure tactics, or techniques that take advantage of people's trust or limited understanding to push them into choices they wouldn't otherwise make.** Give people the information that matters to their decision at a time when they can still weigh it. ([ICC Code, Article 4](https://iccwbo.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/ICC_2024_MarketingCode_2024.pdf), [AMA Statement of Ethics, Honesty](https://www.ama.org/ama-statement-of-ethics/)) See `RESP-10`.

## How to apply

- `7` [Influencers' guide to making clear that ads are ads](https://www.asa.org.uk/resource/influencers-guide.html) (ASA and CAP, 4th edition): when influencer content counts as advertising and how to label it clearly, with visual examples and advice on affiliate marketing, developed with the CMA. Supports `MKT-4` and `MKT-5`.
- `7` [ICC Framework for Responsible Environmental Marketing Communications](https://iccwbo.org/news-publications/business-solutions/icc-framework-for-responsible-environmental-marketing-communications-2/) (5th edition, 2025): a practical resource, aligned with the 2024 ICC Code, with examples and guidance for making environmental claims that are truthful, clear and backed by reliable scientific evidence. Supports `MKT-7`, `MKT-8` and `MKT-9`.

## Open questions

Questions on which serious traditions or experts disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Business Ethics: 5.3 Advertising](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-business/#Adve): is persuasive advertising, as opposed to informative advertising, a form of manipulation? Critics argue that associative advertising creates desires by going around people's capacity for conscious choice and so undermines their autonomy; defenders reply that our desires are shaped by our surroundings anyway and that most people see through such attempts, while some argue that children and other vulnerable groups need special protection or should not be targeted at all.

## Tutorials and further reading

- `8` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, The Ethics of Manipulation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-manipulation/): what manipulation is, how it differs from persuasion, and why it can be wrong, including the debate on whether advertising is manipulative.
- `7` [Unfair commercial practices (CMA207)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unfair-commercial-practices-cma207/unfair-commercial-practices) (UK Competition and Markets Authority, updated 2025): guidance on the UK's rules on unfair commercial practices in the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, including the 32 practices that are banned in all circumstances, such as fake reviews and pressure selling.
