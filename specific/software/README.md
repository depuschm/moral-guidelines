# Software engineering

Scope: the duties of people who design, build, test, maintain and run software, whether as employees, contractors, open-source contributors, teachers or students. These guidelines adopt the profession's codes of ethics and add rules only where the codes need combining or making concrete. Ethics for AI systems are in [Artificial intelligence](../ai/README.md), collecting and analysing data about people in [Data and analytics](../data/README.md), finding and disclosing vulnerabilities in [Security research](../security/README.md), and deceptive patterns and inclusive design in [Product and UX design](../design/README.md). Speaking up at work is covered in [Workplace and leadership](../workplace/README.md), and owning mistakes and resisting pressure to do wrong in [Responsibility and accountability](../../general/responsibility.md).

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `10` [ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct](https://www.acm.org/code-of-ethics) (Association for Computing Machinery, 2018): general ethical principles (contribute to human well-being, avoid harm, be honest, be fair, respect the work of others, privacy and confidentiality), professional responsibilities (quality, competence, review, risk evaluation, authorised access, security) and leadership principles for everyone whose work involves computing. **Follow it as the baseline.**
- `9` [Software Engineering Code of Ethics and Professional Practice](https://www.computer.org/education/code-of-ethics) (IEEE Computer Society and ACM joint task force, 1999): eight principles with concrete clauses for software engineers, from approving software only when it is safe and tested to realistic estimates, documentation, fair management and lifelong learning. It makes the public interest central.
- `9` [IEEE Code of Ethics](https://ieee-cas.org/about/ieee-code-ethics) (IEEE, as published by the IEEE Circuits and Systems Society): the commitments of IEEE members: holding paramount the safety, health and welfare of the public, ethical design, privacy, honest claims and estimates, crediting others' contributions, technical competence, and treating all people fairly.
- `8` [NIST SP 800-218, Secure Software Development Framework (SSDF) Version 1.1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/218/final) (US National Institute of Standards and Technology, 2022): a core set of secure development practices to add to any development life cycle, to reduce vulnerabilities in released software and address their root causes.
- `8` [WCAG 2 Overview](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/) (W3C Web Accessibility Initiative): introduces the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines, the international standard for making web content accessible to people with disabilities. WCAG 2.2 is also the ISO/IEC 40500:2025 standard.

## Key rules

### The public good

- `9` `SWE-1` **Put the health, safety and welfare of the public first: when what a client, employer or user wants conflicts with it, the public comes first.** The codes accept duties to clients and employers only as far as they are consistent with the public interest. See `HARM-4`. ([Software Engineering Code, Principles 1 and 2](https://www.computer.org/education/code-of-ethics), [IEEE Code of Ethics](https://ieee-cas.org/about/ieee-code-ethics), [ACM Code, 1.1 and 3.1](https://www.acm.org/code-of-ethics))
- `8` `SWE-2` **Before you build or change a system, work out who it could affect and how, including people who aren't its users and those with the least power, and look again as it changes.** The ACM Code asks for thorough evaluations of systems and their impacts, including possible risks, and the Software Engineering Code asks engineers to consider how the least empowered will be affected. See `REASON-3`. ([ACM Code, 2.5](https://www.acm.org/code-of-ethics), [Software Engineering Code, preamble and 3.03](https://www.computer.org/education/code-of-ethics))
- `8` `SWE-3` **Raise concerns about software that could cause harm with the people responsible, take them to an appropriate authority if they don't act, and refuse to take part in work you believe breaks your profession's code.** Assess the situation carefully before you report, because poorly founded reports can also do harm. Managers must not punish anyone for raising ethical concerns ([Workplace and leadership](../workplace/README.md)). See `ACCT-9`. ([ACM Code, 1.2](https://www.acm.org/code-of-ethics), [Software Engineering Code, 1.04, 2.07, 5.11, 5.12, 6.10, 6.12 and 6.13](https://www.computer.org/education/code-of-ethics), [IEEE Code of Ethics](https://ieee-cas.org/about/ieee-code-ethics))

### Honesty, competence and credit

- `8` `SWE-4` **Be honest about what software can and can't do: describe its capabilities, limits, risks and test results accurately, and don't make claims you can't support.** The Software Engineering Code rules out not only false claims but also ones that are speculative, misleading or doubtful, and the ACM Code asks professionals to be transparent about a system's capabilities and limitations. See `HON-2`. ([ACM Code, 1.3](https://www.acm.org/code-of-ethics), [Software Engineering Code, 1.06 and 6.07](https://www.computer.org/education/code-of-ethics), [IEEE Code of Ethics](https://ieee-cas.org/about/ieee-code-ethics))
- `7` `SWE-5` **Give realistic estimates of cost, time and quality, with their uncertainty, and tell your client or employer early if a project is likely to fail, overrun or cause other problems.** See `HON-1`. ([Software Engineering Code, 2.06 and 3.09](https://www.computer.org/education/code-of-ethics), [IEEE Code of Ethics](https://ieee-cas.org/about/ieee-code-ethics))
- `7` `SWE-6` **Take on only work you are competent to do, or say plainly what you lack, and keep your knowledge up to date.** Where a task is outside your competence, ask someone who has it. ([ACM Code, 2.2 and 2.6](https://www.acm.org/code-of-ethics), [Software Engineering Code, 2.01, 3.04, 7.08 and Principle 8](https://www.computer.org/education/code-of-ethics), [IEEE Code of Ethics](https://ieee-cas.org/about/ieee-code-ethics))
- `7` `SWE-7` **Own your mistakes: find, fix and report errors in your work, and seek and accept honest review of it.** See `ACCT-2`. ([Software Engineering Code, 1.01 and 6.08](https://www.computer.org/education/code-of-ethics), [IEEE Code of Ethics](https://ieee-cas.org/about/ieee-code-ethics), [ACM Code, 2.4](https://www.acm.org/code-of-ethics))
- `7` `SWE-8` **Use code, data, models and tools only as their licences and owners allow, and credit the people whose work you build on.** Check the licence before you copy or depend on someone else's code. See `HON-9`. ([ACM Code, 1.5](https://www.acm.org/code-of-ethics), [Software Engineering Code, 2.02 and 7.03](https://www.computer.org/education/code-of-ethics), [IEEE Code of Ethics](https://ieee-cas.org/about/ieee-code-ethics))

### Quality, safety, security and access

- `9` `SWE-9` **Approve or release software only when you have good grounds to believe it is safe and adequately tested, and take special care with systems that people's lives, health or essential services depend on.** The Software Engineering Code adds that software should not diminish quality of life or privacy, or harm the environment. See `HARM-4`. ([Software Engineering Code, 1.03 and 3.10](https://www.computer.org/education/code-of-ethics), [ACM Code, 2.1 and 3.7](https://www.acm.org/code-of-ethics))
- `8` `SWE-10` **Build security and privacy in from the start: model the threats, collect and keep only the personal data the software needs, and make secure settings the default.** The ACM Code asks for systems that are robustly and usably secure, and the SSDF lists threat modelling and secure default settings among its practices. The rules for personal data are in [Data and analytics](../data/README.md). See `RESP-8`. ([ACM Code, 1.6 and 2.9](https://www.acm.org/code-of-ethics), [Software Engineering Code, 3.12](https://www.computer.org/education/code-of-ethics), [NIST SSDF, PW.1 and PW.9](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/218/final))
- `8` `SWE-11` **Make software accessible to people with disabilities, following WCAG for web content, and think about who else it could exclude, for example through cost or the devices it needs.** The ACM Code says that failing to design for inclusiveness and accessibility may be unfair discrimination. Inclusive design is covered in [Product and UX design](../design/README.md). See `FAIR-3`. ([ACM Code, 1.4](https://www.acm.org/code-of-ethics), [Software Engineering Code, 1.07](https://www.computer.org/education/code-of-ethics), [W3C, WCAG 2 Overview](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/))

## How to apply

- `7` [Consequence Scanning](https://www.adalovelaceinstitute.org/resource/doteveryone-consequence-scanning/) (Doteveryone, hosted by the Ada Lovelace Institute): a short team exercise, run when a product is conceived, during roadmap planning and when creating features, that asks what the intended and unintended consequences are and what to do about them. Supports `SWE-2` and `SWE-3`.
- `7` [Ethical Toolkit](https://www.scu.edu/ethics-in-technology-practice/ethical-toolkit/) (Markkula Center for Applied Ethics): seven tools for engineering teams, such as ethical risk sweeping, ethical pre-mortems and post-mortems, expanding the ethical circle and "think about the terrible people". Supports `SWE-2`, `SWE-3` and `SWE-9`.
- `6` [Threat Modeling Manifesto](https://www.threatmodelingmanifesto.org/): values and principles for threat modelling, built on four questions: what are we working on, what can go wrong, what are we going to do about it, and did we do a good enough job. Supports `SWE-10`.
- `5` [REUSE](https://reuse.software/) (Free Software Foundation Europe): recommendations and a tool for stating the licence and copyright of every file in a project clearly and in a machine-readable way. Supports `SWE-8`.

## Open questions

Questions on which serious traditions or experts disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `8` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Computing and Moral Responsibility](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/computing-responsibility/): who is responsible when complex software causes harm. Many people and organisations contribute to a system (the "problem of many hands"), and authors disagree on how to assign responsibility among them, whether computers can themselves be moral agents, and whether responsibility should be rethought as a shared practice.
- `7` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Intellectual Property: Justifications and Critiques](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/intellectual-property/#JustCrit): whether, and how far, exclusive rights over software and other intellectual works are justified. Personality-based, utilitarian and Lockean arguments support them, while critics argue that information is not property and that, because one person's use doesn't deprive another, it should be shared more freely.

## Tutorials and further reading

- `6` [Ethics in Technology Practice](https://www.scu.edu/ethics-in-technology-practice/) (Markkula Center for Applied Ethics): free materials for practising ethics in technology work: an overview, ethical lenses, a decision-making framework, case studies, a sample design workflow and workshop slides.
- `6` [Guide to the Software Engineering Body of Knowledge (SWEBOK), Version 4](https://www.computer.org/education/bodies-of-knowledge/software-engineering) (IEEE Computer Society): the generally accepted knowledge of software engineering, with chapters on software quality, software security and professional practice.
- `5` [The Open Source Definition](https://opensource.org/osd) (Open Source Initiative): the criteria a licence must meet to count as open source, useful for understanding the terms on which you may reuse and share open-source code.
