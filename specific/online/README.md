# Online life and social media

Scope: how individuals treat other people online, on social media, in messaging apps, forums, comment sections and games: what they share, how they speak to and about others, how they use other people's images, information and work, and what they do when they see harm. The general rules on [honesty](../../general/honesty.md), [harm and care](../../general/harm-and-care.md) and [respect, autonomy and consent](../../general/respect-and-consent.md) apply online as they do offline. Professional news reporting is covered in [Journalism](../journalism/README.md), how products and platforms are designed in [Product and UX design](../design/README.md), and conduct between players in [Games](../games/README.md). This is moral guidance, not legal advice: laws on harassment, intimate images and copyright differ between countries.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `10` [Universal Declaration of Human Rights](https://www.un.org/en/about-us/universal-declaration-of-human-rights) (United Nations, 1948): everyone's right to privacy and to protection from attacks on their honour and reputation (Article 12), and to freedom of opinion and expression (Article 19).
- `9` [General comment No. 25 (2021) on children's rights in relation to the digital environment](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/g21/053/43/pdf/g2105343.pdf) (UN Committee on the Rights of the Child): how the Convention on the Rights of the Child applies online, including children's privacy, their protection from violence and exploitation, and their access to information and expression.
- `8` [United Nations Global Principles for Information Integrity](https://www.un.org/en/information-integrity/global-principles) (United Nations, 2024): principles and recommendations for states, technology companies, media and others to make the information ecosystem more trustworthy, safe and free, including media and information literacy for the public.
- `7` [Cyberbullying: What is it and how to stop it](https://www.unicef.org/stories/how-to-stop-cyberbullying) (UNICEF): what cyberbullying is, its effects, and how to get help, report it and support a friend, with advice from UNICEF and from the major platforms.
- `7` [Online safety](https://ico.org.uk/for-the-public/online/social-networking) (UK Information Commissioner's Office): the regulator's advice to the public on social networking: thinking before you post, privacy settings, and getting consent before you upload other people's pictures or information.
- `7` [About CC Licenses](https://creativecommons.org/cc-licenses/) (Creative Commons): the six Creative Commons licences and what each allows others to do with a work, such as sharing, adapting and commercial use, always with credit to the creator.

## Key rules

Only rules the adopted references don't already cover, or choices they leave open.

### Sharing information and other people's work

- `7` `ONLINE-1` **Check before you share: stop, ask who is behind a claim and where they got it, read beyond the headline, and look for other trustworthy reports, especially when a post tells you what you want to hear.** Check that images and videos are genuine, for example with a reverse image search, before you pass them on as real. See `REASON-2`. ([WHO, Let's flatten the infodemic curve](https://www.who.int/news-room/spotlight/let-s-flatten-the-infodemic-curve), [Mike Caulfield, SIFT (The Four Moves)](https://hapgood.us/2019/06/19/sift-the-four-moves/))
- `7` `ONLINE-2` **Credit creators and respect their terms: don't post someone else's work as your own, follow the licence when there is one, such as a Creative Commons licence, and ask permission when there isn't.** Copyright protects most creative work automatically, without any notice or registration; exceptions such as fair use or fair dealing differ between countries. See `HON-9`. ([WIPO, Frequently Asked Questions: Copyright](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/copyright/faq-copyright), [Creative Commons, Recommended practices for attribution](https://wiki.creativecommons.org/Recommended_practices_for_attribution))

### Treating others

- `9` `ONLINE-3` **Don't harass, threaten, bully or impersonate anyone online, and don't attack people for who they are, such as their religion, ethnicity, nationality, gender or other identity.** The effects of online abuse can last a long time and affect people mentally, emotionally and physically, and in many countries, such as the UK, harassment and threats online can be crimes. See `HARM-2`. ([UNICEF, Cyberbullying: What is it and how to stop it](https://www.unicef.org/stories/how-to-stop-cyberbullying), [UN, What is hate speech?](https://www.un.org/en/hate-speech/understanding-hate-speech/what-is-hate-speech), [ICO, Online safety](https://ico.org.uk/for-the-public/online/social-networking))
- `8` `ONLINE-4` **Don't join pile-ons against a person, and never publish someone's home address, workplace, phone number or other personal details to expose them (doxing).** Doxing can lead to stalking, threats and physical violence, and coordinated waves of abuse are used to silence people, especially women activists. See `RESP-9`. ([UN Women, FAQs: Digital abuse, trolling, stalking, and other forms of technology-facilitated violence](https://www.unwomen.org/en/articles/faqs/digital-abuse-trolling-stalking-and-other-forms-of-technology-facilitated-violence-against-women), [UN Secretary-General, Report on violence against women and girls (A/77/302)](https://www.unwomen.org/sites/default/files/2022-10/A-77-302-SG-report-EVAWG-en.pdf))
- `7` `ONLINE-5` **When you see someone being abused online, report it, check in with the person targeted, and help them get support.** Listen to them, and respect their choice about whether to make a formal report. See `ACCT-11`. ([UNICEF, Cyberbullying: What is it and how to stop it](https://www.unicef.org/stories/how-to-stop-cyberbullying))

### Images and privacy

- `9` `ONLINE-6` **Never share, or threaten to share, intimate images of someone without their consent, and never make or share sexual deepfakes of real people.** It is a crime in many places, including England and Wales, where the offence also covers images that only appear to show the person, and the US, whose TAKE IT DOWN Act of 2025 covers AI-made "digital forgeries". See `RESP-5`. ([Sexual Offences Act 2003, section 66B](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2003/42/section/66B), [TAKE IT DOWN Act, Public Law 119-12](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ12/html/PLAW-119publ12.htm), [UN Women, FAQs: Digital abuse](https://www.unwomen.org/en/articles/faqs/digital-abuse-trolling-stalking-and-other-forms-of-technology-facilitated-violence-against-women))
- `7` `ONLINE-7` **Ask before you post photos, videos or personal details of other people, respect a no, and take special care with children, including your own.** The UK data protection regulator advises getting people's consent before uploading their pictures or information, and the UN Committee on the Rights of the Child names parents sharing photographs online as one of the threats to children's privacy. See `RESP-8`. ([ICO, Online safety](https://ico.org.uk/for-the-public/online/social-networking), [UN CRC, General comment No. 25, paragraph 67](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/g21/053/43/pdf/g2105343.pdf))
- `6` `ONLINE-8` **Protect your accounts: use a strong, separate password for your email, turn on two-step verification and keep your devices and apps updated.** Someone who takes over your email can use it to get into many of your other accounts. ([NCSC, Top tips for staying secure online](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/top-tips-for-staying-secure-online))

### Reporting harmful content

- `9` `ONLINE-9` **If you come across sexual images of children, don't save or send them to anyone, even to warn others: report the web address to the platform and to a hotline, such as the Internet Watch Foundation in the UK or another member of the INHOPE network.** Reporting is anonymous, and every share harms the child again. ([CEOP Education, What to do if you come across child sexual abuse material online](https://www.ceopeducation.co.uk/parents/articles/what-to-do-if-you-come-across-child-sexual-abuse-material/), [IWF, Report online child sexual abuse imagery](https://www.iwf.org.uk/en/uk-report/), [INHOPE, What is a hotline?](https://inhope.org/articles/what-is-a-hotline))
- `7` `ONLINE-10` **Report harmful content to the platform, and call the police if someone is in immediate danger.** Platforms often need someone to report abuse before they can find and remove it. See `ACCT-11`. ([UNICEF, Cyberbullying: What is it and how to stop it](https://www.unicef.org/stories/how-to-stop-cyberbullying), [Report Harmful Content](https://reportharmfulcontent.com/))

## How to apply

Practices, methods and tools that help people follow the key rules.

- `7` [StopNCII.org, How it works](https://stopncii.org/how-it-works/): for adults whose intimate images have been shared, or who are afraid they will be: it makes a digital fingerprint (hash) of the image on your own device, and participating platforms use it to find and remove copies, without the image leaving your device. Supports `ONLINE-6`.
- `7` [Take It Down](https://takeitdown.ncmec.org/) (National Center for Missing & Exploited Children): the same hashing approach for nude or sexual images taken of you when you were under 18, usable anonymously. Supports `ONLINE-6` and `ONLINE-9`.
- `6` [SIFT (The Four Moves)](https://hapgood.us/2019/06/19/sift-the-four-moves/) (Mike Caulfield): a quick routine for checking a claim before sharing it: stop, investigate the source, find better coverage, and trace claims to their original context. Supports `ONLINE-1`.
- `6` [Report Harmful Content](https://reportharmfulcontent.com/) (SWGfL): explains the major platforms' community standards and links straight to their reporting tools. Supports `ONLINE-3` and `ONLINE-10`.

## Open questions

Questions on which serious traditions or experts disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `8` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Freedom of Speech](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/freedom-speech/): how should free speech be balanced against protection from harm online, and what duties do platforms have? One view holds that platforms are private and may host or remove whatever speech they choose; another treats them as public forums that must respect users' free speech. Philosophers also disagree on whether platforms have a duty to restrict or stop amplifying harmful speech, and whether the law should enforce it.
- `7` [Abuse and anonymity](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/639730d78fa8f553092e67d0/Report_into_the_Connection_between_Abuse_and_Anonymity.pdf) (Revealing Reality for the UK Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport, 2022): should people be allowed to stay anonymous online? Some argue that anonymity creates the conditions for abuse and call for more identification and verification; others point to the privacy, safety and freedom of expression it protects, for example for people at risk, and to evidence that removing it would not reduce abuse.

## Tutorials and further reading

- `6` [Media and Information Literacy](https://www.unesco.org/en/media-information-literacy) (UNESCO): UNESCO's work on the skills people need to deal with mis- and disinformation, hate speech, falling trust in media and the effects of AI, with a guide for families.
- `5` [Misinformation](https://saferinternet.org.uk/online-issue/misinformation) (UK Safer Internet Centre): advice for parents, carers and teachers on helping young people think critically, check before they share and report harmful content.
