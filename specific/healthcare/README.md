# Healthcare

Scope: doctors, nurses, midwives and other health professionals in their care of patients, and the organisations they work for. Research with patients and volunteers is covered in [Research with people and animals](../research/README.md), and health data in [Data and analytics](../data/README.md). The general guidelines on [respect, autonomy and consent](../../general/respect-and-consent.md), [harm and care](../../general/harm-and-care.md), [honesty and trust](../../general/honesty.md) and [fairness and justice](../../general/fairness.md) matter most here. Professional regulators and national laws set binding standards that differ between countries; check yours. This is moral guidance, not legal or medical advice.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `10` [WMA International Code of Medical Ethics](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-international-code-of-medical-ethics/) (World Medical Association, revised 2022): **the baseline for physicians**: their duties to patients, to colleagues and other health professionals, to society and to the profession. The WMA encourages everyone involved in healthcare to adopt its principles.
- `10` [The ICN Code of Ethics for Nurses](https://www.icn.ch/sites/default/files/2024-12/ICN_Code-of-Ethics_EN_Web.pdf) (International Council of Nurses, revised 2021): **the baseline for nurses**: their responsibilities to patients, in practice, to the profession and to global health, with examples of how nurses and nurse managers, educators and researchers, and national nurses' associations can apply each element.
- `9` [WMA Declaration of Geneva: The Physician's Pledge](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-geneva/) (World Medical Association, revised 2017): the modern form of the physician's oath: a short pledge that makes the health and well-being of the patient the physician's first consideration.
- `9` [Universal Declaration on Bioethics and Human Rights](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/universal-declaration-bioethics-and-human-rights) (UNESCO, 2005): the international instrument on the ethical issues of medicine, the life sciences and related technologies as applied to people, including human dignity, benefit and harm, autonomy, consent, privacy, equality and non-discrimination.
- `8` [AMA Code of Medical Ethics](https://code-medical-ethics.ama-assn.org/) (American Medical Association): the Principles of Medical Ethics and detailed opinions on specific situations, such as disclosing errors and gifts from industry. Written for physicians in the United States.
- `7` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Bioethics and Moral Reasoning, 1.2 The principles-based approach](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bioethics-moral-reasoning/#PrinBaseAppr): an overview of the four principles of biomedical ethics set out by Beauchamp and Childress: respect for autonomy, non-maleficence (not harming), beneficence (doing good) and justice, and how they are specified and balanced against each other.

## Key rules

### Care, consent and confidentiality

- `10` `MED-1` **Get the patient's voluntary informed consent before any examination or treatment, and respect their decision to refuse or withdraw it, even when you disagree.** Give them, in a way they can understand, the information they need to make their own decision; if their capacity to decide is limited, involve them as much as possible and work with their trusted representative to follow their known wishes, and in an emergency, when no one can decide for them, act in their best interests. See `RESP-6`. ([WMA, International Code of Medical Ethics, paragraphs 13–18](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-international-code-of-medical-ethics/), [UNESCO, Universal Declaration on Bioethics and Human Rights, Article 6](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/universal-declaration-bioethics-and-human-rights), [ICN Code of Ethics for Nurses, 1.3](https://www.icn.ch/sites/default/files/2024-12/ICN_Code-of-Ethics_EN_Web.pdf))
- `9` `MED-2` **Make the health and well-being of the patient your first consideration, and give care in their best interests, not in your own, your employer's or anyone else's.** Weigh the intended benefit of each intervention against its possible harm, and don't let instructions that go against medical considerations change your professional judgement. See `HARM-11`. ([WMA, Declaration of Geneva](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-geneva/), [WMA, International Code of Medical Ethics, paragraphs 1, 6 and 14](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-international-code-of-medical-ethics/), [AMA, Principles of Medical Ethics, VIII](https://code-medical-ethics.ama-assn.org/principles))
- `9` `MED-3` **Keep what patients tell you and what you learn about them confidential, even after they have died.** Share information with others involved in their care only as far as their care needs, and disclose it without consent only in exceptional cases, when an overriding ethical duty leaves no other option, and then only the minimum necessary. See `RESP-9`. ([WMA, International Code of Medical Ethics, paragraphs 7 and 22](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-international-code-of-medical-ethics/), [WMA, Declaration of Geneva](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-geneva/), [ICN Code of Ethics for Nurses, 1.4](https://www.icn.ch/sites/default/files/2024-12/ICN_Code-of-Ethics_EN_Web.pdf), [UNESCO, Universal Declaration on Bioethics and Human Rights, Article 9](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/universal-declaration-bioethics-and-human-rights))
- `9` `MED-4` **Base care on the patient's health needs alone: never let age, disability, ethnic origin, gender, nationality, political views, race, religion, sexual orientation, social standing or any other factor come between you and your duty to the patient.** See `FAIR-1`. ([WMA, Declaration of Geneva](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-geneva/), [WMA, International Code of Medical Ethics, paragraph 2](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-international-code-of-medical-ethics/), [UNESCO, Universal Declaration on Bioethics and Human Rights, Articles 10 and 11](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/universal-declaration-bioethics-and-human-rights))

### Honesty and professional conduct

- `9` `MED-5` **Keep professional boundaries with patients: never exploit their trust or vulnerability, never behave abusively towards them, and never have a sexual relationship with a current patient.** ([WMA, International Code of Medical Ethics, paragraph 27](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-international-code-of-medical-ethics/), [ICN Code of Ethics for Nurses, 2.5](https://www.icn.ch/sites/default/files/2024-12/ICN_Code-of-Ethics_EN_Web.pdf))
- `8` `MED-6` **Be open and honest with patients when something goes wrong in their care: tell them promptly, apologise, explain the short- and long-term effects, and offer to put matters right where you can.** An apology is not an admission of legal liability; in England, health and care providers also have a statutory duty of candour under Regulation 20 of the Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014. See `ACCT-2`. ([GMC and NMC, Openness and honesty when things go wrong: the professional duty of candour](https://www.nmc.org.uk/globalassets/sitedocuments/nmc-publications/openness-and-honesty-when-things-go-wrong-the-professional-duty-of-candour-1224-2015.pdf), [AMA, Opinion 8.6 Promoting patient safety](https://code-medical-ethics.ama-assn.org/ethics-opinions/promoting-patient-safety), [CQC, Regulation 20: Duty of candour](https://www.cqc.org.uk/guidance-regulation/providers/regulations-service-providers-and-managers/health-social-care-act/regulation-20))
- `8` `MED-7` **Don't let gifts, payments or other benefits from drug and device companies influence your clinical judgement: decline cash and any gift meant to influence prescribing or referrals, and declare and manage any conflict of interest you can't avoid.** Gifts carry a risk of subtly biasing your judgement, or seeming to. See `FAIR-5`. ([WMA, International Code of Medical Ethics, paragraphs 5 and 25](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-international-code-of-medical-ethics/), [WMA, Statement concerning the relationship between physicians and commercial enterprises](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-statement-concerning-the-relationship-between-physicians-and-commercial-enterprises/), [AMA, Opinion 9.6.2 Gifts to physicians from industry](https://code-medical-ethics.ama-assn.org/ethics-opinions/gifts-physicians-industry))
- `8` `MED-8` **Practise only within your competence, keep your knowledge and skills up to date throughout your career, and refer the patient to a qualified colleague when their care is beyond you.** ([WMA, International Code of Medical Ethics, paragraphs 11 and 20](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-international-code-of-medical-ethics/), [ICN Code of Ethics for Nurses, 2.1 and 2.3](https://www.icn.ch/sites/default/files/2024-12/ICN_Code-of-Ethics_EN_Web.pdf))
- `6` `MED-9` **Look after your own health and well-being so that you can practise safely, and get help when you are not fit to practise.** ([WMA, Declaration of Geneva](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-geneva/), [WMA, International Code of Medical Ethics, paragraph 28](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-international-code-of-medical-ethics/), [ICN Code of Ethics for Nurses, 2.2 and 2.4](https://www.icn.ch/sites/default/files/2024-12/ICN_Code-of-Ethics_EN_Web.pdf))

### Human rights and patient safety

- `10` `MED-10` **Never take part in or help with torture or other cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment, and never use your medical knowledge to violate human rights, even under threat.** See `RIGHTS-1`. ([WMA, International Code of Medical Ethics, paragraph 10](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-international-code-of-medical-ethics/), [WMA, Declaration of Geneva](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-declaration-of-geneva/), [UN, ICCPR Article 7](https://treaties.un.org/doc/Publication/UNTS/Volume%20999/volume-999-I-14668-English.pdf#page=5))
- `8` `MED-11` **Speak up when patient safety is at risk, whether from a colleague's conduct, a policy, the working conditions or a misuse of technology, and report it to the people who can act.** See `ACCT-9`. ([WMA, International Code of Medical Ethics, paragraphs 32 and 37](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-international-code-of-medical-ethics/), [ICN Code of Ethics for Nurses, 2.10 and 2.11](https://www.icn.ch/sites/default/files/2024-12/ICN_Code-of-Ethics_EN_Web.pdf), [GMC and NMC, Openness and honesty when things go wrong](https://www.nmc.org.uk/globalassets/sitedocuments/nmc-publications/openness-and-honesty-when-things-go-wrong-the-professional-duty-of-candour-1224-2015.pdf))

## How to apply

Practices, methods and tools that help people follow the key rules.

- `7` [Shared decision making, NICE guideline NG197](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng197) (National Institute for Health and Care Excellence, 2021): how professionals and patients make decisions together, including how to communicate risks, benefits and consequences and how to use decision aids. Supports `MED-1`.
- `6` [The Caldicott Principles](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-caldicott-principles) (UK National Data Guardian): eight principles for using and sharing confidential information in health and social care, starting with justifying the purpose of each use. Supports `MED-3`.
- `6` [Open Payments](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/key-initiatives/open-payments) (US Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services): a public database of the payments that drug and medical device companies make to physicians and other health professionals in the United States, so that patients and others can see these financial relationships. Supports `MED-7`.

## Open questions

Questions on which serious traditions or experts disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `8` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Voluntary Euthanasia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/euthanasia-voluntary/): may a doctor end a patient's life, or help them end it, at their competent, voluntary and lasting request? Supporters appeal to respect for a person's own choices and to the relief of suffering that can't otherwise be relieved; opponents argue that intentionally ending a life is wrong, that a request may not be truly voluntary or lasting, that palliative care is the better answer, or that allowing it leads down a slippery slope. The [WMA is firmly opposed](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/declaration-on-euthanasia-and-physician-assisted-suicide/) to euthanasia and physician-assisted suicide, and the law differs between countries.
- `7` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Conscience, 6.1 Freedom of conscience and conscientious objection today](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/conscience/#FreeConsConsObjeToda): may health professionals refuse to provide lawful treatments they object to on grounds of conscience, such as abortion? Some argue that professional duties come first and objection shouldn't be allowed; others that conscience should be protected as far as possible without endangering patients' health; others accept objection only under conditions, such as giving reasons. The [WMA's code](https://www.wma.net/policies-post/wma-international-code-of-medical-ethics/) (paragraph 29) allows it only if the patient is not harmed or discriminated against and is told at once of their right to see another physician, and the ICN code requires nurses who object to make sure people still get the care they need.
- `7` [WHO, COVID-19: Ethics, resource allocation and priority setting](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/coronavirus-disease-covid-19-ethics-resource-allocation-and-priority-setting): how should scarce resources, such as ventilators or vaccines in a pandemic, be shared? The principles WHO describes are treating everyone's interests equally (first come, first served, or a lottery), achieving the best outcomes (such as saving the most lives), giving priority to the worst off, and giving priority to those who help others, such as health workers; WHO notes that different principles may fit different resources and levels of scarcity. The [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Justice and Access to Health Care](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/justice-healthcareaccess/) covers the wider debate about what care a society owes everyone.

## Tutorials and further reading

- `6` [WMA Medical Ethics Manual](https://www.wma.net/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/Ethics_manual_3rd_Nov2015_en.pdf) (World Medical Association, 3rd edition, 2015): an introduction to medical ethics for students and doctors, with chapters on physicians' relationships with patients, society and colleagues, and on medical research.
- `6` [Gillon, Medical ethics: four principles plus attention to scope](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2540719/) (BMJ, 1994): a short introduction to using the four principles, and to deciding whom they apply to, in clinical decisions.
