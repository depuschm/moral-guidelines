# Business and human rights

Scope: what companies, and the people who run and work for them, owe to the people their business affects: respecting human rights across the value chain, putting harm right, refusing bribery and other corruption, keeping honest accounts, paying tax, and dealing fairly with customers, suppliers and competitors. The general guidelines on [human rights](../../general/human-rights.md), [fairness and justice](../../general/fairness.md) and [honesty and trust](../../general/honesty.md) also apply. A company's duties to its own staff are in [Workplace and leadership](../workplace/README.md), honest advertising is in [Marketing and advertising](../marketing/README.md), and the duties of the public officials that companies deal with are in [Public service](../public-service/README.md). This is moral guidance, not legal advice: anti-bribery, accounting, tax and due diligence laws differ between countries, so check what applies to you with a lawyer.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/g11/121/90/pdf/g1112190.pdf) (UN Human Rights Council, 2011, annex to document A/HRC/17/31): **the baseline**: the corporate responsibility to respect human rights, human rights due diligence and access to remedy, with a commentary on each principle.
- `9` [OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises on Responsible Business Conduct](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0144) (2023 update, part of the OECD Declaration on International Investment and Multinational Enterprises): government-backed recommendations to companies on disclosure, human rights, employment, the environment, bribery, consumer interests, competition and taxation. National Contact Points in the adhering countries promote them and help put them into practice.
- `9` [United Nations Convention against Corruption](https://www.unodc.org/documents/treaties/UNCAC/Publications/Convention/08-50026_E.pdf) (UNCAC, UN Office on Drugs and Crime): the global anti-corruption treaty. Article 12 covers the private sector, including books and records, and Articles 15, 16 and 21 define bribery of national and foreign public officials and bribery in the private sector.
- `8` [OECD Convention on Combating Bribery of Foreign Public Officials in International Business Transactions](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0293) (OECD Anti-Bribery Convention, 1997): requires its parties to make bribing a foreign public official a crime and to ban the false accounting used to hide it. The [2021 Anti-Bribery Recommendation](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0378) adds guidance on facilitation payments, company compliance programmes and protecting people who report.
- `8` [OECD Due Diligence Guidance for Responsible Business Conduct](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0443) (2018): the six-step due diligence framework that puts the OECD Guidelines and the UN Guiding Principles into practice, from embedding responsible business conduct in policies to providing for remediation.
- `8` [The Ten Principles of the UN Global Compact](https://unglobalcompact.org/what-is-gc/mission/principles): ten commitments on human rights, labour, the environment and anti-corruption, drawn from the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, the ILO Declaration, the Rio Declaration and UNCAC, which participating companies sign up to.
- `7` [Corporate sustainability due diligence](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/business-and-industry/company-law-and-corporate-governance/corporate-sustainability-due-diligence_en) (European Commission): the EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (EU) 2024/1760, as amended by the Omnibus I simplification package (Directives (EU) 2025/794 and 2026/470). It will require EU companies with at least 5,000 employees and EUR 1.5 billion net worldwide turnover, and non-EU companies with that turnover in the EU, to carry out human rights and environmental due diligence, with national rules applying from 26 July 2029.

## Key rules

### Human rights due diligence

- `9` `BIZ-1` **Respect human rights wherever you operate: avoid causing or contributing to harm to people's rights through your own activities, and seek to prevent or reduce harm linked to your operations, products or services through your business relationships, even where you didn't contribute to it.** This responsibility exists over and above compliance with national law, and good works elsewhere don't make up for failing to respect rights. ([UN Guiding Principles 11 and 13](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/g11/121/90/pdf/g1112190.pdf), [OECD Guidelines, Chapter IV](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0144)) See `RIGHTS-12`.
- `8` `BIZ-2` **Carry out human rights due diligence across your value chain and keep it going: identify and assess actual and potential harms, act on what you find, check whether your response works, and report how you deal with them.** Start before a new activity, contract or acquisition, consult the people who may be affected, and where you can't cover every supplier at once, begin where harm would be most severe: greatest in scale and scope, or hardest to put right. ([UN Guiding Principles 14, 17 to 21 and 24](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/g11/121/90/pdf/g1112190.pdf), [OECD Due Diligence Recommendation, six steps](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0443))
- `7` `BIZ-3` **If a business partner is causing harm, use your leverage to make it stop before you walk away, and decide whether to end the relationship only after weighing the harm that ending it could itself cause.** The more severe the abuse, the sooner you need to see change; while you stay, be able to show what you are doing to reduce the harm. ([UN Guiding Principle 19, commentary](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/g11/121/90/pdf/g1112190.pdf))

### Remedy and grievances

- `9` `BIZ-4` **Where you have caused or contributed to harm, provide for or cooperate in putting it right through legitimate processes.** Remedy can include apologies, restitution, rehabilitation, compensation and guarantees that the harm won't happen again. ([UN Guiding Principles 22 and 25](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/g11/121/90/pdf/g1112190.pdf), [OECD Guidelines, Chapter IV](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0144)) See `ACCT-4`.
- `8` `BIZ-5` **Never retaliate against workers, their representatives or anyone else who investigates or raises concerns about your operations, and take steps to stop your business partners from doing so.** Help create an environment in which people feel safe to raise concerns. ([OECD Guidelines, Chapter II, A.9 and A.10](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0144)) See `ACCT-10`.
- `7` `BIZ-6` **Set up, or take part in, a grievance mechanism that people affected by your operations can use early and directly, and make it legitimate, accessible, predictable, equitable, transparent, compatible with human rights, a source of learning, and based on dialogue.** Don't use it to block access to the courts or to undermine trade unions, and aim for solutions agreed through dialogue: a company can't fairly judge complaints against itself, so where a decision is needed, leave it to an independent third party. ([UN Guiding Principles 29 and 31](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/g11/121/90/pdf/g1112190.pdf))

### Bribery, accounts and tax

- `9` `BIZ-7` **Don't offer, promise or give a bribe, and don't ask for or accept one, whether from public officials or from people in other companies, and don't use agents, consultants, suppliers or joint venture partners to pay one for you.** A bribe is any undue advantage, in money or in kind, offered so that someone misuses their position. ([OECD Guidelines, Chapter VII.1](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0144), [UNCAC Articles 15, 16 and 21](https://www.unodc.org/documents/treaties/UNCAC/Publications/Convention/08-50026_E.pdf), [UN Global Compact, Principle 10](https://unglobalcompact.org/what-is-gc/mission/principles)) See `FAIR-6`.
- `8` `BIZ-8` **Keep complete and accurate books and records: no off-the-books accounts, unrecorded or disguised transactions, made-up expenses or false documents, and don't destroy records before the law allows.** These are the usual ways of hiding bribery and fraud, which is why anti-corruption treaties require countries to ban them. ([UNCAC Article 12(3)](https://www.unodc.org/documents/treaties/UNCAC/Publications/Convention/08-50026_E.pdf), [OECD Anti-Bribery Convention, Article 8](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0293)) See `HON-2`.
- `8` `BIZ-9` **Don't evade tax: follow both the letter and the spirit of the tax laws in every country where you operate, pay on time, give the tax authorities the information they need, and set transfer prices at arm's length.** Following the spirit means following what the legislature intended; it doesn't mean paying more than the law requires. ([OECD Guidelines, Chapter XI](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0144)) See `FAIR-11`.
- `7` `BIZ-10` **Don't make facilitation payments, the small payments demanded to speed up routine government actions such as customs clearance or permits, and record accurately any payment you are forced to make to protect someone's life, safety or liberty.** The OECD notes that such payments are generally illegal where they are made and asks companies to prohibit or discourage them, and some laws, such as the UK Bribery Act 2010, make no exception for them. ([OECD Guidelines, Chapter VII.3](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0144), [OECD Anti-Bribery Recommendation, XIV](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0378), [UK Ministry of Justice, Bribery Act 2010 guidance, paragraphs 44 to 48](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5d80cfc3ed915d51e9aff85a/bribery-act-2010-guidance.pdf))
- `7` `BIZ-11` **Report honestly to investors and the public: disclose timely, reliable, clear, complete and accurate information on your financial results and on how you deal with your impacts on people and the environment.** If your operations risk severe human rights impacts, report on them formally, with enough detail for others to judge whether your response is adequate. ([OECD Guidelines, Chapter III](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0144), [UN Guiding Principle 21](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/g11/121/90/pdf/g1112190.pdf)) See `HON-2`.

### Fair dealing

- `8` `BIZ-12` **Deal honestly with customers: give them accurate, clear information that lets them make informed choices, make products that are safe in foreseeable use, protect their data, and offer fair, easy ways to complain and get redress.** Take extra care with customers who may be vulnerable. The rules for advertising are in [Marketing and advertising](../marketing/README.md). ([OECD Guidelines, Chapter VIII](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0144))
- `7` `BIZ-13` **Deal fairly with suppliers: agree fair payment terms and pay in full and on time, plan orders with them, avoid last-minute changes, and pay prices that cover the cost of decent wages and working conditions.** Prices below the cost of production, short-term planning, last-minute changes and late payments destabilise suppliers and lead to problems with overtime and wages for their workers. ([Common Framework for Responsible Purchasing Practices, Principles 3 to 5](https://www.cfrpp.org/s/CFRPP-full-Framework.pdf))
- `7` `BIZ-14` **Compete fairly: never agree with competitors to fix prices, rig bids, limit output or share out markets, customers or suppliers, and cooperate with competition authorities when they investigate.** ([OECD Guidelines, Chapter X](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0144))

## How to apply

- `7` [An Anti-Corruption Ethics and Compliance Programme for Business: A Practical Guide](https://www.unodc.org/documents/corruption/Publications/2013/13-84498_Ebook.pdf) (UNODC, 2013): practical guidance on building an anti-corruption programme: risk assessment, support from senior management, a clear policy, policies for risk areas such as gifts and hospitality, applying the programme to business partners, internal controls and record keeping, training, and channels for seeking advice and reporting violations. Supports `BIZ-7`, `BIZ-8` and `BIZ-10`.
- `6` [UN Guiding Principles Reporting Framework](https://www.ungpreporting.org/) (Shift and Mazars): comprehensive guidance, with implementation and assurance guidance, for companies reporting on how they respect human rights in practice. Supports `BIZ-2` and `BIZ-11`.
- `6` [Common Framework for Responsible Purchasing Practices](https://www.cfrpp.org/s/CFRPP-full-Framework.pdf) (Ethical Trading Initiative, Ethical Trade Norway, Fair Wear and the German Partnership for Sustainable Textiles, 2022): five principles for buyers, from integrating responsible purchasing into the business to fair payment terms and sustainable costing, with practices for each. Supports `BIZ-3` and `BIZ-13`.

## Open questions

Questions on which serious traditions or experts disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Business Ethics: 3.1 Ends: shareholder primacy or stakeholder balance?](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-business/#EndsSharPrimStakBala): should a company be run in the interests of its shareholders, or balance the interests of everyone with a stake in it? Defenders of shareholder primacy appeal to managers' duties to the owners and to efficiency, while stakeholder theorists argue that employees, customers, suppliers and communities also have claims on what the company does with its surplus; critics of stakeholder theory ask who counts as a stakeholder and what balancing their interests would mean.
- `5` [Knuutinen, Corporate Social Responsibility, Taxation and Aggressive Tax Planning](https://reference-global.com/article/10.1515/ntaxj-2014-0003) (Nordic Tax Journal, 2014, open access): is legal but aggressive tax avoidance wrong? Some see tax as a cost that a company may lawfully keep as low as possible; others see it as a contribution to the society in which the company does business, and hold that planning which meets the letter of the law but not the reasonable expectations of stakeholders is irresponsible.

## Tutorials and further reading

- `8` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Business Ethics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/ethics-business/): the main debates in business ethics, from the purpose of the firm and corporate governance to marketing, employment, whistleblowing and corporate social responsibility.
- `8` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Corruption](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/corruption/): what corruption is, how personal and institutional corruption differ, theories of political corruption, and the integrity systems that institutions use against it.
- `6` [UNODC Education for Justice, Anti-Corruption Module 5: Private Sector Corruption](https://www.unodc.org/e4j/en/anti-corruption/module-5/index.html): a university teaching module on the forms, consequences and causes of corruption in business, and on how companies and governments can prevent it.
