# Public service

Scope: people who hold public office or work for the state: elected and appointed office-holders, civil servants, local government staff, the police, regulators and people delivering public services under contract. It covers acting in the public interest, impartiality and merit, conflicts of interest, gifts, leaving office, openness, accountability and reporting wrongdoing. The general guidelines on [fairness and justice](../../general/fairness.md), [honesty and trust](../../general/honesty.md) and [responsibility and accountability](../../general/responsibility.md) also apply. Bribery and corruption seen from the side of companies are covered in [Business and human rights](../business/README.md), and fair treatment of staff in [Workplace and leadership](../workplace/README.md). This is moral guidance, not legal advice: the rules on declarations, gifts and post-office employment differ between countries and between public bodies, so follow your own code of conduct and ask your ethics adviser when in doubt.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `9` [The Seven Principles of Public Life](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-7-principles-of-public-life/the-7-principles-of-public-life--2) (UK Committee on Standards in Public Life, 1995, now kept by the Ethics and Integrity Commission; known as the Nolan Principles): **the baseline**: selflessness, integrity, objectivity, accountability, openness, honesty and leadership, for everyone who holds public office or delivers public services.
- `9` [OECD Recommendation of the Council on Public Integrity](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0435) (2017): thirteen principles for a public integrity system, from commitment at the top and high standards of conduct to merit-based appointment, an open culture, protection for people who report, oversight and transparency.
- `9` [United Nations Convention against Corruption](https://www.unodc.org/documents/treaties/UNCAC/Publications/Convention/08-50026_E.pdf) (UNCAC, UN Office on Drugs and Crime): the global anti-corruption treaty. Articles 7 to 10 cover merit in the public sector, codes of conduct and declarations of interests, public procurement and public reporting, and Articles 15 to 19 define bribery, embezzlement, trading in influence and abuse of functions.
- `8` [International Code of Conduct for Public Officials](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/n97/762/73/pdf/n9776273.pdf) (UN General Assembly resolution 51/59, 1996): the UN's short code on loyalty to the public interest, impartiality, conflicts of interest, declaring assets, gifts, confidential information and political activity.
- `7` [Model Code of Conduct for Public Officials](https://www.jus.uio.no/english/services/library/treaties/04/4-04/public-officials.html) (Council of Europe, Recommendation No. R (2000) 10, text at the University of Oslo treaty library): a detailed model code for national codes, with articles on conflicts of interest, declarations, gifts, how to react to improper offers, reporting, information and leaving public service.
- `7` [The Civil Service Code](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/civil-service-code/the-civil-service-code) (UK): the core values of integrity, honesty, objectivity and impartiality for UK civil servants, with what each requires and how to raise a concern.
- `7` [OECD Recommendation of the Council on Transparency and Integrity in Lobbying and Influence](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0379) (revised 2024): transparency of lobbying and influence, an integrity framework for public officials who are lobbied, and the management of moves into and out of public office, including cooling-off periods.

## Key rules

### Public interest and impartiality

- `9` `PUB-1` **Act solely in the public interest: never use your office, its powers, public money, property or official information for your own benefit or that of your family, friends, party or business associates.** ([Nolan Principles: Selflessness](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-7-principles-of-public-life/the-7-principles-of-public-life--2#selflessness) and [Integrity](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-7-principles-of-public-life/the-7-principles-of-public-life--2#integrity), [UN Code of Conduct, paragraphs 1, 4 and 6](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/n97/762/73/pdf/n9776273.pdf), [UNCAC Articles 17 and 19](https://www.unodc.org/documents/treaties/UNCAC/Publications/Convention/08-50026_E.pdf)) See `FAIR-5`.
- `9` `PUB-2` **Decide impartially, on the merits and the best evidence: treat everyone fairly, give no undue preference to any person or group, and never discriminate.** ([Nolan Principles: Objectivity](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-7-principles-of-public-life/the-7-principles-of-public-life--2#objectivity), [UN Code of Conduct, paragraph 3](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/n97/762/73/pdf/n9776273.pdf), [Council of Europe Model Code, Articles 5 to 7](https://www.jus.uio.no/english/services/library/treaties/04/4-04/public-officials.html)) See `FAIR-4`.
- `8` `PUB-3` **Appoint and promote people, and award public contracts, on merit through fair and open competition with objective criteria set in advance, and keep out favouritism and nepotism.** ([UNCAC Articles 7(1) and 9(1)](https://www.unodc.org/documents/treaties/UNCAC/Publications/Convention/08-50026_E.pdf), [OECD Public Integrity Recommendation, principle 7](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0435))
- `7` `PUB-4` **If you are a non-elected official, serve the lawful government of the day impartially, whatever its politics and your own, and don't let your office be used for party political purposes.** ([UK Civil Service Code: Political impartiality](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/civil-service-code/the-civil-service-code#political-impartiality), [Council of Europe Model Code, Articles 4 and 16](https://www.jus.uio.no/english/services/library/treaties/04/4-04/public-officials.html), [UN Code of Conduct, paragraph 11](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/n97/762/73/pdf/n9776273.pdf))

### Conflicts of interest, gifts and leaving office

- `9` `PUB-5` **Declare your private interests, and deal with any conflict of interest before it affects your work: watch for conflicts, disclose them as soon as you are aware of them, and step back from decisions where your interests, or those of your family or associates, could influence you or appear to.** ([Council of Europe Model Code, Articles 13 and 14](https://www.jus.uio.no/english/services/library/treaties/04/4-04/public-officials.html), [UNCAC Article 8(5)](https://www.unodc.org/documents/treaties/UNCAC/Publications/Convention/08-50026_E.pdf), [Nolan Principles: Integrity](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-7-principles-of-public-life/the-7-principles-of-public-life--2#integrity)) See `FAIR-5`.
- `8` `PUB-6` **Refuse gifts, hospitality and favours that could influence your judgement, or reasonably look as if they could, and report any bribe that is offered to you.** Conventional hospitality and minor gifts are usually allowed, but follow your organisation's rules, declare what it asks you to, and ask your manager when in doubt. ([UN Code of Conduct, paragraph 9](https://documents.un.org/doc/undoc/gen/n97/762/73/pdf/n9776273.pdf), [Council of Europe Model Code, Articles 18 and 19](https://www.jus.uio.no/english/services/library/treaties/04/4-04/public-officials.html), [UK Civil Service Code: Integrity](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/civil-service-code/the-civil-service-code#integrity))
- `7` `PUB-7` **Respect cooling-off periods when you leave public office: for the period the rules set, don't act or lobby for anyone on matters you dealt with in office, and never use confidential information from your former job.** While you are still in post, disclose any job offer that could create a conflict of interest. ([Council of Europe Model Code, Article 26](https://www.jus.uio.no/english/services/library/treaties/04/4-04/public-officials.html), [OECD Lobbying and Influence Recommendation, VIII](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0379), [UNCAC Article 12(2)(e)](https://www.unodc.org/documents/treaties/UNCAC/Publications/Convention/08-50026_E.pdf))

### Openness and accountability

- `8` `PUB-8` **Be truthful: never give ministers, the legislature or the public information you know or believe to be false or misleading, and don't withhold information that should properly be released.** ([Nolan Principles: Honesty](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-7-principles-of-public-life/the-7-principles-of-public-life--2#honesty), [Council of Europe Model Code, Article 22(4)](https://www.jus.uio.no/english/services/library/treaties/04/4-04/public-officials.html), [UK Civil Service Code: Honesty](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/civil-service-code/the-civil-service-code#honesty)) See `HON-1`.
- `8` `PUB-9` **Be open about decisions and the reasons for them: publish information about how your body works and decides, and answer requests for information on time; withhold information only for clear and lawful reasons.** ([Nolan Principles: Openness](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-7-principles-of-public-life/the-7-principles-of-public-life--2#openness), [UNCAC Article 10](https://www.unodc.org/documents/treaties/UNCAC/Publications/Convention/08-50026_E.pdf), [OECD Public Integrity Recommendation, principle 13](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0435)) See `FAIR-8`.
- `8` `PUB-10` **Accept scrutiny: answer for your decisions to the public and to the bodies that oversee you, such as audit institutions, ombudsmen, regulators and courts, and respond properly to their findings.** ([Nolan Principles: Accountability](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-7-principles-of-public-life/the-7-principles-of-public-life--2#accountability), [OECD Public Integrity Recommendation, principle 12](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0435)) See `ACCT-1`.
- `8` `PUB-11` **Report corruption and other wrongdoing through the proper channels, and make sure that colleagues who report in good faith and on reasonable grounds suffer no reprisal.** ([Council of Europe Model Code, Article 12](https://www.jus.uio.no/english/services/library/treaties/04/4-04/public-officials.html), [UNCAC Articles 8(4) and 33](https://www.unodc.org/documents/treaties/UNCAC/Publications/Convention/08-50026_E.pdf), [OECD Public Integrity Recommendation, principle 9](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0435)) See `ACCT-9`.
- `7` `PUB-12` **Lead by example: if you hold senior office, show these principles in your own conduct, treat others with respect, support integrity actively and challenge poor behaviour wherever it occurs.** ([Nolan Principles: Leadership](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-7-principles-of-public-life/the-7-principles-of-public-life--2#leadership), [OECD Public Integrity Recommendation, principles 1 and 6](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0435), [Council of Europe Model Code, Article 25](https://www.jus.uio.no/english/services/library/treaties/04/4-04/public-officials.html))

## How to apply

- `7` [OECD Guidelines for Managing Conflict of Interest in the Public Service](https://legalinstruments.oecd.org/en/instruments/OECD-LEGAL-0316) (2003): core principles and practical steps for identifying, declaring, managing and resolving conflicts of interest, including after people leave public office. Supports `PUB-5`, `PUB-6` and `PUB-7`.
- `7` [Council of Europe Model Code, Article 19: Reaction to improper offers](https://www.jus.uio.no/english/services/library/treaties/04/4-04/public-officials.html): a step-by-step routine for an official who is offered a bribe: refuse it, note who offered it, find witnesses, write it down and report it at once. Supports `PUB-6` and `PUB-11`.

## Open questions

Questions on which serious traditions or experts disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `7` [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Civil Disobedience](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/civil-disobedience/): when, if ever, may an official disclose secret information without authorisation, as Edward Snowden did? Codes of conduct require officials to keep information confidential and to raise concerns through internal and authorised external channels. Some argue that an unauthorised disclosure can be justified as principled disobedience when it exposes serious wrongdoing that those channels failed to address, and the [Tshwane Principles](https://www.justiceinitiative.org/publications/global-principles-national-security-and-freedom-information-tshwane-principles) set out conditions under which such disclosures should be protected; others stress the duty of confidentiality, the harm leaks can do, and the democratic authority of the institutions that keep the secrets.
