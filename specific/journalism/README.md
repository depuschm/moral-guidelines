# Journalism

Scope: reporters, editors, photographers, producers and anyone else who gathers and publishes news or commentary for the public, whatever the medium and whether or not they are paid. How individuals share and post online is covered in [Online life and social media](../online/README.md), advertising and sponsored content in [Marketing and advertising](../marketing/README.md), and AI systems that generate content in [Artificial intelligence](../ai/README.md). The general guidelines on [honesty and trust](../../general/honesty.md), [harm and care](../../general/harm-and-care.md) and [respect, autonomy and consent](../../general/respect-and-consent.md) matter most here. Media law, such as the law on defamation, privacy, contempt of court and reporting restrictions, differs between countries; check with a media lawyer where it applies. This is moral guidance, not legal advice.

> Entries are sorted by score from 1 to 10 ([criteria](../../RANKING.md)): 9–10 start here or fundamental, 6–8 recommended, 1–5 further reading or situational.

## Adopted references

- `10` [SPJ Code of Ethics](https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/) (Society of Professional Journalists, revised October 2026): **the baseline**: four principles (seek truth and report it, minimise harm, act independently, and be accountable and transparent), each with concrete practices. It is a guide, not a set of enforceable rules.
- `10` [Global Charter of Ethics for Journalists](https://www.ifj.org/who/rules-and-policy/global-charter-of-ethics-for-journalists) (International Federation of Journalists, 2019): **the global baseline**, adopted by the world's largest organisation of journalists: sixteen articles on truth, verification, fair methods, corrections, sources, privacy, discrimination and independence, based on the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.
- `8` [BBC Editorial Guidelines](https://www.bbc.com/editorialguidelines/guidelines) (BBC, 2025): a major broadcaster's detailed, public editorial code, on impartiality, accuracy, conflicts of interest, harm and offence, fairness, privacy, crime reporting and children, including the decisions that must be referred to senior editorial or legal advisers.
- `8` [Editors' Code of Practice](https://editorscode.org.uk/the-code/) (Editors' Code of Practice Committee, in force from 1 January 2025): the code that IPSO enforces for the UK newspapers and magazines that subscribe to it: sixteen clauses, including accuracy, privacy, harassment, children, reporting suicide and crime, subterfuge and confidential sources, with a defined public interest test.
- `8` [Preventing suicide: a resource for media professionals](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240076846) (WHO, update 2023): what to do and not do when reporting suicide, based on the evidence that coverage can either increase suicides or help prevent them.
- `7` [Five core principles of ethical journalism](https://ethicaljournalismnetwork.org/who-we-are) (Ethical Journalism Network): truth and accuracy, independence, fairness and impartiality, humanity, and accountability, in one short page.
- `7` [Ethical reporting guidelines](https://www.unicef.org/media/reporting-guidelines) (UNICEF): principles and practical guidelines for interviewing and reporting on children and young people.

## Key rules

### Truth and accuracy

- `9` `JOUR-1` **Verify information before you publish it, and say clearly what you could not confirm.** Use original sources where you can, check the origin and authenticity of documents, images and video, and don't let the pressure to be first override verification. See `HON-1`. ([SPJ Code of Ethics, Seek truth and report it](https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/), [IFJ, Global Charter, articles 3 and 5](https://www.ifj.org/who/rules-and-policy/global-charter-of-ethics-for-journalists), [EJN, Truth and accuracy](https://ethicaljournalismnetwork.org/who-we-are))
- `9` `JOUR-2` **Never fabricate or plagiarise: attribute other people's work, and clearly label illustrations, reconstructions and any content that could be mistaken for a real recording or photograph.** See `HON-9`. ([SPJ Code of Ethics, Seek truth and report it](https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/), [IFJ, Global Charter, articles 3 and 10](https://www.ifj.org/who/rules-and-policy/global-charter-of-ethics-for-journalists))
- `8` `JOUR-3` **Correct significant errors promptly and with due prominence, say what was wrong, and apologise where appropriate.** Correct the online versions that remain available too, not only the next edition or broadcast. See `ACCT-2`. ([SPJ Code of Ethics, Be accountable and transparent](https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/), [IFJ, Global Charter, article 6](https://www.ifj.org/who/rules-and-policy/global-charter-of-ethics-for-journalists), [Editors' Code of Practice, clause 1](https://editorscode.org.uk/the-code/), [BBC Editorial Guidelines, Accuracy 3.4.34–3.4.35](https://www.bbc.com/editorialguidelines/guidelines/accuracy/guidelines))
- `8` `JOUR-4` **Give the people and organisations you criticise or accuse of wrongdoing a fair opportunity to respond before you publish.** ([SPJ Code of Ethics, Seek truth and report it](https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/), [IFJ, Global Charter, article 5](https://www.ifj.org/who/rules-and-policy/global-charter-of-ethics-for-journalists), [Editors' Code of Practice, clause 1](https://editorscode.org.uk/the-code/))
- `8` `JOUR-5` **Keep news, opinion and advertising clearly apart: label opinion and analysis as such, and label sponsored content and partnerships prominently.** Rules on advertising itself are in [Marketing and advertising](../marketing/README.md). ([SPJ Code of Ethics, Seek truth and report it and Act independently](https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/), [IFJ, Global Charter, articles 2 and 13](https://www.ifj.org/who/rules-and-policy/global-charter-of-ethics-for-journalists), [Editors' Code of Practice, clause 1](https://editorscode.org.uk/the-code/))

### Independence and sources

- `9` `JOUR-6` **Protect confidential sources: keep any promise of confidentiality you make, and protect their identity, including from metadata in the documents and photos you publish.** Grant anonymity only as a last resort, for sources with information of real public interest who could face danger or retribution, and consider their motives first. See `HON-5`. ([IFJ, Global Charter, articles 7 and 14](https://www.ifj.org/who/rules-and-policy/global-charter-of-ethics-for-journalists), [Editors' Code of Practice, clause 14](https://editorscode.org.uk/the-code/), [SPJ Code of Ethics, Seek truth and report it](https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/), [Nieman Reports, When is it ethical to publish stolen data?](https://niemanreports.org/when-is-it-ethical-to-publish-stolen-data/))
- `8` `JOUR-7` **Avoid conflicts of interest: refuse gifts, favours, fees, free travel and special treatment that could influence your coverage, and disclose the interests you can't avoid to your editors and, where relevant, to your audience.** Never use financial information for your own profit before it is published. See `FAIR-5`. ([SPJ Code of Ethics, Act independently](https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/), [IFJ, Global Charter, article 13](https://www.ifj.org/who/rules-and-policy/global-charter-of-ethics-for-journalists), [EJN, Independence](https://ethicaljournalismnetwork.org/who-we-are), [Editors' Code of Practice, clause 13](https://editorscode.org.uk/the-code/))

### Harm and privacy

- `9` `JOUR-8` **Weigh the public's need for information against the harm that publishing may cause, and take particular care with victims of crime, bereaved people and others who are vulnerable or inexperienced with the media.** Approach people in grief or shock with sympathy and discretion, and don't pander to lurid curiosity. See `HARM-10`. ([SPJ Code of Ethics, Minimize harm](https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/), [IFJ, Global Charter, article 8](https://www.ifj.org/who/rules-and-policy/global-charter-of-ethics-for-journalists), [EJN, Humanity](https://ethicaljournalismnetwork.org/who-we-are), [Editors' Code of Practice, clause 4](https://editorscode.org.uk/the-code/))
- `9` `JOUR-9` **Never identify a child who is the victim or witness of a sexual offence, don't identify any victim of sexual assault without adequate justification, and interview children under 16 about their own or another child's welfare only with the consent of a parent or other responsible adult.** Change the name and hide the face of children whose identification could expose them to harm, such as children accused of crimes. ([Editors' Code of Practice, clauses 6, 7, 9 and 11](https://editorscode.org.uk/the-code/), [UNICEF, Ethical reporting guidelines](https://www.unicef.org/media/reporting-guidelines), [SPJ Code of Ethics, Minimize harm](https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/))
- `8` `JOUR-10` **Intrude on someone's privacy only when a clear public interest justifies it, such as exposing crime or serious wrongdoing, protecting health and safety, or stopping the public being misled, and be ready to explain how you reached that decision at the time.** Private people have a stronger claim to privacy than public figures, and being able to obtain information legally doesn't make publishing it right. See `RESP-8`. ([Editors' Code of Practice, clause 2 and The public interest](https://editorscode.org.uk/the-code/), [SPJ Code of Ethics, Minimize harm](https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/), [BBC Editorial Guidelines, Privacy](https://www.bbc.com/editorialguidelines/guidelines/privacy/guidelines))
- `8` `JOUR-11` **Use deception, hidden cameras or recorders, or undercover methods only as a last resort: when there is evidence of something the public needs to know and you can't get it openly.** Otherwise say that you are a journalist and get information by fair means. See `HON-2`. ([IFJ, Global Charter, article 4](https://www.ifj.org/who/rules-and-policy/global-charter-of-ethics-for-journalists), [Editors' Code of Practice, clause 10](https://editorscode.org.uk/the-code/), [SPJ Code of Ethics, Seek truth and report it](https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/), [BBC Editorial Guidelines, Privacy 7.4.11 Secret recording](https://www.bbc.com/editorialguidelines/guidelines/privacy/guidelines))
- `8` `JOUR-12` **Report suicide in ways that don't increase the risk of further suicides: don't describe the method or name the location, don't use sensational headlines, and tell readers where they can find help.** Stories of people who came through a suicidal crisis can help prevent suicides. ([WHO, Preventing suicide: a resource for media professionals](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240076846), [Editors' Code of Practice, clause 5](https://editorscode.org.uk/the-code/))
- `7` `JOUR-13` **Mention a person's race, religion, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability or health only when it is relevant to the story, and avoid stereotypes and prejudicial references.** See `FAIR-1`. ([Editors' Code of Practice, clause 12](https://editorscode.org.uk/the-code/), [IFJ, Global Charter, article 9](https://www.ifj.org/who/rules-and-policy/global-charter-of-ethics-for-journalists), [SPJ Code of Ethics, Seek truth and report it](https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/))

## How to apply

Practices, methods and tools that help people follow the key rules.

- `6` [Verification Handbook](https://datajournalism.com/read/handbook/verification-3) (DataJournalism.com): the European Journalism Centre's guide to investigating social media accounts, bots, private messaging apps, deepfakes and other forms of disinformation and media manipulation. Supports `JOUR-1`.
- `6` [SecureDrop](https://securedrop.org/) (Freedom of the Press Foundation): an open-source system that news organisations can install to accept documents securely from anonymous sources. Supports `JOUR-6`.
- `6` [Trust Indicators](https://thetrustproject.org/) (The Trust Project): eight disclosures a news site can publish, such as its ethics policy, its corrections commitments and labels that distinguish news from opinion, analysis and paid content. Supports `JOUR-3` and `JOUR-5`.

## Open questions

Questions on which serious traditions or experts disagree. These guidelines don't pick a side.

- `6` [ONA, Naming criminal suspects](https://ethics.journalists.org/topics/naming-criminal-suspects/): should news media name people suspected of a crime before they are charged? Once someone is charged, many hold that naming them is part of the public record and keeps the justice system accountable; before charge, others urge caution or anonymity because of the presumption of innocence and the lasting harm to people never charged or later cleared. The [SPJ code](https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/) asks journalists to "cautiously consider" it, and in English law the [UK Supreme Court held in Bloomberg v ZXC (2022)](https://supremecourt.uk/uploads/uksc_2020_0122_press_summary_3e62011350.pdf) that, in general, a person under criminal investigation has a reasonable expectation of privacy until charged.
- `6` [Nieman Reports, When is it ethical to publish stolen data?](https://niemanreports.org/when-is-it-ethical-to-publish-stolen-data/): when should journalists publish leaked, stolen or hacked material? Some argue that authentic material of clear public interest should be published, as the Pentagon Papers were; others urge restraint when it invades privacy, could endanger people or national security, or may have been leaked to manipulate the press, and stress careful verification and redaction. The UK [Editors' Code](https://editorscode.org.uk/the-code/) (clause 10) allows publishing material obtained by hacking or removing documents only in the public interest.
